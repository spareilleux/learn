  Lesson 12 measured the wall at 1.216 G arcs on an 8 GiB heap. What this table adds is
  the shape of the approach: the number of arcs per marking climbs with n, so each extra
  component costs more than the markings it adds.

What the structure costs by comparison

  net                       places  invariants  siphons   traps
  philosophers-3                 9           6        6       6
  philosophers-4                12           8        8       8
  philosophers-5                15          10       10      10

  The invariants are a matrix computation and keep answering. The siphons are the brute
  force of lesson 7, capped at twenty places, and that cap is this analyser's, not the
  theory's: the question is NP-hard, and a constraint solver would push it further.

The extension that lifts the limit, and what it costs

  self-inhibited: one place, one transition, one inhibitor arc.
    as an ordinary net, the coverability tree says bounded = False, p <= omega
    with the inhibitor arc, the reachable set is 2 markings, p <= 1, complete = True

  Karp and Miller accelerate on the argument that a sequence reaching a strictly greater
  marking can be repeated. An inhibitor arc breaks it: the token that appeared is exactly
  what now blocks the transition that produced it. The tree is not slow here, it is wrong.

  flush(n)       with the zero test     without it
  flush-1                         3              4
  flush-2                         4              6
  flush-4                         6             10
  flush-8                        10             18

  `finish` is blocked while `a` holds a token, so it fires once, after the last `move`.
  Drop the inhibitor arc and it fires at any point: 2(n+1) markings instead of n+2, and a
  model that no longer says what it was written to say.

  That zero test is what makes two places into the counters of a two-counter machine.
  Boundedness, reachability and liveness are all undecidable for these nets, which is why
  InhibitorNet has a reachable-set builder with a limit and no coverability counterpart.
