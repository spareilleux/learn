# Answer-free learner trial: guitar-audio evidence

Draft protocol. No participant has completed these tasks. Before the trial, prepare a copy of the corresponding frozen lesson with every inline answer block hidden or removed; give that copy and the linked exercise inputs to the learner. Withhold the original answered lesson and `review-rubric.json` until all responses are collected. If answer hiding cannot be guaranteed, record the exercise as open-book assisted recall and do not interpret it as independent transfer. Record prior experience, allowed tools, time, observed difficulties and exact source hashes. Distinguish a mistaken answer from a missing or inaccessible artifact. A corrected second attempt is a separate observation.

1. **GAS001 — align the evidence.** From the pinned pair, identify the recording and rendering boundaries, give the correct recorded file-time interval for the one-second first-note FFT, and state one supported numerical claim and one unsupported realism claim.
2. **AUD002 — control the comparison.** Derive the playback gain from the saved early RMS values. Predict the later RMS mismatch after that gain. Specify what must remain immutable and one condition still requiring listening.
3. **PHY002 — choose the transform.** Correct the claim that changing audio cannot be transformed by FFT. Calculate the sample count and nominal bin spacing for the proposed 50 ms window. Explain what changing only hop size can and cannot improve.
4. **AUD003 — preserve the transient.** Calculate the time span and one-sided bin count for a 2,048-sample window at 44.1 kHz. Explain why Haar denoising output cannot substitute for an unchanged-audio Morlet measurement, and interpret the failed archived noise gate within its binary/source limitation.
5. **AUD004 — respect the falsifier.** Use the saved synthetic receipt to decide whether the declared advantage hypothesis passed. Show the timing arithmetic, name the passed and failed conditions, and state which real-audio or executable evidence is still missing.
6. **GAS002 — avoid a causal shortcut.** Explain why increasing base brightness to the proposed value is not a measured spectral improvement. State the baseline/candidate control order, the illustrative partial-ratio target, and a guard that can reject a candidate despite a better ratio.
7. **GAS003 — separate parameter from output.** Calculate the saved late-minus-early error and proposed candidate interval. Explain why a feedback coefficient slightly above one alone cannot establish an unstable WAV. Identify the missing harness change needed to execute the candidate.

For every task, cite the source/fixture revision and preserve the distinction between a calculation, archived observation, proposed experiment and human listening result. Do not report a learner pass from this unexecuted protocol.
