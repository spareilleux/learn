[Open the 3D observatory](../demos/observatory/) in a browser with WebGL 2. The first load downloads about 53 MB of engine and scene data. On a phone or tablet, touch controls appear; they were tested in iPhone and iPad emulations, not yet on a real device (*to verify*).

Click the scene to look around. Move with **WASD** (or **ZQSD**); hold **Shift** to sprint, press **Space** to jump and **C** to crouch (**C** while sprinting slides). The steel box, the piers, the statue's pedestal and the shelves stop you. **Esc** releases the mouse. If your browser does not grant pointer lock, hold the right mouse button and drag to look. Press **1–7** for camera viewpoints: the camera flies there in about a second, rising and turning on the way. Press **F** for free flight, then **E/Space** to rise and **C** to descend. If your system asks for reduced motion, the viewpoints change at once, and the view no longer widens, tilts or dips when you sprint, slide or land. Mouse capture was verified in the native build, but its behavior in ordinary Chrome, Edge and Firefox windows still needs testing; please report browser-specific problems.

On a touch screen, the stick at the bottom left walks, and sprints past its rim; a drag anywhere else looks around. **▲** jumps and **▼** crouches; **Fly** switches to free flight, where they rise and descend; **Views** steps through the seven viewpoints; **Courses** opens the catalogue. Tap a book for its card, and again to open the course. On a phone held upright the view is widened: on an iPhone, 50 degrees across instead of 32.

Every course of this site is shelved in the library, among the other books. On the ground floor each area of the site has its bay, marked by a banner in its colour hanging under the gallery, with the area's name in gilt. At eye level, under a lacquered sign that names the area and counts its courses, a brass lamp lights them: a series in one binding between two brass bookends, whose spines carry the real titles. Aim at a spine, with the crosshair while the mouse is captured or with the pointer: the book slides out and its card appears. Click it or press **Enter** to open the course in a new tab. Press **L** for a catalogue of every course. The scene reads the course list from the site when it starts, so a new course reaches the shelves without a new build. The **EN / FR / ES** selector at the top right, or **T**, switches the titles, signs, cards, catalogue and course links. English is the default unless you came from a French or Spanish page, and the page's address keeps your choice. The keyboard help stays in English.

In the hall, in front of the Streeling University bay, a marble statue of Hari Seldon faces the centre from a dark pedestal lettered in gilt with his dates in the Galactic Era; the sphere in his hands glows faintly. The capitals, the bases of the piers, the arches, the entablature and the ribs of the ceiling are picked out in gilt, and the floor is paved in rings of pale and dark marble around a compass rose whose points reach out from under the steel box. A moonbeam through the oculus casts the shadows of the box, the piers and the statue across the paving, and the marble darkens where they meet the floor.

Double-click the steel box (double-tap it on a touch screen) to step into the multiverse corridor: an endless row of numbered steel doors under faint ceiling lamps, lit by the lantern you carry, each glowing with the light of another world. The idea comes from Blake Crouch's novel *Dark Matter*; the corridor and its twelve worlds are shaders of our own. The **Leave** button, **Esc**, or the lit doorway behind you bring you back in front of the box.

This prototype contains a Blender-authored, textured steel portal and textures generated locally with ComfyUI. The marble, the grain of the book bindings, the tooled stone, the grime on the steel and three stained-glass panels (sun and moon, an elven tree, an alembic) come from cached SDXL 1.0; the pier ornament comes from SDXL 1.0 with cached ControlNet Union SDXL. Shaders turn them into leather and cloth bindings with raised bands and gilt rules, dressed stone with joints and coffers, brushed steel and backlit stained glass. The balustrade has turned bronze balusters under a walnut handrail. The statue was generated locally too: Z-Image-Turbo drew an invented elderly scholar, not the likeness of any actor, and TRELLIS.2 turned the image into a mesh, remeshed in ComfyUI and normalised in Blender; a shader gives it white marble.

The source scene's richer desktop rendering uses Godot Forward+ features, including screen-space reflections, indirect light, volumetric fog and glow. The browser build uses Godot's WebGL 2 Compatibility renderer, so those desktop-only effects are reduced or absent: the stained glass throws no shafts of light there, and the books, bronze and steel get simple stand-ins for the missing indirect light. For anti-aliasing, a computer renders the scene 1.5 times finer than the screen and scales it down, and a phone or tablet uses 4x multisampling; each steps down if frames get slow. Their cost on a real phone is not measured yet (*to verify*). Neither build claims real-time hardware ray tracing; Blender Cycles baked the steel textures, but does not render the interactive scene.

The room is still stylized, especially its central beam, and the lettering on the book spines is decorative glyphs, not readable titles; only the courses' spines carry real ones. This is a playable prototype, not a finished photorealistic environment. The [Blender course](../blender/) and [ComfyUI course](../comfyui/) explain the tools behind the asset pipeline.
