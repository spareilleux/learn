[Open the 3D observatory](../demos/observatory/) in a desktop browser with WebGL 2. The first load downloads about 53 MB of engine and scene data. Phones and tablets are not supported: this prototype needs a keyboard and mouse.

Click the scene to look around. Move with **WASD** (or **ZQSD**); hold **Shift** to sprint. **Esc** releases the mouse. If your browser does not grant pointer lock, hold the right mouse button and drag to look. Press **1–7** for camera viewpoints, or **F** for free flight, then **E/Space** to rise and **C** to descend. This is an exploratory camera, not yet a collision-aware game controller. Mouse capture was verified in the native build, but its behavior in ordinary Chrome, Edge and Firefox windows still needs testing; please report browser-specific problems.

Every course of this site is shelved in the library, among the other books. On the ground floor, at eye level, each area of the site has its bay: its courses form a series in one binding between two brass bookends, with the area's name on a card on the shelf edge, and their spines carry the real titles. Aim at a spine, with the crosshair while the mouse is captured or with the pointer: the book slides out and its card appears. Click it or press **Enter** to open the course in a new tab. Press **L** for a catalogue of every course. The scene reads the course list from the site when it starts, so a new course reaches the shelves without a new build. Titles are in the language of the page you came from, otherwise in your browser's language.

In the hall, in front of the Streeling University bay, a marble statue of Hari Seldon faces the centre from a dark pedestal lettered in gilt with his dates in the Galactic Era; the sphere in his hands glows faintly. The capitals, the bases of the piers, the arches, the entablature and the ribs of the ceiling are picked out in gilt, and the floor is paved in rings of pale and dark marble around a compass rose whose points reach out from under the steel box.

This prototype contains a Blender-authored, textured steel portal and textures generated locally with ComfyUI. The marble, the grain of the book bindings, the tooled stone, the grime on the steel and three stained-glass panels (sun and moon, an elven tree, an alembic) come from cached SDXL 1.0; the pier ornament comes from SDXL 1.0 with cached ControlNet Union SDXL. Shaders turn them into leather and cloth bindings with raised bands and gilt rules, dressed stone with joints and coffers, brushed steel and backlit stained glass. The balustrade has turned bronze balusters under a walnut handrail. The statue was generated locally too: Z-Image-Turbo drew an invented elderly scholar, not the likeness of any actor, and TRELLIS.2 turned the image into a mesh, remeshed in ComfyUI and normalised in Blender; a shader gives it white marble.

The source scene's richer desktop rendering uses Godot Forward+ features, including screen-space reflections, indirect light, volumetric fog and glow. The browser build uses Godot's WebGL 2 Compatibility renderer, so those desktop-only effects are reduced or absent: the stained glass throws no shafts of light there, and the books, bronze and steel get simple stand-ins for the missing indirect light. Neither build claims real-time hardware ray tracing; Blender Cycles baked the steel textures, but does not render the interactive scene.

The room is still stylized, especially its central beam, and the lettering on the book spines is decorative glyphs, not readable titles; only the courses' spines carry real ones. This is a playable prototype, not a finished photorealistic environment. The [Blender course](../blender/) and [ComfyUI course](../comfyui/) explain the tools behind the asset pipeline.
