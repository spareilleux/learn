Lessons 1 and 8 put notes on the neck, and lesson 14 arranged them into voicings. This lesson looks at the neck as a physical object: where the frets lie, how many of them a hand covers, the five chord shapes of the CAGED system that slide along it, and the tunings that change it. [Guitar Alchemist](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga) (GA) measures all of it: two classes compute fret distances, three measures score how hard a shape is to play, a configuration file lists tunings, and a tool of its MCP server offers easier voicings to a guitarist who cannot play a barre chord. The course program computes the same quantities from their definitions and compares.

All GA links point to commit [`a826864`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/tree/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6). The outputs come from the command below; the entry point is `l17`.

```bash
dotnet run --project code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory -c Release -- l17
```

## Where the frets go

### The idea

Wikipedia: "the ratio of the distances of two consecutive frets to the bridge is … (the twelfth root of two), or approximately 1.059463", and "Theoretically, the twelfth fret should divide the string in two exact halves" ([Fret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fret)). The Streeling module [MUS-009 · Tuning and Fretboard Geometry](../../streeling/music/mus-009-tuning-fretboard-geometry/) derives the distance from the nut to fret *n*, d(n) = L · (1 − 2^(−n/12)), where L is the scale length, the vibrating length of the open string, and gives figures: "On a 648 mm scale, frets 1, 5, 7, 12 and 24 lie 36.4, 162.5, 215.5, 324 and 486 mm from the nut" ([`mus-009-tuning-fretboard-geometry.md#L84`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/Demerzel/blob/d459d8e5f0210cbad00f49c49196fc61160aba76/state/streeling/courses/music/en/mus-009-tuning-fretboard-geometry.md#L84)). The frets get closer together up the neck. Above the 12th fret, the neck is a half-size copy of the part below: the same five frets measure half as much from fret 12 as from the nut.

## How far a hand reaches

### The idea

Wikipedia describes the basic position of the fretting hand: "each of the four fingers of the fretting hand controlling one of the first four frets (index finger on fret 1, little finger on fret 4, etc.)" ([Guitar tunings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guitar_tunings)). Four fingers, four frets: the first position. Because the frets get closer together, the same hand covers more frets higher up. Measured in millimetres, a reach is a fixed length, not a fixed number of frets.

## CAGED

### The idea

A barre chord is "a type of chord on a guitar or other stringed instrument played by using one finger to press down multiple strings across a single fret of the fingerboard". "The CAGED system is an acronym for the chords C, A, G, E, and D." "By replacing the nut with a full bar, a player can use the chord shapes for C, A, G, E, and D anywhere on the fret board to play any major chord in any key." The shape must leave room for the barre: movable chords can be built "provided that the shape leaves the first finger free to create the barre, and that the chord does not require the fingers to extend beyond a four fret range". And the order: "CAGED is also a mnemonic for the sequence that a chord appears in as it moves up the neck; for example, C Major can be played as an open C-shape chord at the nut, as an A-shape chord barred at the third fret, as a G-shape barred at the fifth fret, as an E-shape barred at the eighth fret, and as a D-shape barred at the 10th fret" ([Bar chord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_chord)).

## The effort of a shape

### The idea

What makes a shape hard to play? The course measures what a ruler can check: the frets held, from the lowest to the highest; their span in millimetres on 647.7 mm; and the number of fretted notes. A shape with more than four fretted notes needs a barre, since the hand has four fingers for them. The "four fret range" of the bar chord article is the other limit.

### In GA: a cost and two analyzers

`PhysicalCostService.CalculateStaticCost` "Calculates the "effort" required to play a specific static shape", and the class is "Used to rank multiple ways of playing the same notes" ([`PhysicalCostService.cs#L6-L17`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Services/Fretboard/Analysis/PhysicalCostService.cs#L6-L17)). It adds five terms: a stretch beyond the reach above ([`#L27-L48`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Services/Fretboard/Analysis/PhysicalCostService.cs#L27-L48)); "fat fingers" above fret 17 ([`#L53-L59`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Services/Fretboard/Analysis/PhysicalCostService.cs#L53-L59)); a register term, which subtracts 0.5 for each open string and, with the default profile, adds a penalty when the average fret is below 3 or above 9 ([`#L62-L99`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Services/Fretboard/Analysis/PhysicalCostService.cs#L62-L99)); skipped strings ([`#L102-L119`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Services/Fretboard/Analysis/PhysicalCostService.cs#L102-L119)); and a tension of about 0.1 per fretted string, more on the thick strings ([`#L122-L131`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Services/Fretboard/Analysis/PhysicalCostService.cs#L122-L131)). ## Open tunings

### The idea

"An open tuning allows the guitarist to play a chord by strumming the open strings (no strings fretted)." Wikipedia's table gives open C as "C–G–C–G–C–E", open D as "D–A–D–F♯–A–D", open E as "E–B–E–G♯–B–E" and open G as "D–G–D–G–B-D". The same article explains why standard tuning needs several shapes for one chord: "The irregular major third breaks the fingering patterns of scales and chords, so that guitarists have to memorize multiple chord shapes for each chord." It also says which way to retune: "tuning down the open string from E to D or C reduces the risk of breaking strings, which is associated with tuning strings up to a higher pitch" ([Guitar tunings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guitar_tunings)). GTR-002 adds: "CAGED is specific to standard tuning. Change the tuning, and the shapes change" ([`#L120`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/Demerzel/blob/d459d8e5f0210cbad00f49c49196fc61160aba76/state/streeling/courses/guitar-studies/en/gtr-002-caged-geometry.md#L120)). MUS-009 found two octave errors in GA's `Instruments.yaml`: string 2 of open C and open A an octave too low ([`mus-009-tuning-fretboard-geometry.md#L111-L114`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/Demerzel/blob/d459d8e5f0210cbad00f49c49196fc61160aba76/state/streeling/courses/music/en/mus-009-tuning-fretboard-geometry.md#L111-L114)).

## Exercises

1. GA's reach constant admits frets 1 to 5 from fret 1. How many frets does it admit from fret 12, and why more?
2. In C major, which note does the A shape, x35553, play on string 2, and on which strings does it play C?
3. Why can `DetectCagedShape` never answer "A-Shape" or "C-Shape"?
4. Why does `BiomechanicalAnalyzer` rate F, 133211, easier than open C, x32010?
5. What does `ga_easier_voicings` offer for Bm under "no-barre", and why does neither voicing play B minor?

<details>
<summary>Solutions</summary>

1. Ten, frets 12 to 21. The constant is a fraction of the whole string, and above fret 12 the frets are half as far apart as the frets at the nut.
2. E, at the fifth fret of string 2. It plays C on strings 5 and 3.
3. Line 265 returns null when the first position is muted, and both tests need it muted (lines 297 and 323). Only the E-shape test, which needs that position played, can succeed.
4. It measures the span from the lowest played fret, open strings included: open C spans frets 0 to 3, F frets 1 to 3. F's six notes also get a full stability score. The result: 0.92 for F, 0.82 for C.
5. B7, x21202, which plays B D♯ A B F♯, a major chord; and B5, x244xx, B F♯ B, with no third. B minor needs D.

## Key takeaways

- Fret *n* lies at L(1 − 2^(−n/12)) from the nut. GA's two calculators agree with the formula at every fret, on 647.7 and 648 mm; the document next to them quotes 5-fret spans that neither gives. Five frets from fret 12 measure exactly half of five frets from the nut.
- GA's comfortable reach is the distance from the nut to fret 4, but it is compared with the span between fretted notes, so it admits five frets from fret 1 and ten from fret 12. No measure reads the player's hand size.
- The five CAGED shapes of C sit at frets 0, 3, 5, 8 and 10, and in every key their order is C A G E D read from one of its letters. GA's shape detector can only answer "E-Shape", and only for a voicing written from string 6, the reverse of GA's own order.
- Of GA's three playability measures, the cost has no barre term, one analyzer calls open C "Intermediate", and the other rates F "Very Easy".
- `Tunings.toml` repeats `Instruments.yaml`, octave errors included, and nothing reads it. `ga_easier_voicings` answers from a table of 41 shapes, two of which play a note outside their chord, and offers B7 or B5 for Bm.
