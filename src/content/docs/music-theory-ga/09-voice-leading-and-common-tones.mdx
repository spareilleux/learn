## Common tones

### The idea

A **common tone** is a note that two chords share. Open Music Theory calls it the smallest possible move: "not moving at all, but instead remaining on a single tone that is common to both chords" ([Open Music Theory, "Jazz Voicings"](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/jazz-voicings/)). The rule is old. Rameau taught that a voice goes to the nearest note, and in 1841 the organist Johann August Dürrnberger called it the "rule of the shortest way": notes that the next chord reuses stay in their part, the others move to the closest note of the next chord ([Voice leading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_leading), Wikipedia, "Chord connection").

Among the triads of a key, the distance between the roots decides how many notes two chords share. Roots a third apart share two notes, a fifth apart one, a step apart none. A shared note changes role: C is the root of C major and the third of A minor. The program intersects the chords' pitch classes and names each common tone's interval in both chords:

```text
== Common tones: C major and the other chords of its key (GA: Chord.FromSymbol, PitchClassSet.Intersect)

  E: M3 in C, P1 in Em; G: P5 in C, m3 in Em
C F            0            0            ok
  C: P1 in C, P5 in F
C G            7            7            ok
  G: P5 in C, P1 in G
C Am           0 4          0 4          ok
  C: P1 in C, m3 in Am; E: M3 in C, P5 in Am
C Bdim         (none)       (none)       ok
```

Seventh chords share more. In the ii–V–I of lesson 7, the third of each chord becomes the seventh of the next: F, the third of Dm7, is the seventh of G7, and B, the third of G7, is the seventh of Cmaj7. Open Music Theory builds its two-voice jazz voicings on that exchange, to "lead from the third of one chord to the seventh of the next" (same chapter), and the Streeling module [MUS-005 · Jazz Harmony for Guitar](../../streeling/music/mus-005-jazz-harmony/) calls those notes guide tones (section "4. Guide Tones and Voice Leading").

## The smallest voice leading

### The idea

**Voice leading** is the way each voice moves when the harmony changes (Open Music Theory's glossary, same chapter). It "should generally be smooth—that is, voices should ideally move as little as possible when going to a new chord (except the bass, which often leaps)". To compare two ways of moving, count semitones: send each note of the first chord to a note of the second, each by the shorter way round the octave, at most six semitones, and add them up. That sum is the **size** of the voice leading, and the smallest one is the smoothest. Dmitri Tymoczko's geometry of chords is built on finding these minimal voice leadings between any two chords ([Tymoczko 2006](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1126287); in his 2011 book, tonal music asks for chords "connected by efficient voice leadings", as quoted in Wikipedia's [Voice leading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_leading)).

When the chords differ in size, one note of the smaller chord is doubled: one voice splits in two, or two voices merge. The course tries every doubling and every assignment of voices ([`Lesson11.Smallest`](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/50a94bb/code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory/Lesson11.cs#L127-L140)). It works on pitch classes: where a voice goes, not in which octave.

The figure puts C major and F major on one bracelet. C is shared and ringed; E sits next to F, and G two steps below A: C stays, E rises a half step, G a whole step, three semitones in all.


C to A minor moves one voice by a whole step. C to D minor shares nothing, so every voice moves. C to F♯, a tritone away, costs six semitones, yet no voice moves more than three. Each change of the ii–V–I moves two voices by a step and keeps the two others; of the guide tones, the third stays to become the next seventh, and the seventh falls a half step to the next third. G7 to C has four voices for three notes, so two of them meet on C: B rises, D falls.

### In GA: the MCP tool, on guitar voicings

The MCP tool `ga_voice_leading_pair` works on real voicings: it searches guitar voicings for both chords and sorts the pairs by a distance on their MIDI pitches ([`CompositionTools.cs#L312-L336`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/GaMcpServer/Tools/CompositionTools.cs#L312-L336)): sort both voicings, pair them from the bottom, and charge 3 semitones for each note left over. Here octaves count, since a guitar voicing has a register. When both voicings have as many notes, pairing them in order is the best one can do: on a line, two crossing pairs are never shorter than the same pairs uncrossed. When they differ, the comment says the shorter voicing "pairs against its nearest neighbors", but the code pairs it against the lowest notes. The same comment warns that this "is not a formal Hungarian-optimal assignment", only a ranking "good enough to surface smooth voice-leadings among candidates". The program copies the function ([`Lesson11.PairDistance`](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/50a94bb/code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory/Lesson11.cs#L219-L236)) and compares it with every pairing, at the same 3 semitones per note left over ([`Lesson11.VoicingDistance`](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/50a94bb/code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory/Lesson11.cs#L238-L249)):

## Parallel fifths and octaves

### The idea

Two voices move in **parallel** when they move in the same direction by the same interval. Species counterpoint forbids two perfect consonances of the same size in a row: "P5–P5 or P8–P8. This includes both simple and compound intervals; for example, P5–P12 is considered the same as P5–P5." The reason: "Parallel fifths and octaves promote tonal fusion at the expense of melodic independence" ([Open Music Theory, "First-Species Counterpoint"](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/first-species-counterpoint/)). Common-practice harmony keeps the rule between any two voices.

A guitar breaks it all the time. Moving a barre chord along the neck moves every string by the same interval, so every fifth and every octave inside the shape moves in parallel. A power chord is a fifth, and a riff of power chords is nothing but parallel fifths; Wikipedia's [Consecutive fifths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consecutive_fifths) notes that "the prohibition is not relevant since there is no intention to create independent voices". The rule belongs to a style, not to the instrument.

### In GA

[`ProgressionVoiceLeadingAnalyzer`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Services/Fretboard/Voicings/Analysis/ProgressionVoiceLeadingAnalyzer.cs#L33-L68) takes each string as a voice. For every pair of strings sounding in both voicings, if both move in the same direction and the interval, folded into one octave, is a fifth before and after, it reports parallel fifths; if it is 0 before and after, parallel octaves. The program compiles GA's file as it is and compares it with its own reading of the rule ([`Lesson11.Parallels`](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/50a94bb/code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory/Lesson11.cs#L281-L299)). Strings are numbered from 1, the high E, to 6, the low E; "6-5" is the pair of the two lowest strings:

They agree on every pair. The barre chord holds three fifths and four octaves, and all seven move together. Two of the three open-chord changes, C → G and G7 → C, each hide one pair of parallel octaves, on strings 5 and 2. In the open G7 below, B2 and B3 are the same note, the leading tone, doubled: both rise to C, C3 and C4. A harmony textbook would not double the leading tone for that reason; the open shape does.

## Distances between chord types

### The idea

[Appendix B](../appendix-optic/) climbed the OPTIC ladder: octave, permutation, transposition, inversion, cardinality. Clifton Callender, Ian Quinn and Dmitri Tymoczko measured voice leadings on each rung ([Callender, Quinn and Tymoczko 2008](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1153021)). With O and P, the distance is between pitch-class sets, as above: C major to F major is 3. Add T, and it becomes a distance between chord *types*: C major to F major is 0, both major triads; C major to C minor is 1, E to E♭; a major triad to an augmented one is 1. Add I, and major and minor become the same type. The program computes the three distances by brute force, every assignment of voices, every transposition, the inversion ([`Lesson11.Optic`](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/50a94bb/code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory/Lesson11.cs#L319-L351)), as "OP OPT OPTI":

```text
== Distance from C major under OP, OPT and OPTI (GA: VoiceLeadingSpace)
chords         course       GA           check
C F            3 0 0        3 0 0        ok
C Eb           3 0 0        3 0 0        ok
C Am           2 1 0        2 1 0        ok
C Cm           1 1 0        1 1 0        ok
C Caug         1 1 1        1 1 1        ok
C Cdim         2 1 1        2 1 1        ok
C F#           6 0 0        6 0 0        ok
Cmaj7 C7       1 1 1        1 1 1        ok
C7 Cdim7       3 1 1        3 1 1        ok
```

### In GA

[`VoiceLeadingSpace`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Services/Atonal/VoiceLeadingSpace.cs) takes the four switches and computes the same distance; the program compiles GA's file as it is, and the GA column above is its answer. With octave and permutation equivalence, it does not try every assignment of voices: it sorts both chords and tries only the cyclic shifts of one against the other ([`#L63-L91`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Services/Atonal/VoiceLeadingSpace.cs#L63-L91)). That shortcut is right, over every pair of three-note and four-note sets:

```text
== GA's shortcut (cyclic shifts of the sorted chords) against every assignment of voices
sets           ordered pairs  GA differs from the course
3 notes, OP    48400          0
3 notes, OPT   48400          0
4 notes, OP    245025         0
4 notes, OPT   245025         0
```

Without octave equivalence, transposition goes wrong: it tries only shifts of 0 to 11 semitones, upward ([`#L46-L61`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Services/Atonal/VoiceLeadingSpace.cs#L46-L61)), so the second chord can never move down to meet the first:

```text
== Without octave equivalence, with transposition: GA only shifts upward, by 0 to 11 semitones
from -> to             course GA     check
C4 major -> B3 major   0      0      ok
B3 major -> C4 major   0      3      DIFF
C4 major -> C5 major   0      36     DIFF
C5 major -> C4 major   0      3      DIFF
```

Two major triads are the same type under T, at distance 0, but GA's answer depends on which comes first, and a distance that changes with the order is not a distance.

GA's other OPTIC distance is between set classes: [`SetClassOpticIndex.Distance`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Services/Atonal/SetClassOpticIndex.cs#L53-L76). For two classes of the same size, its comment weighs "the nearest-neighbor sum approach (approximation)" against "strict best-permutation (expensive)" and takes the first, "a reasonable Hausdorff-like distance": each note of the first class goes to the nearest note of the second ([`#L167-L224`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Services/Atonal/SetClassOpticIndex.cs#L167-L224)), so several voices may land on the same note. The program compiles that file too and compares it with the one-to-one distance on the same prime forms:

```text
== Set classes of the same size: GA's SetClassOpticIndex sums each note's nearest neighbour
set classes      course GA     check
(012) -> (048)   6      2      DIFF
(048) -> (012)   6      6      ok
  12 trichord classes, 144 ordered pairs:
  GA below the one-to-one minimum: 35
  GA above it: 0
  GA's answer changes when the two are swapped: 70
```

The three notes of the cluster (012) crowd round one note of the augmented triad, 2 semitones in all; one-to-one, two of them must travel 3 semitones each. The class's comment also cites the Callender–Quinn–Tymoczko article as *Music Theory Online* 14(3), with a link that lands on that journal's error page ([`#L15-L18`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Services/Atonal/SetClassOpticIndex.cs#L15-L18)); the article appeared in *Science*.

Neither class decides anything in GA yet. At `a826864`, `VoiceLeadingSpace` is called only by its tests, and `SetClassOpticIndex` by `ShapeGraphBuilder`, only when `VoiceLeadingWeight` is above zero ([`ShapeGraphBuilder.cs#L233-L260`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Services/Fretboard/Shapes/ShapeGraphBuilder.cs#L233-L260)), and its default is 0 ([`ShapeGraphBuildOptions.cs#L44`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Services/Fretboard/Shapes/ShapeGraphBuildOptions.cs#L44)). So GA has four answers to "how far is G from C": 3 semitones for the chatbot, which uses pitch classes; 28 for the MCP tool on the open shapes; 0 for `VoiceLeadingSpace` under T, since both are major triads; and 0 for `SetClassOpticIndex`, which compares their set classes. Each answers a different question. Only the first two reach a user, and both carry the defects shown above.

## Exercises

1. Which notes do F and Dm share, and what is each note's role in both chords?
2. What is the smallest voice leading from Am to F?
3. Move the A barre chord (`577655`) up two frets, to B. Which pairs of strings move in parallel fifths, and which in parallel octaves?
4. How far is C major from E♭ major under OP, and under OPT?

1. F and A. F is the root of F and the minor third of Dm; A is the major third of F and the fifth of Dm. The roots are a third apart, so the triads share two notes.
2. One semitone: A and C stay, E rises to F. A minor and F major are a third apart, like C and A minor.
3. The same seven as the F barre: fifths on strings 6-5, 6-2 and 4-2, octaves on 6-4, 6-1, 4-1 and 5-2. The shape decides, not the fret.

## Key takeaways

- A common tone is the smallest move. Triads whose roots are a third apart share two notes, a fifth apart one, a step apart none; in ii–V–I, the third of each chord becomes the seventh of the next.
- The size of a voice leading is the sum of the semitones each voice moves, the short way round. Between chords of different sizes, which note is doubled matters: always doubling the root misses the minimum in 43.8 % of triad–seventh pairs.
- On real voicings, octaves count. Pairing notes in order is best when both voicings have as many notes; GA's MCP tool pairs from the bottom when they don't, and charges a dropped or added bass far too much.
- GA's parallel-fifth analyser follows the textbook rule string by string. Barre shapes and power chords break the rule by construction, and rock does not ask them to follow it.
- `VoiceLeadingSpace`'s shortcut is right, but without octave equivalence its transposition shifts only upward, so the distance changes with the order; `SetClassOpticIndex` lets voices share a note, so its distance changes with the order. GA's core finds common tones right; the F# closure behind `ga_common_tones` lost notes at `a826864`.
