Lesson 2 turned the major scale into seven modes. This lesson does the same with two other seven-note scales, melodic minor and harmonic minor, whose modes give jazz most of its dominant scales. Then it ranks modes from bright to dark, and asks what a modal family is. [Guitar Alchemist](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga) (GA) answers in five places: generic mode classes, an interface that names scale degrees, three brightness measures, a service that ranks modes, and a configuration file of 165 modes. The course program checks each one, and runs GA's own code wherever it can.

All GA links point to commit [`a826864`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/tree/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6). The outputs come from the command below; the entry point is `l13`.

```bash
dotnet run --project code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory -c Release -- l13
```

## The modes of melodic minor

### The idea

Wikipedia: "A great deal of modern jazz harmony arises from the modes of the ascending form of the melodic minor scale, also known as the jazz melodic minor scale. This scale is essentially a diatonic major scale with a lowered third, for example A-B-C-D-E-F♯-G♯-A" ([Jazz scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_scale)). Its steps are 2 1 2 2 2 2 1 (lesson 2), and its modes are the rotations of those steps. The same article names them and gives each the chord it goes with: Dorian ♭2, Lydian augmented, Lydian dominant (also called the acoustic scale), Mixolydian ♭6, Locrian ♮2 (it lists "Half-diminished, Locrian ♯2, or Aeolian ♭5") and the altered scale, or Super Locrian. Lydian dominant goes with "C7 (9, ♯11, 13)" and the altered scale with "C7 (♭9 or ♯9, ♯11, ♭13)": two ways to color a dominant seventh.

Open Music Theory builds chord-scales from the modes of major, then adds: "A similar approach to the one above can be used to derive more chord-scale relationships from the melodic minor, harmonic minor, and harmonic major modes" ([Open Music Theory, "Chord-Scale Theory"](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/chord-scale-theory/)). The Streeling module [MUS-005 · Jazz Harmony for Guitar](../../streeling/music/mus-005-jazz-harmony/) does it in section 6, "Chord-Scale Theory": V7♯11 takes Lydian dominant and V7alt the altered scale, both from "The Melodic Minor Mother Scale".

The course writes each mode with lesson 2's notation: each degree compared with the major scale, `>` on a degree that differs from the reference mode (Aeolian when the third is minor, Ionian otherwise), and ♮ on a degree the reference lowers ([`Lesson13.Formula`](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/13e7607/code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory/Lesson13.cs#L39-L51)).

## The modes of harmonic minor

### The idea

Wikipedia: "A harmonic minor scale can be built by lowering the 3rd and 6th degrees of the parallel major scale by one semitone", and its steps are "whole, half, whole, whole, half, augmented second, half" ([Harmonic minor scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonic_minor_scale)): 2 1 2 2 1 3 1. Its table of modes, on A: harmonic minor, Locrian ♮6, Ionian ♯5 ("augmented major"), Dorian ♯4 (also Romanian minor), Phrygian dominant, Lydian ♯2, and a seventh mode it calls "Super-Locrian scale" with a doubly flattened seventh, "or altered diminished, Ultralocrian". Phrygian dominant is the mode of the fifth degree, the one a minor key's V7 colors: the article gives it "A7 (♭9, 11, ♭13)".

The augmented second shows in the formulas. The seventh mode, on G♯, is the altered scale with its seventh lowered once more: from G♯ to F is a diminished seventh, so the course writes ♭♭7.

## One interface for every kind of degree

### The idea

A mode is a scale plus a degree of it. In harmony, a Roman numeral names a degree by the triad built on it (lesson 6): upper case for major, lower case for minor, ° for diminished, + for augmented. On the fourth degree of A harmonic minor, D F A is minor: iv. On the fourth degree of A melodic minor, D F♯ A is major: IV. The course writes the triads of each scale this way ([`Lesson13.TriadNumerals`](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/13e7607/code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory/Lesson13.cs#L91-L104)).

### In GA

GA makes the pair literal: [`ScaleMode<TScaleDegree>`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Core/Theory/Tonal/Modes/ScaleMode.cs#L62-L79) is generic over the type of the degree, and each scale has its own: `MajorScaleDegree`, `NaturalMinorScaleDegree`, `HarmonicMinorScaleDegree`, `MelodicMinorScaleDegree`, `HarmonicMajorScaleDegree` and fifteen more, each a value from 1 to the scale's size that checks its range. It is the "smart enum" pattern: a type per kind of degree, with behavior attached, so a melodic minor degree cannot be passed where a major one is expected. Each type names its degrees through [`IScaleDegreeNaming`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Core/Theory/Tonal/Primitives/IScaleDegreeNaming.cs#L10-L23): `ToName()` returns "the full name of the scale degree (e.g., "Ionian", "Dorian")", and `ToShortName()` "the short name of the scale degree (e.g., "I", "II")". Because the interface is the same, one generic method reads every type ([`Lesson13.ShortNames`](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/13e7607/code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory/Lesson13.cs#L87-L89)), and the course compares the short names with the triads:


The five types follow three conventions. [`MajorScaleDegree`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Core/Theory/Tonal/Primitives/Diatonic/MajorScaleDegree.cs#L64-L74) and [`HarmonicMajorScaleDegree`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Core/Theory/Tonal/Primitives/Diatonic/HarmonicMajorScaleDegree.cs#L67-L77) write the degree number, as the interface's example does. [`NaturalMinorScaleDegree`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Core/Theory/Tonal/Primitives/Diatonic/NaturalMinorScaleDegree.cs#L64-L74) writes the triads, all right. The two others write triads too, except for one degree each: harmonic minor's fourth is "iv#4" ([`#L67-L77`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Core/Theory/Tonal/Primitives/Diatonic/HarmonicMinorScaleDegree.cs#L67-L77)), the triad's numeral with the name of the Dorian ♯4 mode attached, and melodic minor's fourth is "IV7" ([`#L64-L74`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Core/Theory/Tonal/Primitives/Diatonic/MelodicMinorScaleDegree.cs#L64-L74)), the only seventh chord of the list: D F♯ A C, the chord of Lydian dominant. A caller cannot know which convention it gets. None exists yet: at `a826864`, nothing in GA calls `ToShortName`.

## Brightness

### The idea

Lesson 2 cited Open Music Theory's ranking of the modes from bright to dark, and showed why it is so regular: from Lydian to Locrian, each mode lowers one note of the previous one. A number captures it: add up the semitones of the degrees above the tonic. Ionian gives 0 + 2 + 4 + 5 + 7 + 9 + 11 = 38; Lydian raises the fourth, 39; Locrian, the darkest, 33. Each lowered degree takes one away, so the sum ranks the modes of major exactly as the textbook does, and it applies to any scale. The Streeling module [MUS-006 · The Scale Universe](../../streeling/music/mus-006-the-scale-universe/) defines it the same way: "**Brightness** is the sum of the pitch classes (bit positions set)". The course computes it from the steps ([`Lesson13.Brightness`](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/13e7607/code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory/Lesson13.cs#L53-L54)).

### In GA: three measures

GA has three things called brightness:

- [`PitchClassSet.StepBrightness`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Core/Theory/Atonal/PitchClassSet.cs#L262-L283) "measures brightness based on the preponderance of large steps": it averages the steps between consecutive pitch classes, around the circle, and divides by 6. The code calls it a "Very rough proxy". It feeds one dimension of GA's embeddings ([`ModalVectorService.cs#L157`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Business.ML/Embeddings/Services/ModalVectorService.cs#L157)).
- `IcvBrightness`, in [`TheoryVectorService.cs#L159-L172`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Business.ML/Embeddings/Services/TheoryVectorService.cs#L159-L172), weighs the major thirds and perfect fifths of the interval-class vector (lesson 4) against its minor seconds and tritones, and maps the balance to the range 0 to 1. It is dimension 21 of the structure vector `TheoryVectorService` computes ([`#L82-L83`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Business.ML/Embeddings/Services/TheoryVectorService.cs#L82-L83)), which GA's embedding generator writes from index 6 ([`MusicalEmbeddingGenerator.cs#L125`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Business.ML/Embeddings/MusicalEmbeddingGenerator.cs#L125), [`EmbeddingSchema.cs#L334`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Business.ML/Embeddings/EmbeddingSchema.cs#L334)): index 27 of the full embedding. The method is internal, so the program copies it as it is ([`Lesson13.IcvBrightness`](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/13e7607/code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory/Lesson13.cs#L106-L120)).

StepBrightness gives all 21 modes the same value. The steps of a set go once around the circle, so they always add up to 12: with *n* notes their average is 12/*n*, and the value is 2/*n*, the number of notes in disguise. Over all 4,096 sets it takes 11 values, one per size, except that sets of 4 notes (2/4) share 0.5 with the empty set and single notes, for which the code returns 0.5. ## Modal families in a configuration file

### The idea

For the course, a modal family is the set of rotations of one scale (lesson 2), and all the rotations share one interval-class vector (lesson 4). So a family can declare its vector once, and the notes of each member must have it. The converse does not hold: harmonic major has harmonic minor's vector without being one of its modes.

### In GA: `Modes.yaml`

GA also lists modes in a configuration file, [`Modes.yaml`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Business.Config/Modes.yaml), 828 lines read by the F# module `ModesConfig`. Each family has a name, sometimes an `IntervalClassVector`, and modes: their notes on C, a description for 77 of the 165 modes and alternate names for 79. `GetAllModes` gives every mode its family's declared vector ([`ModesConfig.fs#L265-L298`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Business.Config/ModesConfig.fs#L265-L298), [`#L281`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Business.Config/ModesConfig.fs#L281)). The program, which already compiles `GA.Business.Config`, checks each declared vector against the notes of the family's first mode, and each member against the first ([`Lesson13.cs#L218-L259`](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/13e7607/code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory/Lesson13.cs#L218-L259)):


## Key takeaways

- Melodic minor is a major scale with a lowered third, harmonic minor a natural minor with a raised seventh. Each has seven modes, among them Lydian dominant, the altered scale and Phrygian dominant, the dominant scales of jazz. GA's generic `ScaleMode<TScaleDegree>` spells and writes all fourteen as the textbook does.
- One interface, `IScaleDegreeNaming`, names the degrees of every scale type, but its `ToShortName` mixes three conventions: degree numbers, triad numerals, and two short names that are neither (`iv#4`, `IV7`). Nothing calls it yet.
- The sum of a mode's degrees ranks modes from bright to dark, and GA's `RankByBrightness` computes exactly that. GA's two other brightness measures cannot rank modes: `StepBrightness` is 2/*n*, the number of notes, and `IcvBrightness` is the same for every mode of a scale.
- A GA modal family is the set of sets that share an interval-class vector, so harmonic minor's family holds harmonic major's modes too, and the ranking has 14 rows.
- In `Modes.yaml`, 9 of 18 declared vectors are wrong, 60 of 134 members lack their family's vector, 8 names are cut by YAML comments and the loader drops every alternate name. Looked up by vector, harmonic minor is found nowhere and G7 is "Major Seventh"; looked up by name, 5 of GA's own 14 names are missing and "Ionian Augmented" lands in the wrong family.
