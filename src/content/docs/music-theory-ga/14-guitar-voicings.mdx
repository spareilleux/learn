Lesson 3 named a guitar voicing by its chord. This lesson names it by the way its notes are arranged: close or drop, with or without its root, a shell of three notes, and the guide tones that carry a progression. [Guitar Alchemist](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga) (GA) labels the voicings it stores in the same words: a generator lists every voicing on the neck, analyzers label each one `Drop-2`, `rootless` or `shell-voicing`, filters select voicings by those labels, and a configuration file holds comping recipes its chatbot can retrieve. The course program builds 128 drop voicings from the definition, takes from GA's generator every voicing of a seventh chord on C, and compares the labels.

All GA links point to commit [`a826864`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/tree/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6). The outputs come from the command below; the entry point is `l16`.

```bash
dotnet run --project code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory -c Release -- l16
```

## Close and drop voicings

### The idea

Wikipedia shows three voicings of a C major triad: "The first is in close position (the most compact voicing), while the second and third are in open position (that is, with wider spacing)." Arrangers name some open positions by the notes they move: "One nomenclature for describing certain classes of voicings is the "drop-n" terminology, such as drop-2 voicings, drop-4 voicings, etc." In it, "The implicit, non-dropped, default voicing in this system has all voices in the same octave, with individual voices numbered from the top down", and "A drop-2 voicing lowers the second voice by an octave." Several voices can drop: "There are four drop-2-and-4 voicings for G 7. Reading down from the top voice, they are G D F B, B F G D, D G B F, and F B D G." The system "doesn't provide a term for more than one voice on the same pitch", and "doesn't cover the dropping of voices by two or more octaves" ([Voicing (music)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voicing_(music))). That section of the article cites no source, and says so; the Streeling module [MUS-005 · Jazz Harmony for Guitar](../../streeling/music/mus-005-jazz-harmony/) gives the same definition: "Take a close-position four-note chord and "drop" the second-highest note down an octave."

Guitarists need it. Wikipedia again: "the perfect fourth intervals between the guitar's strings typically make most close position chords cumbersome and impractical to play", while "the fingerings used for drop voicings on guitar are easily moved horizontally and vertically around the fingerboard".

The course follows the definition to the letter ([`Lesson16.cs#L43-L68`](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/1d58ba8/code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory/Lesson16.cs#L43-L68)). Under a top note, it puts every other note of the chord as high as it goes, which gives the close position; it lowers voices 2, 3 or 4 by an octave, alone or together; and it names any voicing back by comparing each note with its place in the close position under the same top note. A note more than an octave below that place is "beyond drop-n", and a voicing with a doubled pitch class is "doubled, not named".

### In GA: one label, "Drop-2"

Raising the lowest note of a close position gives another close position, an inversion. In a voicing with one dropped voice, the dropped voice is the lowest, and raising it puts it back in place. With two dropped voices, the second one stays an octave down, and the notes span more than an octave. So GA's "Drop-2" means "close, or one voice dropped", and no voicing is ever "Drop-3". The seven voicings that span more than an octave are "rootless", though all eight have their C; none is open; the close position, 11 semitones wide, gets `drop-2-voicings` with the others. ## On the guitar: open chords, and MUS-005's drop 2

### The idea

On the guitar, "open" has another meaning. "While open chords are the most commonly employed voicings on the guitar and other fretted instruments for the volume and resonance they produce", says the same article: chords with open strings, such as lesson 3's. Each of those doubles a note: x32010 plays C E G C E, outside a system that "doesn't provide a term for more than one voice on the same pitch". A rootless voicing leaves out the root, for a reason Open Music Theory gives: "if there is a bass player in the performing ensemble, you may deliberately omit the root in the other voices" ([Open Music Theory, "Jazz Voicings"](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/jazz-voicings/)).

MUS-005 showed a "Drop-2 Cmaj7 (string set 5-4-3-2)": x3200x, whose E was "the 3rd — dropped from close position" ([`mus-005-jazz-harmony.md#L214-L223`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/Demerzel/blob/6aef19b3a7c0ceac400f89f5585f4c033d61c99c/state/streeling/courses/music/en/mus-005-jazz-harmony.md#L214-L223), as the module read until [Demerzel #1098](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/Demerzel/pull/1098) corrected it). Its notes, C3 E3 G3 B3, lie within an octave: it is a close position. Under its top note B, nothing is dropped. The drop 2 with C in the bass on those strings, which the module now shows, is x3545x, C3 G3 B3 E4: under the top note E4, the close position is G3 B3 C4 E4, and lowering C4, the second voice from the top, gives it. The figure shows both, with a drop 3 of Cmaj7 and a G7 shell, the subject of the next section:

## Shells

### The idea

MUS-005: "The root, 3rd, and 7th are the **essential tones** that define a chord's quality and function. The 5th is almost always omitted (it adds no harmonic information unless altered)." Freddie Green, in Count Basie's band, played them "almost always on strings 6-4-3 (or 5-4-3). Only three notes — root (or shell bass note), 3rd, 7th." Open Music Theory, for two voices above the bass: "you should typically use the third and seventh of the chord (omitting the fifth)". The course plays them on those strings: G7, Gmaj7 and Gm7 with the root on the sixth string, the seventh below the third (3x34xx, 3x44xx, 3x33xx), and C7, Cmaj7 and Cm7 with the root on the fifth, the third below the seventh (x323xx, x324xx, x313xx). Then it asks GA about the shapes of the previous section ([`Lesson16.cs#L236-L252`](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/1d58ba8/code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory/Lesson16.cs#L236-L252)).

Between G7 and C7, "rootless" decides, that is the spread: with the seventh below the third, 15 or 16 semitones separate the bass from the top; with the third below the seventh, 10 or 11. All six shells have their root in the bass.

## Every voicing on the neck

### The idea

How many voicings does a guitar have? GA's answer follows from its rules, and they can be counted. [`VoicingGenerator`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Services/Fretboard/Voicings/Generation/VoicingGenerator.cs#L156-L279) slides a window of five frets along the 24-fret neck, starting on frets 0 to 20. In each window, every string is muted or plays one of the five frets, and a voicing needs at least two notes ([`#L18-L144`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Services/Fretboard/Voicings/Generation/VoicingGenerator.cs#L18-L144)). That is 6⁶ = 46,656 combinations per window, minus the silent one and the 30 with a single note: 46,625. Two neighbouring windows share four frets, so 5⁶ − 1 − 24 = 15,600 voicings, which the generator keeps once by comparing diagrams ([`#L250-L278`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Services/Fretboard/Voicings/Generation/VoicingGenerator.cs#L250-L278)). The program runs it in sequence ([`Lesson16.cs#L256-L265`](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/1d58ba8/code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory/Lesson16.cs#L256-L265)):

```text
== VoicingGenerator.GenerateAllVoicings (GA.Domain.Services, compiled) on GA's standard guitar, run in sequence
                       course       GA           check
voicings               667125       667125       ok
the course: 21 windows × 46625 − 20 shared × 15600
```

## Guide tones

### The idea

MUS-005: "The **3rd** and **7th** of each chord are called **guide tones**", and in a ii–V–I "The guide tones **exchange roles**: the 3rd of one chord becomes the 7th of the next, and vice versa. This creates a two-voice counterpoint that descends chromatically: C-B, F-E." Open Music Theory says it of any two chords a fifth apart: "one voice will alternate between the third and the seventh of the chord, and the other voice will do the same but in the opposite order". The course checks four moves in each key: the thirds of ii and V stay, as the sevenths of V and I, and the sevenths of ii and V fall a half step, to the next third.

3. Which of the six shells does the `ShellVoicings` filter keep, and why not x324xx?
4. Read "Cm(maj9): 3-5-4-5" on strings 5-4-3-2. Which notes are they, and which voicing of this lesson is it?

## Key takeaways

- A drop-*n* voicing lowers voice *n* of a close position by an octave, counting from the top. GA's single test answers "Drop-2" for a close position and for any voicing with one dropped voice, and nothing for the others, so its `Drop3` and `Drop2And4` filters keep nothing.
- In GA, "rootless" means spread over more than an octave, and no voicing is open: 672 of the 687 seventh chords on four strings are "rootless", each with its root.
- GA has three rules for a shell: three or four strings; more than an octave and three pitch classes; more than an octave and three notes. None checks for a root, a third and a seventh.
- GA's generator lists 667,125 voicings, as the window arithmetic says; among them are all of MUS-005's drop-2 and drop-3 shapes, and drop 2s on four more string sets.
- The guide tones of GA's twelve ii–V–I rows move as MUS-005 says, but GA's analysis never records guide tones, and none of the fifteen fingerings of its comping recipes plays the chord it names.
