Lesson 3 stopped at the ninth chord. This lesson climbs the stack of thirds to the eleventh and the thirteenth, then changes notes in it: the altered chords of jazz. It ends with chords made of two triads, upper structures and polychords. [Guitar Alchemist](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga) (GA) has code for each step: two chord-symbol parsers, a service that names extensions, another that names alterations, a factory of chord templates, an MCP tool for polychords, and content files its chatbot can retrieve. The course program reads 41 chord symbols, runs GA's code on them, and compares.

All GA links point to commit [`a826864`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/tree/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6). The outputs come from the command below; the entry point is `l15`.

```bash
dotnet run --project code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory -c Release -- l15
```

## Extended chords

### The idea

Open Music Theory: "The presence of an extension in a chord symbol implies the presence of all other extensions below it as well. So, an eleventh chord is not just a triad plus the eleventh—it's a triad plus an eleventh, ninth, and seventh." And: "While the default quality of the seventh is minor, extensions and additions are assumed to be major (ninths and thirteenths) or perfect (elevenths) unless otherwise indicated" ([Open Music Theory, "Chord Symbols"](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/chord-symbols/)). The course reads each symbol as that full stack: C11 is C E G B♭ D F, C13 is C E G B♭ D F A, Cmaj13 has B instead of B♭, Cm13 E♭ instead of E ([`Lesson15.Symbols`](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/ac620ce/code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory/Lesson15.cs#L15-L64)).

Players rarely sound the full stack. On the eleventh: "A perfect eleventh creates a highly dissonant minor ninth interval with the major third of major and dominant chords. To reduce this dissonance the third is often omitted", which turns C11 into "a suspended ninth chord (e.g. C9sus4, C–G–B♭–D–F)" ([Eleventh chord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleventh_chord)). On the thirteenth: "It is customary to omit the eleventh on dominant or major thirteenth chords because the eleventh conflicts with the third". Kept whole, a thirteenth chord holds the seven notes of a scale, and Wikipedia shows what that means: "Cmaj13 (C–E–G–B–D–F–A) becomes Em11♭9♭13 (E–G–B–D–F–A–C) then G13 (G–B–D–F–A–C–E), and so on, when inverted" ([Thirteenth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteenth_(interval))). The fifth goes too: "The most common note to omit is the fifth of the chord", because it "tends to strengthen the root of the chord without adding much character on its own" ([Open Music Theory, "Jazz Voicings"](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/jazz-voicings/)). Three common guitar voicings show it:

## Reading chord symbols

### The idea

"Chord symbols don't reference a specific key. Instead, symbols are always assumed to be a certain quality unless otherwise indicated: Triads are assumed to be major. Sevenths added to the triad are assumed to be minor. All other extensions and added tones are assumed to be major/perfect." The spellings vary: Open Music Theory lists "Cmi, Cm, C-" for C minor, "Co, Cdim, Cm(♭5), Cm(-5)" for C diminished, "C+, Caug", then "Cma7, Cmaj7, C∆7", "Cmi7, Cm7, C-7", "Cø7, Cm7♭5, C-7♭5" and "Co7", because "chord symbols were created along the way as jazz developed and were never completely standardized". Wikipedia captions its major seventh "Major seventh chord on C, notated as CΔ7", with a Greek delta, and writes the major seventh M7 when it uses letters ([Chord notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chord_notation)).

Alterations are relative: "♯11 really means "raise the eleventh"", so "a C7(♯11) chord would include an F♯ above the root—an augmented eleventh—instead of the typical F♮". The suspension comes after the extension, "as in C7sus", where sus replaces the third with the fourth. "Cadd2 indicates a C-major triad with an additional D that is voiced inside the triad: C–D–E–G". After a slash comes the bass: "C/E means to play a C major triad with an E in the bass", and "the bass note does not need to be a member of the chord! C/F♯ would indicate to play a C major triad over an F♯" ([Open Music Theory, "Chord Symbols"](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/chord-symbols/)). And alt: "C7alt" supplants "C7♯5♭9♯9♯11" ([Altered scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altered_scale)).

The course takes 41 symbols on C, in four groups ([`Lesson15.Symbols`](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/ac620ce/code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory/Lesson15.cs#L15-L64)): the 14 extended chords, 11 altered chords, 12 spellings of familiar chords taken from the two sources, and four slash chords and words: C/E, C/F♯, and two English words that start with a note letter, "Can" and "Give", which a parser should reject. A slash chord's pitch classes include its bass.

### In GA: two parsers

GA's C# has two chord-symbol parsers. `Chord.FromSymbol` is one. [`ChordSymbolParser`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Services/Chords/Parsing/ChordSymbolParser.cs#L9-L54), in `GA.Domain.Services`, is the other: GaApi's [`VoicingFilterService`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Apps/ga-server/GaApi/Services/VoicingFilterService.cs#L17-L31) uses it to find voicings for a chord name, and the chatbot's [`SpectralRagOrchestrator`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Business.Core.Orchestration/Services/SpectralRagOrchestrator.cs#L140-L149) to find the chords in a message. Both split the symbol with the same regular expression, a letter A to G and an optional `#` or `b`, ignoring case, then lower-case the rest and look it up in a table ([`Chord.cs#L110-L141`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Core/Theory/Harmony/Chord.cs#L110-L141), [`ChordSymbolParser.cs#L56-L96`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Services/Chords/Parsing/ChordSymbolParser.cs#L56-L96)). When the suffix is not in the table, `FromSymbol` throws. `ChordSymbolParser` tries [`ParseComplexSuffix`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Services/Chords/Parsing/ChordSymbolParser.cs#L98-L140): it tests whether the suffix contains `alt`, `b5`, `#5`, `b9`, `#9`, `#11` or `b13`, in that order, builds the first formula that matches ([`#L176-L188`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Domain.Services/Chords/Parsing/ChordSymbolParser.cs#L176-L188)), and otherwise returns a major triad: "Default to major if unknown". `FromSymbol` reads 14 of 41 right: the eleven extended chords, Cø7, and the two words, which it rejects. It rejects every altered chord, `C7sus` and `C9sus4`, `Cadd2`, both slash chords and ten of the twelve spellings, among them the delta of each source: GA's table has △, U+25B3, where Open Music Theory writes ∆, U+2206, and Wikipedia Δ, U+0394. `ChordSymbolParser` rejects nothing and reads 17 right. The formulas of its fallback explain the rest. `Flat5` is the intervals 4 and 6, so `C7b5` loses its seventh, and `Sharp5` the same for `C7#5`. `Sharp11` is 4, 7, 11 and 18, a major seventh, so `C7#11` and `C9#11` come back as C E G B F♯, the second without its ninth. `alt` is 4, 7, 10, 13 and 15: a perfect fifth, ♭9 and ♯9, and no altered fifth or ♭13. `C7b9#11` stops at `b9` and `C7#9b13` at `#9`, losing their second alteration; `Cm(b5)` matches `b5` and gets a major third. Thirteen chords and the word "Can" come out as C major, "Give" as G major. `Cm7b5` is in the table, but `Cm7♭5` with the sign ♭ is not: it is C major too.

Where the major triad goes, read, not run: `VoicingFilterService` asks the fretboard for the parsed chord's pitch classes ([`#L28-L31`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Apps/ga-server/GaApi/Services/VoicingFilterService.cs#L28-L31)), so voicings for "Cmi7" would be voicings of C major, without an error. The orchestrator splits a message on spaces and keeps every token that parses ([`#L142-L149`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Business.Core.Orchestration/Services/SpectralRagOrchestrator.cs#L142-L149), [`#L217-L226`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Business.Core.Orchestration/Services/SpectralRagOrchestrator.cs#L217-L226)). Its list of words to skip has "This", "Please", "Key", "The", "A" and "An", but not "Can": "Can you analyze this progression: Dm7 G7 Cmaj7" would attach a progression whose first step is "Can", read as C major. GA's tests list `C7b5`, `C7#5`, `Galt` and `C/E` among the valid symbols and check only that a chord comes back, not which one ([`ChordSymbolParserTests.cs#L11-L18`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Tests/Common/GA.Business.Core.Tests/Chords/ChordSymbolParserTests.cs#L11-L18), [`#L33-L40`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Tests/Common/GA.Business.Core.Tests/Chords/ChordSymbolParserTests.cs#L33-L40)).

## Altered chords

### The idea

"Whereas chord extension generally involves adding notes that are logically implied, chord alteration involves changing some of the typical notes. This is usually done on dominant chords, and the four alterations that are commonly used are the ♭5, ♯5, ♭9 and ♯9." Dan Haerle "argues that only fifths and ninths may be altered, as all other alterations may be interpreted as an unaltered chord tone or, enharmonically, as an altered fifth or ninth" ([Altered chord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altered_chord)). The altered scale, the seventh mode of melodic minor (lesson 11), holds them all: "The altered forms of some of the non-essential tones coincide (augmented eleventh with diminished fifth and augmented fifth with minor thirteenth)", and the scale "lacks a major ninth, a perfect eleventh, a perfect fifth, and a major thirteenth" ([Altered scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altered_scale)).

So the pitch classes alone cannot tell ♯11 from ♭5, or ♭13 from ♯5: F♯ is both. The symbol tells. In C7(♯11), C E G B♭ F♯, the fifth G stays and F♯ is an eleventh above it; in C7(♭5), C E G♭ B♭, G♭ replaces the fifth. Likewise D♯ in C7(♯9) is a ninth, not a minor third: the chord already has its third, E. The course gives each of GA's chords the notes its name says ([`Lesson15.cs#L68-L85`](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/ac620ce/code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory/Lesson15.cs#L68-L85)).

## Upper structures

### The idea

Wikipedia: "In jazz, the term upper structure or "upper structure triad" refers to a voicing approach developed by jazz pianists and arrangers defined by the sounding of a major or minor triad in the uppermost pitches of a more complex harmony." The name is the interval from the chord's root to the triad's: over C7, an E♭ major triad is "upper structure flat three, or US♭III for short" ([Upper structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_structure), after Mark Levine's *The Jazz Piano Book*). The Streeling module [MUS-005 · Jazz Harmony for Guitar](../../streeling/music/mus-005-jazz-harmony/) puts it this way: "you play a **simple triad** — something your hands already know — while the bass player provides the root and seventh."

Which triads work over C7, C E G B♭? The course keeps those without F, the eleventh, which makes a minor ninth with the third, and without B, a major seventh against B♭ ([`Lesson15.cs#L386-L402`](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/ac620ce/code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory/Lesson15.cs#L386-L402)):

## Polychords

### The idea

"In music and music theory, a polychord consists of two or more chords, one on top of the other", written "with the top chord above a line and the bottom chord below". Extended chords can be read the same way: "G7♯11♭9 (G–B–D–F–A♭–C♯) is formed from a G major triad (G–B–D) and a D♭ major triad (D♭–F–A♭)" ([Polychord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polychord)). Lesson 12 met the most famous one, Stravinsky's Petrushka chord, C major over F♯ major: six notes, the tritone scale.

## Exercises

1. Which notes does C7(♯9) have, and why does `GenerateNameWithAlterations` call it C7?
2. What does `ChordSymbolParser` return for `Cø7`, and for `Cm7♭5`, the same chord in Open Music Theory's list?
3. Over C7, which major triad makes C7♯9♭13? What are its notes' degrees over C?
4. Why does the card of D over E♭ print "mode 0"?

<details>
<summary>Solutions</summary>

1. C E G B♭ D♯, `0 3 4 7 T`. `FromSemitones` makes 3 semitones a third, and `DetectAlterationFromSemitones` never reads a third as ♯9, so the chord has no alteration: a dominant seventh, "C7". The output above shows it, with "No alterations".
2. `Cø7` is in its table: `0 3 6 T`, C E♭ G♭ B♭. `Cm7♭5`, with the sign ♭, matches neither the table nor any of the fallback's tests, which look for the letter b, so it returns a C major triad, `0 4 7`. The table of the 41 symbols shows both.
3. A♭ major, US♭VI: A♭ C E♭ are ♭13, 1 and ♯9 over C, and with C7 they make `0 3 4 7 8 T`.
4. `ModeIndex` is −1 when the set is not in its family's list, and the family holds only sets that contain C. D E♭ F♯ G A B♭ has no C; the tool prints −1 + 1.

## Key takeaways

- A chord symbol names the highest extension and implies the ones below it: C13 is seven notes, the F major collection, the same set as Fmaj13 and Gm13. Players leave out the eleventh, often the fifth, and GA's catalog stores those voicings, while its parser builds the full stack.
- `Chord.FromSymbol` reads 14 of 41 symbols right and rejects the altered chords and most textbook spellings; `ChordSymbolParser` rejects nothing, reads 17 right and turns everything it does not know, English words included, into a major triad. Both read `CM7` as a minor seventh.
- Pitch classes cannot tell ♯11 from ♭5 or ♭13 from ♯5; GA's alteration service always picks ♭5 and ♯5, reads ♯9 as a minor third, and names none of its own nine altered chords back. One alteration makes a dominant "alt".
- Over C7, twelve triads avoid the eleventh and the major seventh: Wikipedia's nine upper structures and three plain ones. GA has no pattern for them.
- `ga_polychord`'s own example names the wrong set, `AdvancedHarmony.yaml` counts twelve pitches where the Petrushka chord has six, and D♭/C, which MUS-005 called a Cmaj7♯11 sound until Demerzel #1098, is not one.
