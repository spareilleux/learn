Lesson 9 measured how far apart two chords are, in common tones and in semitones of voice leading. Neo-Riemannian theory draws a map from that idea. It has three moves, P, L and R, each of which turns a major or minor triad into another by keeping two notes and moving the third, and a lattice of notes, the Tonnetz, on which each move flips a triangle over one of its edges. [Guitar Alchemist](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga) (GA) has four pieces on the subject: a catalogue of six transformations and seven cycles, `NeoRiemannian.yaml`; the F# module that loads it, `NeoRiemannianConfig`; three methods of a C# service, `SemanticAnalysisService`; and a React component, `TonnetzDiagram`. The course program defines P, L and R from the published definition, builds the Tonnetz from the twelve pitch classes, and checks each piece against them. Its first run found something no prediction had written down: the loader returns an empty catalogue.

All GA links point to commit [`a826864`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/tree/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6). The program calls `NeoRiemannianConfig` through GA's own F# project, compiles `SemanticAnalysisService.cs` as it is, and reads the component as text. At that commit no other code of GA names the module or the service: no app, tool or test calls them. No MCP tool and no chatbot was called. The outputs come from the command below; the entry point is `l19`.

```bash
dotnet run --project code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory -c Release -- l19
```

## P, L and R

### The idea

Wikipedia: "The three transformations move one of the three notes of the triad to produce a different triad", and they "are their own inverses (a second application undoes the first)" ([Neo-Riemannian theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Riemannian_theory)). Each has a name from older harmony:

- "The P transformation exchanges a triad for its parallel: applied to a major triad, it lowers the third by a semitone (C major to C minor)";
- "The R transformation exchanges a triad for its relative: applied to a major triad, it raises the fifth by a tone (C major to A minor)";
- "The L transformation exchanges a triad for its mediant or submediant: applied to a major triad, it lowers the root by a semitone (C major to E minor)".

Lesson 5 met the first two as relations between keys: C major and C minor are parallel keys, C major and A minor relative keys. L stands for the leading-tone exchange: in C major, the root C moves to B, the leading tone.

One sentence of the same article turns the three into one rule: "P preserves the perfect fifth interval (so given say C and G there are only two candidates for the third note: E and E♭), R preserves the major third interval (given C and E our candidates are G and A), and L preserves the minor third interval (given E and G our candidates are C and B)". A major or a minor triad has one fifth, one major third and one minor third. Keep the two notes of one of them; the third note has exactly one other place to go. The course writes that rule once ([`Lesson19.Flip`](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/aab44fd/code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory/Lesson19.cs#L56-L65)): if the notes kept are the pitch classes *a* and *b*, the note *x* moves to *a* + *b* − *x*, its mirror image about the pair. For P on C major, C and G stay, 0 and 7, and E, 4, moves to 0 + 7 − 4 = 3, E♭.

The Streeling module [MAT-022 · Symmetry, Groups and Invariants](../../streeling/mathematics/mat-022-symmetry-groups-invariants/) says the same: "Each keeps two notes of the triad and reflects the third about them, so each is an inversion chosen by the triad itself, and each is an **involution**: applied twice, it returns the triad it started from" ([`mat-022-symmetry-groups-invariants.md#L96`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/Demerzel/blob/0f7a5fd110acfc4835776d38ab1a115533467d34/state/streeling/courses/mathematics/en/mat-022-symmetry-groups-invariants.md#L96)).

The program applies the three to C major and to C minor ([`Lesson19.cs#L235-L249`](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/aab44fd/code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory/Lesson19.cs#L235-L249)):

```text
== P, L and R keep two notes of a triad and move the third to its mirror image about them: the course's three, from C major and from C minor
triad  op  keeps   moves         gives  notes
C      P   C G     E to E♭ (-1)  Cm     C E♭ G
C      L   E G     C to B (-1)   Em     E G B
C      R   C E     G to A (+2)   Am     A C E
Cm     P   C G     E♭ to E (+1)  C      C E G
Cm     L   C E♭    G to A♭ (+1)  A♭     A♭ C E♭
Cm     R   E♭ G    C to B♭ (-2)  E♭     E♭ G B♭
```

On a minor triad each letter moves its note the other way, and for L and R it is another member of the triad that moves. L keeps the minor third in both cases: E and G in C major, C and E♭ in C minor.

## GA's catalogue, and a loader that returns nothing

`NeoRiemannian.yaml` describes itself as "Generated programmatically from first principles" ([`NeoRiemannian.yaml#L1-L20`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Business.Config/NeoRiemannian.yaml#L1-L20)). ### Added after the first run: what the loader catches

The loader does its work inside `try … with _ -> [], [], []` ([`#L65-L124`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Business.Config/NeoRiemannianConfig.fs#L65-L124)): whatever exception is thrown, it answers with three empty lists. It keeps the result in a `lazy` value ([`#L126`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Business.Config/NeoRiemannianConfig.fs#L126)), so the file is read once per process. [YamlDotNet](https://github.com/aaubry/YamlDotNet), the library that reads the file, cannot create a `NeoRiemannianYaml`, the record the module declares for the file's shape. It also declares each of them `private` ([`#L42-L57`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Business.Config/NeoRiemannianConfig.fs#L42-L57)). The program asks the compiled assembly what that produced, by reflection ([`Lesson19.cs#L303-L317`](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/aab44fd/code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory/Lesson19.cs#L303-L317)):

**If you write C#**, the same pair of choices gives the same result: a nested class marked `private` or `internal` handed to a serializer that wants a public constructor, and a `catch` that returns an empty default. Each is reasonable alone. Together they turn a failure into "nothing found", which looks like an answer.

### S, N and H

Wikipedia builds three more relations from the first three: "The N (or Nebenverwandt) relation exchanges a major triad for its minor subdominant, and a minor triad for its major dominant (C major and F minor). The "N" transformation can be obtained by applying R, L, and P successively"; "The S (or Slide) relation exchanges two triads that share a third (C major and C♯ minor); it can be obtained by applying L, P, and R successively in that order"; "The H relation (LPL) exchanges a triad for its hexatonic pole (C major and A♭ minor)". The file's header writes them `L o P o R`, `R o L o P` and `L o P o L`. A word such as "LPR" can be read from the left, L first, or from the right, as mathematicians read a composition. The program applies each word both ways and compares with the file's own mappings ([`Lesson19.cs#L343-L349`](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/aab44fd/code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory/Lesson19.cs#L343-L349)):

```text
== S, N and H as the file's header writes them, read from left to right and from right to left, against the file's own mappings
name  header     left to right  right to left  order  from C
S     L o P o R  24 of 24       24 of 24       2      C♯m
N     R o L o P  24 of 24       24 of 24       2      Fm
H     L o P o L  24 of 24       24 of 24       2      G♯m
```

Both readings give the file's mappings on all 24 triads. MAT-022 names the reason among its pitfalls: "Left to right and right to left agree only when the product is an involution, as for S" ([`#L205`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/Demerzel/blob/0f7a5fd110acfc4835776d38ab1a115533467d34/state/streeling/courses/mathematics/en/mat-022-symmetry-groups-invariants.md#L205)). Each of the three words is of order 2: applied twice, it brings every triad back. The course spells Wikipedia's A♭ minor as G♯ minor.

GA's catalogue of invariants states invariant 15 as "PLR composites match: L∘P∘R = S, P∘L = N" ([`invariants-catalog.md#L40`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/docs/methodology/invariants-catalog.md#L40), and again at [`#L100`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/docs/methodology/invariants-catalog.md#L100)). The program tests the second half ([`Lesson19.cs#L351-L355`](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/aab44fd/code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory/Lesson19.cs#L351-L355)):

```text
== docs/methodology/invariants-catalog.md, invariant 15, "PLR composites match: L∘P∘R = S, P∘L = N": words of two and three letters against the file's N
PL   equals N on  0 of 24 triads; order 3; from C: A♭
LP   equals N on  0 of 24 triads; order 3; from C: E
PLR  equals N on 24 of 24 triads; order 2; from C: Fm
RLP  equals N on 24 of 24 triads; order 2; from C: Fm
```

P∘L equals N on no triad, in either reading. Each letter swaps major and minor, so a word of two letters sends a major triad to a major triad, C major to A♭ major or to E major, and N swaps them. The words of three letters PLR and RLP both equal N on the 24 triads. The row of the catalogue just above, invariant 14, says that P, L and R are involutions and marks it true "by construction", with `NeoRiemannianConfig.fs` as its artifact ([`#L39`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/docs/methodology/invariants-catalog.md#L39)): the mappings of the file do satisfy it, and the module returns none of them.

## The Tonnetz

### The idea

Wikipedia: "the Tonnetz (German for 'tone net') is a conceptual lattice diagram representing tonal space first described by Leonhard Euler in 1739" ([Tonnetz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonnetz)). In its neo-Riemannian form it "is a planar array of pitches along three simplicial axes, corresponding to the three consonant intervals. Major and minor triads are represented by triangles which tile the plane of the Tonnetz. Edge-adjacent triads share two common pitches, and so the principal transformations are expressed as minimal motion of the Tonnetz" ([Neo-Riemannian theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Riemannian_theory)).

The course program draws a patch of it around C major ([`Diagrams.Tonnetz`](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/aab44fd/code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory/Diagrams.cs#L285-L330)):

<Tonnetz role="img" aria-label="A patch of the Tonnetz: four rows of five notes. Each row is a line of fifths: B flat, F, C, G, D in the row of C. From each note, the note up and to the right is a major third higher and the note down and to the right a minor third higher. The triangle C, E, G, C major, is filled. The three triangles that share an edge with it carry a letter: P on C, E flat, G, below it; L on E, G, B, to its right; R on A, C, E, to its left." />

Fifths run to the right, major thirds up and to the right, minor thirds down and to the right. Every triangle is a triad: those that point up are major, those that point down minor. C major is the filled triangle. Flip it over its edge C–G, the fifth, and it lands on C E♭ G: that is P. Over E–G, the minor third, it lands on E G B: L. Over C–E, the major third, on A C E: R.

The patch already repeats notes: B♭ sits in the row of C and again in the row below, D in the row of C and again in the row above. On an endless sheet every note comes back in both directions. Wikipedia: "neo-Riemannian theory typically assumes enharmonic equivalence (G♯ = A♭), which wraps the planar graph into a torus".

### In numbers

The program builds the same object without a drawing: twelve pitch classes, joined when they lie a minor third, a major third or a fifth apart ([`Lesson19.cs#L430-L457`](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/aab44fd/code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory/Lesson19.cs#L430-L457)):

```text
== The Tonnetz as a graph: the 12 pitch classes, joined when they lie a minor third, a major third or a fifth apart
notes: 12; edges: 36, 12 of interval class 3, 12 of interval class 4, 12 of interval class 5
triangles: 28; with one edge of each kind: 24, all of them among the 24 triads: True; the others: 4, with the edges 4 4 4: C E G♯, C♯ F A, D F♯ A♯, D♯ G B
notes - edges + triads = 0
triads on each note: 6; triads on each edge: 2
the two triads on an edge of interval class 5 are each other's P: 12 of 12
the two triads on an edge of interval class 3 are each other's L: 12 of 12
the two triads on an edge of interval class 4 are each other's R: 12 of 12
```

There are 36 edges and 28 triangles. The 24 triangles with one edge of each kind are the 24 triads. The four others are made of three major thirds: the augmented triads, which on the drawing are not triangles but three notes on one straight line, such as C, E and G♯. With 12 notes, 36 edges and 24 triads, notes − edges + triads is 0, the number of a torus: Wikipedia notes that "the surfaces of toroidal polyhedra all have Euler characteristic 0, like the torus" ([Euler characteristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_characteristic)).

Each note belongs to 6 triads and each edge to 2. The two triads on an edge are each other's P when the edge is a fifth, L when it is a minor third and R when it is a major third, 12 times out of 12 for each kind. That is the rule of the first section read on the map: the edge is the interval kept.

### The graph of the 24 triads

Turn the map inside out: let each triad be a point, and join two triads when one move, P, L or R, leads from one to the other. The program searches that graph from C major, breadth first ([`Lesson19.Words`](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/aab44fd/code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory/Lesson19.cs#L89-L103), [`#L459-L470`](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/aab44fd/code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory/Lesson19.cs#L459-L470)):

```text
== The graph of the 24 triads joined by P, L and R: from C major, the triads at each distance with a shortest word, their common tones with C major and the movement to them
steps  triads  common  movement  triad and word
0      1       3       0         C
1      3       2       1 to 2    Cm P, Em L, Am R
2      6       1       2 to 3    A♭ PL, E♭ PR, E LP, G LR, A RP, F RL
3      8       0 to 1  2 to 5    G♯m PLP, Fm PLR, E♭m PRP, Gm PRL, C♯m LPR, Bm LRL, F♯m RPR, Dm RLR
4      5       0       3 to 6    B PLPR, D♭ PLRL, F♯ PRPR, B♭ PRLR, D LRLR
5      1       0       5         B♭m PLRLR
edges: 36; triads at each distance, from each of the 24: 1 3 6 8 5 1; five steps from C: B♭m; from Cm: D
```

Three triads are one step away, six are two steps away, and the farthest, B♭ minor, is five steps away. The count 1, 3, 6, 8, 5, 1 is the same from each of the 24 triads. The common tones fall as the steps rise, but the movement does not follow: the triad at five steps needs 5 semitones, while one of those at four steps needs 6.

**If you write C#**, the search is fifteen lines. `Triad` is a `readonly record struct`, so two triads with the same root and the same quality are equal and can be the keys of a dictionary. A queue holds the triads still to visit, and the dictionary keeps the first word that reached each triad, which is one of the shortest.

Wikipedia reports that criticism: "the C major triad is closer to F major than to F minor, since C major can be transformed into F major by R-then-L, while it takes three moves to get from C major to F minor (R-then-L-then-P). However, from a chromatic voice-leading perspective F minor is closer to C major than F major is", and "F minor and E♭ minor are both three steps from C major, even though F minor and C major have one common tone, while E♭ minor and C major have none" ([Neo-Riemannian theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Riemannian_theory)). The program measures the three ([`Lesson19.cs#L472-L477`](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/aab44fd/code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory/Lesson19.cs#L472-L477)):

```text
== Wikipedia's three examples, from C major: steps on the graph against common tones and movement
triad  steps  word  common  movement
F      2      RL    1       3
Fm     3      PLR   1       2
E♭m    3      PRP   0       4
```

F major is two steps away and costs 3 semitones; F minor is three steps away and costs 2. The steps of the graph are one measure of distance, and lesson 9's movement another.

## Chromatic mediants

The mediant and the submediant of a key are the chords a third above and a third below its tonic: in C major, E minor and A minor. Wikipedia: "A chromatic mediant relationship defined conservatively is a relationship between two sections and/or chords whose roots are related by a major third or minor third, and contain one common tone (thereby sharing the same quality, i.e. major or minor)", so that "C major has four chromatic mediants: E major, A major, E♭ major, and A♭ major". The article adds that Allen Forte "describes an even more distant "doubly-chromatic mediant" relationship shared by two chords of the opposite mode, with roots a third apart and no common tones; for example C major and E♭ or A♭ minor" ([Chromatic mediant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_mediant)).

Eight triads have a root a third from C. The program sorts them by common tones with C major ([`Lesson19.cs#L481-L491`](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/aab44fd/code/music-theory-ga/GaTheory/Lesson19.cs#L481-L491)):

```text
== The eight triads whose root lies a third from C, from C major: common tones, a shortest word, the file's transform if it has one, and the name of the relation
triad  root      common  word  file  relation
Em     M3 above  2       L     L     diatonic
Am     m3 below  2       R     R     diatonic
E      M3 above  1       LP    -     chromatic mediant
A      m3 below  1       RP    -     chromatic mediant
E♭     m3 above  1       PR    -     chromatic mediant
A♭     M3 below  1       PL    -     chromatic mediant
E♭m    m3 above  0       PRP   -     doubly chromatic mediant
G♯m    M3 below  0       PLP   H     doubly chromatic mediant
GA's cadence "Chromatic Mediant (Metal)" of lesson 7, Em to Gm: common tones 1, word RP, movement 3
```

For these eight, the common tones and the letters add up to three. The two diatonic mediants, E minor and A minor, share two notes and are one letter away: they are L and R. The four chromatic mediants share one note and are two letters away: P and one of the other two, in either order. The two doubly chromatic mediants share none and are three letters away; one of them, A♭ minor, which the course writes G♯ minor, is the file's H, the hexatonic pole. Of the eight, the file has a transformation for three, and none for the four chromatic mediants: they are words of two letters, which `Apply` does not compose.

Lesson 7 met a cadence that GA's `Cadences.yaml` calls "Chromatic Mediant (Metal)", E minor to G minor ([`Cadences.yaml#L124-L129`](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/a826864f3a012cad88e415954bf57eca0ce12aa6/Common/GA.Business.Config/Cadences.yaml#L124-L129)). Its two triads are minor, their roots a minor third apart, and they share one note: it fits Wikipedia's definition, and the word that joins them is R, then P.

<summary>Solutions</summary>

1. A minor is A C E. P keeps the fifth A–E and raises C by a semitone to C♯: A major. L keeps the minor third A–C and raises E by a semitone to F: F major. R keeps the major third C–E and lowers A by a tone to G: C major.

## Key takeaways

- P, L and R each keep one interval of a triad, the fifth, the minor third or the major third, and move the remaining note to its mirror image. Each undoes itself and swaps major and minor. GA's `SemanticAnalysisService` computes all three right on the 24 triads, for a caller that already knows the root.
- At `a826864`, and on `main` on 2026-10-11, `NeoRiemannianConfig` returns no transformation and no cycle: its three records are `private`, YamlDotNet cannot create them, and the loader turns the exception into empty lists. With the three words removed it returns everything ([GA #830](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/issues/830)).
- The content of `NeoRiemannian.yaml` is right: six involutions, their costs, four hexatonic and three octatonic cycles. Four sentences about it are not: P∘L = N in the invariants catalogue, and in the file "shares the root" for N, the roots said to form a hexatonic set, and the numbers of `OCT(i,j)` said to be interval classes ([GA #831](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/issues/831)).
- The Tonnetz has 12 notes, 36 edges and 24 triangles, and closes into a torus. Flipping a triangle over an edge is P, L or R. On the graph of the 24 triads no triad is more than five moves from another, and the number of moves is not the voice-leading distance: F minor is three moves from C major and 2 semitones away.
- The chromatic mediants of C major are the four major triads two letters away; the diatonic mediants are L and R, and the hexatonic pole is one of the two doubly chromatic ones.
- `ExplainTriadRelationship` returns one sentence for every pair of triads. `TonnetzDiagram` places its rows so that three pairs of neighbours are a semitone apart, and fills cells by their position in a list of fifths where the scale's bits stand for pitch classes.
