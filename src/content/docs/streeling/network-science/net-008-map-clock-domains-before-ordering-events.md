## The project question

An audio agent reports a scheduled time in seconds; a UI agent reports a callback time in milliseconds. Can another agent subtract the numbers or infer which action came first? [MCL-007](../../musicology/mcl-007-integrate-a-midi-tempo-map-before-scheduling/) maps musical coordinates to seconds. This lesson distinguishes that map from a relation between two clocks. [NET-004](../../network-science/net-004-semantic-backpressure-and-evidence-dependencies/) requires evidence bound to its action; a timestamp also needs its clock identity and scope.

The dated [Web Audio 1.1 Working Draft](https://www.w3.org/TR/2026/WD-webaudio-1.1-20260922/) describes an audio timeline and a mapping estimate through `getOutputTimestamp()`. [High Resolution Time](https://www.w3.org/TR/2026/WD-hr-time-3-20260901/) defines performance timestamps in milliseconds with a time origin. Both cited documents are working drafts. Reading them does not establish which features a particular browser implements.

## 1. Units alone do not align origins

Let c be an authored audio-context coordinate in seconds and p a performance-clock coordinate in milliseconds. Stipulate the model:

```text
p = a + b*c
a = 10000 ms
b = 1000.1 ms per context second
```

A target at c = 10 maps to p = 20001 ms. A scheduling call at p = 19980 ms precedes that mapped target by 21 ms. Multiplying seconds by 1000 without accounting for origin and rate does not produce this comparison.

### Exercise 1 — Compare compatible coordinates

Calculate the lead between the call and target. Why is directly subtracting 19980 from 10 meaningless, and why is unit conversion alone still insufficient?

> **Answer.** The mapped target is 20001 ms and the lead is 21 ms. The raw numbers use different units and origins. A unit conversion supplies neither the 10000 ms offset nor the assumed rate relation. The result is conditional on the model, not a measured scheduling margin.

## 2. Two anchors fit a rate; they do not validate it

Use authored anchors (c = 0, p = 10000) and (c = 60, p = 70006). Their slope is 60006/60 = 1000.1 ms per context second. Relative to 1000 ms per second, the difference is 100 parts per million. This is an apparent rate in invented data, not observed device drift.

One anchor can fix an offset only after a rate is assumed. Two exact anchors determine an affine line, but cannot establish that clocks remain affine, identify sample noise or justify an indefinite extrapolation.

### Exercise 2 — Identify the missing assumption

Derive the slope, intercept and rate difference. What could not be identified from the first anchor alone?

> **Answer.** The slope is 1000.1, the intercept 10000 ms and the relative rate difference 100 parts per million. With only one anchor, many slopes and matching intercepts pass through the point. A rate assumption or more paired observations is needed; a fitted line alone does not prove clock stability.

## 3. State interpolation and extrapolation separately

The [clock fixture](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/main/code/streeling/evidence/tempo-clocks-2026-10-22/clock-map.json) predicts p = 40003 ms at c = 30 and p = 130012 ms at c = 120. Keeping the same intercept but using nominal slope 1000 predicts 130000 ms at c = 120, a 12 ms discrepancy under the authored model. The point at 30 is between anchors; the point at 120 is beyond them.

### Exercise 3 — Bound the timestamp claim

Calculate both predictions and the discrepancy at 120. Does the two-anchor fit make the later prediction an observed event or validate its uncertainty?

> **Answer.** The predictions are 40003 and 130012 ms; the nominal-rate value at 120 is 130000 ms, differing by 12 ms. Neither prediction is an observed event. Extrapolation requires a continuing model assumption and its own error assessment, especially after suspension, restart or device changes.

## 4. Propagate bounds before accepting a held-out point

Assume each anchor's performance coordinate has error at most 0.5 ms, the context coordinates are exact, and one affine relation applies. At c = 90 the interpolation weights extended beyond the anchors are -0.5 and 1.5. The predicted central value is 100009 ms, with a deterministic error bound of 1 ms. This is a bound under stated assumptions, not a confidence interval.

Now supply an authored held-out value 100011 ms with error at most 0.5 ms. The prediction interval is 100008 to 100010 ms; the held-out interval is 100010.5 to 100011.5 ms. They do not intersect.

### Exercise 4 — Let the model fail

Compute the bound using the absolute weights and compare the 2 ms residual with the combined error allowance. What has been falsified, and which cause remains unknown?

> **Answer.** The bound is 0.5 times (0.5 + 1.5) = 1 ms. Including the held-out error allows at most 1.5 ms residual, smaller than the authored 2 ms. The combined affine relation, coordinate assumptions and error bounds are inconsistent with this held-out record. This does not isolate drift, timestamp error, an origin change or another cause. Do not silently refit and relabel the original model as verified.

## 5. Carry clock evidence through the handoff

The pinned [dashboard component](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/76efbffd5af82b46c3146957a6bd4504008eeb89/Apps/ga-dashboard/src/app/shared/progression.component.ts#L84-L167) schedules a browser callback, then reads `AudioContext.currentTime` to request oscillator starts. The examined file contains no paired output timestamps or measured clock mapping. It is a selected demonstrator, not proof about every GA playback surface.

The Web Audio draft's paired timestamp is an estimate near an output stream position; it is not a microphone observation or a general multi-host synchronization protocol. It also warns against treating the difference between `currentTime` and the returned output-stream coordinate as a reliable output-latency estimate. Preserve device, context and lifecycle identity when interpreting such evidence.

### Exercise 5 — Propose a bounded receipt

What must accompany a cross-agent timestamp comparison? Give a simpler alternative to introducing a clock DSL, a rejection condition and a measurement still required for physical audio latency.

> **Answer.** Retain clock identifiers, units, origins, paired anchors, model and error assumptions, validity interval, lifecycle changes, source identity and the comparison's endpoints. A single common monotonic acquisition clock may be simpler than a new protocol. Reject or defer comparisons when mappings fail held-out bounds or identity changes invalidate them. A physical audio-latency claim needs an independently defined and calibrated output observation. This is an advisory record proposal; no receipt schema, synchronization service or clock DSL was implemented.
