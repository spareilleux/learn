A coordinator adds routes between fictional evidence stages and claims more throughput because more paths exist. Several paths can share their final link. Reachability and route count do not account for capacity conservation. The scheduling prerequisite measures a finite completion horizon; this model instead bounds an idealized steady rate.

NetworkX’s official flow documentation defines capacities and returns a flow value. We use that concept without calling the package. Show a feasible flow and a cut of equal capacity: the former is a lower witness, the latter an upper bound. Their agreement certifies the optimum for this authored graph. Counting paths alone supplies neither witness.

The sink cut is especially easy to inspect here. Every delivered unit must enter the sink on one of its incoming edges. The control reduces an incoming capacity while keeping every edge present, so all routes still exist but the rate bound drops. Translating this result into a real agent pipeline would require compatible units, actual capacities and workload assumptions; no such measurements are supplied.

### Exercise 1

Give a feasible fixed-case flow.

<details><summary>Answer 1</summary>

Send `2` units through `s-a-t` and `2` through `s-b-t`, with `0` on `a-b`. Total flow is `4` and conservation holds.

</details>

### Exercise 2

Give a matching upper bound.

<details><summary>Answer 2</summary>

The edges entering `t` have total capacity `4`. Every feasible delivered flow crosses that cut, so `4` is also an upper bound.

</details>

### Exercise 3

Count simple routes.

<details><summary>Answer 3</summary>

There are `3`: `s-a-t`, `s-b-t` and `s-a-b-t`. Route count is not flow capacity.

</details>

### Exercise 4

Apply the reduced-capacity control.

<details><summary>Answer 4</summary>

Maximum flow and sink-cut capacity become `3`; simple route count remains `3`. Removing capacity need not remove reachability.

</details>

### Exercise 5

Does the optimum guarantee a project delivery rate?

<details><summary>Answer 5</summary>

No. Steady divisible flow omits task granularity, failures, timing and semantic compatibility. The numerical optimum is conditional on this model.

</details>
