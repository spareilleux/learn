## The project question

Should an Observatory side tool run uncapped because it reports a very high frame rate? The [BRUTAL evaluation](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/76efbffd5af82b46c3146957a6bd4504008eeb89/Experiments/Brutal/README.md) records both a strong coexistence failure and an inconclusive follow-up. A product decision needs both, plus the adjacent task's behavior. This lesson uses that evidence to design a defensible next comparison; [PM-004](../../product-management/pm-004-craft-extension-release-decision/) addresses extension release gates more broadly.

All documents are read at public GA commit `76efbffd5af82b46c3146957a6bd4504008eeb89`. That pins the documents' bytes. The historical run headers separately name earlier revisions and report uncommitted changes. The document snapshot does not identify an exact reproducible binary for those earlier runs. The [historical comparison fixture](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/main/code/streeling/evidence/brutal-composition-2026-10-22/historical-comparison.json) preserves that distinction.

## 1. Measure the task that must keep working

The [uncapped M2 report](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/76efbffd5af82b46c3146957a6bd4504008eeb89/Experiments/Brutal/evidence/m2-20261019-235218.md) reports HallSlice at 10059 fps while the adjacent RTX view fell from a preceding median of 60 fps to a during-run median of 2 fps. It also records a failed camera-key check. The same document records successful presentation and cleanup, so “the window worked” and “the whole evaluation passed” are different claims.

The view's median change is `(2-60)/60`, approximately -96.67 percent. That arithmetic describes the recorded before/during comparison. It does not isolate the underlying scheduling mechanism, prove every uncapped run causes that drop or measure the current machine. A high rate in the producer does not establish useful throughput for the person watching the adjacent task.

### Practice exercise 1: state the product impact

Calculate the relative median change in the adjacent view. Why can HallSlice's own frame rate and a clean exit not approve coexistence?

> **Answer:** `(2-60)/60` is approximately -0.9667, a 96.67 percent decrease in the reported view median. HallSlice's rate and lifecycle describe its own behavior. They do not satisfy the separate requirement that the adjacent view remain usable; the report also includes a failed camera-key check. Preserve the full recorded outcome without promoting this one comparison into a universal causal rule.

## 2. Keep controls that challenge the first interpretation

The [M3 control report](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/76efbffd5af82b46c3146957a6bd4504008eeb89/Experiments/Brutal/evidence/m3-20261020-000727.md) alternates controls without HallSlice and uncapped runs. Its stated passing rule requires the view's minimum to be at least 58 fps.

| Condition | View minima, fps | View medians, fps | Recorded drops |
|---|---|---|---|
| Three controls | 57, 60, 49 | 60, 60, 57 | 2 |
| Three uncapped runs | 60, 60, 38 | 60, 60, 51 | 1 |

Two controls dipped while HallSlice was absent. The public README calls the follow-up inconclusive: the initial severe collapse was not reproduced, and the adjacent view varied on its own. These small sequential samples have differing observation durations and a moving scene. Counting fewer drops in the uncapped group does not prove that uncapped rendering protects the view.

### Practice exercise 2: update the claim

Under the stated 58 fps minimum rule, count drops in both conditions. What does this challenge, and why would “uncapped improves coexistence” also be unsupported?

> **Answer:** Control minima 57 and 49 fail, giving two drops; uncapped minimum 38 fails, giving one. Variation without HallSlice challenges attributing every smaller dip to that process. The small sequential windows do not hold scene phase and other load constant, so the reverse causal claim is unsupported too. Keep the original M2 observation and the inconclusive follow-up visible.

## 3. Check both the metric definition and the decision rule

The public README says M2 timed work from the start of a frame to its present. M3 timed one present to the next. Their p50 values therefore have different endpoints: comparing 0.073 ms in the uncapped M2 report with 0.064 ms in the M3 sweep does not establish a speed improvement under a common measure.

The [M3 sweep](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/76efbffd5af82b46c3146957a6bd4504008eeb89/Experiments/Brutal/evidence/m3-20261020-000131.md) used a stricter view-minimum rule of 60 fps. At caps 120 and 480 it reported a minimum of 59 and marked drops. Reclassifying those values under a hypothetical 58 fps rule would mark them passing, but it would not change the observations or the original recorded rule. Keep original and alternative labels in separate columns. Set the rule for a new trial before its outcomes are known.

### Practice exercise 3: audit an apparent improvement

An agent combines the two p50 values into a “faster” claim and changes the sweep's 59 fps labels to pass. Identify the two independent problems and the evidence needed to compare fairly.

> **Answer:** The p50 values summarize intervals with different endpoints, and the label change applies a different threshold after observing the data. Use a common timing definition and retain each original rule. An alternative 58 fps classification can be shown explicitly, but a new accepted comparison needs a predeclared rule and measurements from the same protocol.

## 4. Separate a requested capability from its observed operation

The public [Colossus source comments](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/76efbffd5af82b46c3146957a6bd4504008eeb89/Experiments/Brutal/Colossus/Program.cs#L29-L41) document `--dlss` and `--rr`, external NVIDIA components and fallback behavior. They also describe a validated Lego scene being handed to the graphics host. These are source observations. The fresh CPU tests in [VC-009](../../visual-computing/vc-009-budget-brutal-scene-expansion/) exercised the pure description path, not those graphics features.

A benchmark record should distinguish the requested mode, the available capability, the mode actually activated and the output/timing observed. If a feature falls back, label that run accordingly. A requested flag or an accepted scene cannot prove DLSS image quality, ray-tracing speed or coexistence on another host.

### Practice exercise 4: classify capability evidence

An agent attaches a command containing `--dlss` and a successful CPU description, then marks the renderer's DLSS performance gate passed. What is missing?

> **Answer:** The record needs an actual graphics run showing capability availability, successful activation or explicit fallback, the effective rendering mode and the declared quality/performance measurements on identified hardware and driver. Neither the flag text nor CPU scene validation supplies those observations. No such graphics result was produced for this lesson.

## 5. Design a comparison that could change the default

For a future coexistence test, freeze source/binary identity and any dirty diff, scene, camera phase, seed, hardware/driver, present mode, cap, timing endpoints and sampling duration. Repeat controls and treatments at matched scene phases; counterbalance their order. Observe the adjacent task as well as the new tool, retain all runs and declare resource headroom and abort guards. These are proposed requirements, not a test executed here.

The product question is whether the chosen default meets the declared coexistence requirement under the tested workload. If controls are too variable to distinguish an effect, report uncertainty and improve the comparison. The historical evidence supports keeping the coexistence risk open; it does not certify an uncapped default or establish a universal safe cap.

### Practice exercise 5: choose the next experiment

Write a bounded next comparison for capped and uncapped operation beside the Observatory. Name a result that would prevent approving an uncapped default and explain what the result would still not establish.

> **Answer:** Compare repeated, order-balanced controls, capped runs and uncapped runs at fixed scene/camera phases with the same timing and observation windows. Pin effective modes, binary/source state, host/driver and a predeclared adjacent-view rule. A reproducible uncapped violation under that protocol prevents approval for the tested workload; an inconclusive result also leaves approval unsupported. A pass would remain conditional on that host, workload and protocol, rather than proving universal GPU coexistence.
