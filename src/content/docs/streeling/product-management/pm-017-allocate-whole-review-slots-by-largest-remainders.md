A future review plan has a fixed number of indivisible slots and a declared mixture of task classes. Multiplying the total by the target proportions usually produces fractional quotas. Rounding every quota independently may produce the wrong total. The largest-remainder method preserves the fixed slot count while making the final rounding decisions visible.

Compute exact quotas from nonnegative weights and their sum. Allocate the floor of every quota, then distribute the remaining slots in descending order of fractional remainder. Declare an index-order tie rule before inspecting a tie. Recompute the full allocation when the total changes rather than appending a slot to an earlier result by intuition.

The control increases the total and changes which class has the largest residual entitlement. These are fictional planning slots, not claims about actual reviewer availability. This method does not minimize estimator variance, guarantee fairness under every definition, or satisfy specialized skill constraints automatically. Quotas encode a chosen policy and require a reason. A future planning DSL would carry the weights, total, floor allocation and tie rule so that another agent can reproduce the exact integer result.

## Corrected exercises

### Exercise 1

Allocate the fixed total.

> **Answer:** Quotas `[7/2,21/10,7/5]` have floors `[3,2,1]`. The remaining slot goes to index `0`, giving `[4,2,1]`.

### Exercise 2

Recompute the larger-total control.

> **Answer:** For `8` slots, quotas are `[4,12/5,8/5]`; index `2` gets the surplus and allocation is `[4,2,2]`.

### Exercise 3

Why declare a tie rule?

> **Answer:** Equal remainders may compete for fewer remaining slots. A fixed rule makes the result reproducible without claiming that the rule is uniquely fair.
