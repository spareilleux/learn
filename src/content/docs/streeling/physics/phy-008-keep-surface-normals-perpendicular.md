## The project question

An agent stretches a sculpted part and rotates a child component. The positions look mathematically valid, but should a transformed local axis still be used as the surface normal? A normal supplies a direction perpendicular to a surface. Lighting and surface sampling can use that direction, so preserving positions alone is insufficient.

The case comes from public [BRUTAL space conventions](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/76efbffd5af82b46c3146957a6bd4504008eeb89/Experiments/Brutal/Lego/Space.fs) and [nested composition](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/76efbffd5af82b46c3146957a6bd4504008eeb89/Experiments/Brutal/Lego/Generate.fs#L165), pinned to GA commit `76efbffd5af82b46c3146957a6bd4504008eeb89`. Matrices act on row vectors, and a child's world matrix is its local matrix times its parent's. [CS-014](../../computer-science/cs-014-compose-brutal-scene-descriptions/) introduces that order; here we test a geometric invariant after composition.

## 1. Test perpendicularity, not just unit length

Consider a local box face with outward normal along positive X and tangents along Y and Z. After an invertible linear transformation, a valid normal must still have zero dot product with both transformed tangents. Normalization makes a vector's length one; it does not repair its direction.

The [PBRT derivation](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Geometry_and_Transformations/Applying_Transformations) obtains the inverse-transpose normal rule from that perpendicularity condition. Keep matrix convention explicit when using the formula. The native teaching wrapper calls [.NET's Vector3.TransformNormal](https://learn.microsoft.com/dotnet/api/system.numerics.vector3.transformnormal?view=net-10.0) with either the world matrix or its explicitly computed inverse transpose. The method name does not supply that choice on the caller's behalf.

### Practice exercise 1: reject a normalized but tilted normal

A unit candidate normal has dot product 0.6 with a unit surface tangent. Has normalization made it a valid normal? State a test that addresses the missing property.

> **Answer:** No. A nonzero dot product means the candidate is not perpendicular to that tangent. Test its dot products with two independent transformed tangents, using a declared numerical tolerance. Unit length is a separate property; renormalizing the same direction cannot make the 0.6 dot product vanish.

## 2. Compose a case that defeats the shortcut

Use a child yaw of 45 degrees under a parent scale of `(2,1,1)`. In row-vector order the linear world matrix is `R45*S`. Its transformed local X and Z axes are `(sqrt2,0,-1/sqrt2)` and `(sqrt2,0,1/sqrt2)`. Their raw dot product is 1.5, so the axes are not perpendicular. Their lengths are both `sqrt(2.5)` and their normalized dot product is 0.6.

This effective shear arises from the composition. In the control order `S*R45`, the scaled axes are rotated together and remain perpendicular. A single nonuniform scale on these axis-aligned box faces does not automatically produce the same discrepancy. The [actual native cases](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/main/code/streeling/evidence/scene-frames-2026-10-22/native-cases.json) retain both orders rather than attributing every error to scale alone.

### Practice exercise 2: distinguish the two compositions

Calculate the raw and normalized dot products for the stated nested X/Z axes. Why is a scale-then-yaw control useful?

> **Answer:** The raw dot product is `2-0.5=1.5`; dividing by the product of lengths 2.5 gives 0.6. In scale-then-yaw order the transformed box axes remain perpendicular. The control shows that composition order and the resulting basis geometry matter; nonuniform scale alone is not a sufficient explanation for this counterexample.

## 3. Compare the two directions directly

For that face, normalizing the transformed X axis gives `(2/sqrt5,0,-1/sqrt5)`. Applying the inverse transpose and normalizing gives `(1/sqrt5,0,-2/sqrt5)`. The latter has dot product zero with the transformed Z tangent; the former has normalized dot product 0.6. Their mutual cosine is 0.8, corresponding to approximately 36.869897646 degrees.

The [normal comparison](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/main/code/streeling/evidence/scene-frames-2026-10-22/normal-comparison.json) separates exact expressions from actual float32 helper arithmetic. The native checks use tolerance `1e-5`. These are calculated directions, not measured pixel errors or evidence that a viewer noticed a lighting change.

### Practice exercise 3: verify the correction

Using the two stated unit candidates and unit Z tangent `(2/sqrt5,0,1/sqrt5)`, compute each candidate's tangent dot product and their mutual cosine. Which invariant selects the perpendicular direction?

> **Answer:** The transformed-axis candidate gives `(4-1)/5=0.6`; the inverse-transpose candidate gives `(2-2)/5=0`. The candidates' mutual cosine is `(2+2)/5=0.8`. Perpendicularity selects the second direction for this transformed face. The approximately 36.869897646-degree difference is geometric, not an observed visual-quality score.

## 4. Locate the source boundary that uses the direction

The inspected [raster shader](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/76efbffd5af82b46c3146957a6bd4504008eeb89/Experiments/Brutal/Colossus/Program.cs#L453) already uses an inverse transpose. A separate [swarm face-sampling path](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/76efbffd5af82b46c3146957a6bd4504008eeb89/Experiments/Brutal/Colossus/Program.cs#L3625-L3638) normalizes a transformed box axis and calculates face area from the cross product of the other axes. Under orthogonal transformed axes, those normal directions agree; the authored composed case exposes a narrower assumption.

### Practice exercise 4: write a bounded engineering finding

An agent reports “BRUTAL lighting is broken by all stretched objects.” Replace that statement with the actual evidence and the next discriminating check.

> **Answer:** The pinned raster shader uses inverse-transpose normals; the separate face sampler uses transformed axes. An authored nested yaw under nonuniform scale makes the latter direction nonperpendicular in CPU arithmetic, while the orthogonal control agrees. Next, exercise the actual sampler on that placement and check its consumers and output before claiming a rendered or visible defect. No such host execution occurred here.

## 5. Handle singularity as a separate question

The existing [validator](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/76efbffd5af82b46c3146957a6bd4504008eeb89/Experiments/Brutal/Lego/Validate.fs) checks positive finite local box sizes and finite placement entries. In the actual teaching drafts, zero X scale passes those checks but has determinant 0 and no inverse. Negative local size and a NaN placement are refused. Bounds can still be computed for the collapsed box; they do not certify a nondegenerate solid or a usable inverse-transpose normal.

A proposed host rule could refuse singular placements where a consumer requires an inverse. Its counterargument is that another consumer may intentionally accept a flattened primitive. Restricting supported transforms may be simpler than broadening every sampler. Either approach needs a defined consumer requirement and compatibility tests; this course changes no validator or public contract. [SEM-006](../../semiotics/sem-006-preserve-orientation-across-scene-frames/) treats orientation separately from invertibility.

### Practice exercise 5: avoid promoting acceptance into a guarantee

A zero-scale draft was accepted and has finite bounds. May an agent claim that every host can safely shade it? What evidence would justify an additional gate?

> **Answer:** No. The observed gate checks local shape and finite placement, while the zero-scale matrix is singular. A consumer that requires inversion needs an explicit handling rule and an actual test of that path. Evidence that the consumer never requires an inverse, or deliberately handles the collapsed case, would weaken the need for a blanket rejection. Acceptance, finite bounds, normal correctness and observed rendering remain separate results.
