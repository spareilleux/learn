## The project question

A guitar-analysis agent sees a falling short-window level and reports faster damping. Could a different mechanism create the same observation? [OpenStax's beat equation](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/17-6-beats) follows from adding two cosines. We use that mathematical identity with independently constructed signals and original exercises. No third-party diagram, activity or recording is reproduced.

Use frequencies 327.031956626 Hz and 329.627556913 Hz, as constructed in [WML-004](../../world-music-languages/wml-004-preserve-intonation-beyond-pitch-classes/). Each component has fixed peak amplitude `a=0.125`, initial cosine phase 0 and sample rate 44100 Hz. No component loses amplitude in the untapered mathematical model. Its changing sum gives a counterexample to the proposed inference.

## 1. Derive the sum before naming its envelope

Write `x_a(t)=a*cos(2*pi*f_a*t)` and `x_b(t)=a*cos(2*pi*f_b*t)`. Their sum equals `2*a*cos(pi*(f_b-f_a)*t)*cos(2*pi*((f_a+f_b)/2)*t)`. The difference is 2.595600287 Hz and the average is 328.329756769 Hz. A numerical comparison of the two forms across four seconds stayed within the declared absolute tolerance `1e-10`.

The signed modulator has frequency `abs(f_b-f_a)/2`, approximately 1.297800144 Hz. The **unsigned** envelope is `2*a*abs(cos(pi*(f_b-f_a)*t))`. Its peaks recur at the difference frequency, approximately 2.595600287 Hz. Forgetting the absolute value creates a factor-of-two interpretation error.

### Practice exercise 1: name the measured recurrence

For these frequencies, calculate the unsigned-envelope recurrence period and compare the signed modulator frequency with the unsigned peak recurrence frequency.

> **Answer:** `T=1/abs(f_b-f_a)` is approximately 0.385267333 seconds. The signed modulator frequency is approximately 1.297800144 Hz; unsigned peaks recur at approximately 2.595600287 Hz. The sign changes reverse carrier phase without making a negative amplitude magnitude.

## 2. A falling envelope can recover without new energy

The unsigned envelope starts at 0.25, first reaches zero near 0.192633666 seconds and returns to its peak near 0.385267333 seconds. Each component's amplitude remains 0.125. Cancellation and reinforcement change the sum without introducing exponential component damping.

![Untapered constructed cosines and their unsigned sum envelope](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/spareilleux/learn/0e1080569fb174d7867eebc9248f4fc9193aa5a8/code/streeling/evidence/intonation-listening-2026-10-22/two-tone-envelope.png)

The upper plot shows the first 0.025 seconds of the untapered model. The lower plot shows its unsigned envelope; dotted lines mark successive recurrence periods. This figure is not a waveform plot of the exported, boundary-faded WAV. A zero of the mathematical envelope is also not a promise of a zero RMS over a finite time window.

### Practice exercise 2: reject an unsupported damping conclusion

An agent fits a decay line only from the initial peak to the first envelope minimum. Why does that interval not establish component damping in this example?

> **Answer:** Both components have constant amplitude, yet their sum cancels near the first minimum and subsequently recovers. The selected interval omits that recovery. The model therefore supplies a counterexample to identifying every falling envelope with component energy loss; it does not diagnose the cause of a particular physical guitar recording.

## 3. State the window and the control

The [measurement record](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/main/code/streeling/evidence/intonation-listening-2026-10-22/beats.json) uses nominal 20 ms RMS windows centered at `T`, `1.5*T` and `2*T`. For each window, the start and exclusive end indices are the ceilings of the corresponding times multiplied by 44100. This explicit half-open convention avoids silently changing the sampled interval.

Relative to the first window, the destructive window falls by 26.357732143 dB; the recovered window differs by +0.000373071 dB. Before measuring, the plan required a dip of at least 10 dB and recovery within 1 dB. Both controls pass. Replacing the second frequency with the first and retaining the same windows gives a change of only +0.000087429 dB at the former dip. Finite carrier sampling explains why the control's short-window ratio is not exactly zero dB.

### Practice exercise 3: interpret the comparison

The destructive window is -26.357732143 dB relative to the first, and the later window is +0.000373071 dB. Which predeclared criteria pass? What does the equal-frequency control add?

> **Answer:** The dip exceeds 10 dB and the recovered window is within 1 dB of the first, so both criteria pass. The equal-frequency control lacks the large dip at those same windows, supporting frequency interference as the mechanism of this constructed comparison. It does not establish the mechanism of an unmeasured physical recording.

## 4. Keep mathematical signals and listening assets distinct

The packet includes an original [four-second mixture illustration](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/main/code/streeling/evidence/intonation-listening-2026-10-22/two-tone-illustration.wav). It keeps the component amplitudes and applies boundary fades over 441 samples, or 10 ms, at each end. Each fade includes its endpoints. The measured windows lie outside those fades. The WAV is mono PCM16, whereas the plotted equations and recorded RMS windows use floating-point untapered signals.

The separate [tone A](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/main/code/streeling/evidence/intonation-listening-2026-10-22/tone-a.wav) and [tone B](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/main/code/streeling/evidence/intonation-listening-2026-10-22/tone-b.wav) each last one second. Their faded float RMS is matched to 0.05 before quantization. These single-tone files support a proposed sequential discrimination task; the simultaneous mixture illustrates interference. Neither file set was played or listened to in this analysis.

The [asset record](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/main/code/streeling/evidence/intonation-listening-2026-10-22/audio-assets.json) specifies nearest-even rounding of `sample*32768`, signed PCM16 decoding by division by 32768, hashes and reconstruction errors. Maximum absolute quantization error is at most `0.5/32768`. Matching numerical RMS does not establish equal perceived loudness or actual playback level.

### Practice exercise 4: choose the appropriate asset

Which asset illustrates simultaneous beating, and which pair fits a sequential higher-pitch question? Does the declared RMS matching establish perceptual loudness equality?

> **Answer:** The four-second mixture illustrates simultaneous beating. The separate one-second tones fit the sequential question. They are different stimuli and tasks. Float RMS matching and PCM verification establish numerical preparation; perceptual loudness equality and playback calibration remain unmeasured.

## 5. Preserve the failed engine result

The earlier [guitar runtime packet](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/main/code/streeling/evidence/guitar-runtime-2026-10-22/README.md) measured a candidate improvement of 0.114166150 dB against a required 1 dB decay gate. That candidate failed. A new constructed counterexample cannot change the old samples, rule or verdict.

For a future physical or engine recording, declare the target component or band, analysis window and controls before comparing decay. Check whether level recovery, interacting partials or a different measurement window undermine the proposed mechanism. Those are possible follow-up checks, not a claim that interference caused the earlier failure. [COG-004](../../cognitive-science/cog-004-score-a-pitch-discrimination-task/) separately examines what a listening score could establish.

### Practice exercise 5: bound the agent's revision

An agent sees the new simulation and changes the old candidate from failed to passed. What should its corrected report say?

> **Answer:** The old candidate remains failed: 0.114166150 dB is below its required 1 dB. The new constant-amplitude model demonstrates that a falling short-window envelope can arise from interference. It motivates controlled follow-up on actual recordings; it neither reverses the old gate nor establishes a physical cause or perceptual benefit.
