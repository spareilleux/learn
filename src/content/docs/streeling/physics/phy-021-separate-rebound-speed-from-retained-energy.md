## Project question

A dropped craft bead in a proposed scene rebounds at a fraction of its approach speed. Calling that fraction retained energy confuses a linear velocity ratio with a quadratic energy ratio. The mass-scaling prerequisite supplies units; this lesson adds a signed impact model and declares which system receives the external impulse.

## Derive the operation before proposing automation

Choose positive velocity away from a fixed wall. Reverse the incoming sign and multiply speed by restitution. Kinetic energy uses the square of velocity, so direction disappears from energy while remaining essential to impulse. Momentum of the bead alone is not conserved: the wall acts on it. [OpenStax](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/9-4-types-of-collisions) discusses the closed-system boundary; our ideal wall deliberately lies outside the bead subsystem. Atlas source defines geometry, without any measured rebound or contact solver.

### Exercise 1

Compute the outgoing velocity.

<details><summary>Answer 1</summary>

It is `2 m/s`, away from the wall. The sign reversal is part of the declared impact rule.

</details>

### Exercise 2

Compute both kinetic energies.

<details><summary>Answer 2</summary>

They are `4 J` before and `1 J` after. The retained fraction is `0.25`, not `0.5`.

</details>

### Exercise 3

Calculate the impulse on the piece.

<details><summary>Answer 3</summary>

It is `3 N*s`. Subtracting a negative incoming momentum increases the signed change.

</details>

### Exercise 4

Apply the elastic control.

<details><summary>Answer 4</summary>

Outgoing velocity is `4 m/s`, retained fraction is `1`, and impulse is `4 N*s`. Equal kinetic energy does not mean unchanged momentum.

</details>
