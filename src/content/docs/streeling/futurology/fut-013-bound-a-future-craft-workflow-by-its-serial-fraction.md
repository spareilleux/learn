## Project question

A proposal gives a craft generator more workers and predicts proportional completion gains. Some work may remain serial: an ordered export step or an indivisible inspection is not shortened merely by adding generators. The queue and break-even prerequisites model different questions. This lesson bounds ideal fixed-work acceleration before forecasting the benefit of future automation.

## Derive the operation before proposing automation

Split the single-worker elapsed baseline by execution time, not source-line count. Keep the serial portion unchanged and divide only the parallel portion by the worker count. [LLNL](https://hpc.llnl.gov/documentation/tutorials/introduction-parallel-computing-tutorial) states this Amdahl model and distinguishes fixed-size strong scaling from a growing workload. The cleanup script performs ordered stages but supplies no timing decomposition for this example. Our perfect-division premise is optimistic; coordination, contention and changed acceptance work can make a real workflow slower than this ideal, or invalidate the partition entirely.

### Exercise 1

Decompose the main baseline.

<details><summary>Answer 1</summary>

Serial work is `7` minutes and divided parallel work is `5.25` minutes. The ideal total is `12.25` minutes.

</details>

### Exercise 2

Compute finite-worker speedup.

<details><summary>Answer 2</summary>

It is `2.2857142857142856`, not the worker count. Serial work remains on the completion path.

</details>

### Exercise 3

Find the limiting ceiling.

<details><summary>Answer 3</summary>

The asymptotic speedup is `4`. This is an upper limit in the declared model, not a measured future capability.

</details>

### Exercise 4

Apply the larger serial fraction.

<details><summary>Answer 4</summary>

The components become `16.8` and `2.8` minutes, total `19.6`, speedup `1.4285714285714286`, and ceiling `1.6666666666666667`.

</details>

### Exercise 5

Could this replace the existing queue or cost models?

<details><summary>Answer 5</summary>

No. Fixed-work elapsed speedup does not determine arrivals, review acceptance, setup cost or correction effort. A real comparison must preserve task scope and measure the relevant time components.

</details>
