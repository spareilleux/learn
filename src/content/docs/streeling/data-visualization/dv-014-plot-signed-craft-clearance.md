## Project question

A craft dashboard reports average clearance magnitude and makes an overlapping strand look as healthy as a separated one. The missing sign changes the scene decision. This lesson designs a diverging position view with a visible contact line, while preserving the underlying authored rows. It extends the shared-scale prerequisite by treating zero as a physical interpretation boundary rather than merely an axis origin.

## Read the retained boundary

The retained crossing fixture stores millimetre positions and a role-based over flag. It does not contain measured clearances. The new signed values are a separate teaching construction; the fixture motivates keeping units and crossing identity attached to a proposed scene annotation.

## Interpret the model

Define a clearance direction before plotting. Negative values mean overlap, zero means contact and positive values mean separation in our stipulated section. Put each identified crossing on a common signed axis and label the contact line. Magnitude can help describe distance from contact but cannot decide on which side a row lies. Averages also cannot certify every crossing: a large positive gap can hide a negative one. Keep the minimum and category counts beside any summary. This deterministic table contains no measurement uncertainty; adding an error bar would require a separate error model.

The direct baseline is a labelled sketch and a hand calculation with the answer panels closed. Preserve intermediate values and units, then apply the changed input in the negative control. A disagreement is useful evidence: locate whether it comes from the definition, arithmetic, representation or intended task before changing the model. Agreement on the main case alone does not establish a universal rule. These finite examples deliberately expose an assumption that a smooth preview could conceal.

```text
{"clearance_mm":[-0.4,0,0.2,0.8]}
minimum=min(clearance); overlap_count=count(c<0); contact_count=count(c==0); mean_absolute=sum(abs(c))/len(c)
```

## Corrected exercises

### Exercise 1

Classify every main-case row relative to contact.

<details><summary>Answer 1</summary>

There is `1` overlapping row, `1` contact row and `2` separated rows. These categories follow the declared sign convention, not a color preference.

### Exercise 2

Compute the minimum and mean absolute clearance.

<details><summary>Answer 2</summary>

The minimum is `-0.4 mm` and mean absolute clearance is `0.35 mm`. The second number alone cannot identify an overlap.

</details>

### Exercise 3

Apply the sign-changed control. What statistic conceals the improvement?

<details><summary>Answer 3</summary>

The minimum becomes `0 mm`, overlap count becomes `0`, and mean absolute clearance remains `0.35 mm`. Absolute aggregation conceals the change of side.

</details>

### Exercise 4

Specify a view that preserves the scene question without relying on color.

<details><summary>Answer 4</summary>

Use identified rows at signed positions, a labelled contact line, exact values and text states. Color may reinforce categories but must not carry the only sign information.

</details>

### Exercise 5

Does a nonnegative minimum prove a moving weave is collision free?

<details><summary>Answer 5</summary>

No. The rows concern a fixed stipulated section. Full geometry, motion, tolerances and unobserved crossings require their own evidence.

</details>
