A Demerzel dashboard reports one combined amount of unresolved work. The same total could hide different proportions of drafting and review work, which would suggest different interventions. Repeating the same aggregate is not automatically a second independent view. Ask whether known state dynamics make the later observation distinguish the hidden components before designing an estimator or blaming a stalled agent.

The earlier guitar lesson makes a mode invisible at one observer position. Here the new issue is temporal: a sensor sees a sum now and another sum after a known update. Write the current observation as the sensor row applied to the initial state. Substitute the transition into the later observation. Stacking those rows creates a small linear system whose determinant tells us whether this exact reconstruction is unique.

In the fixed model the hidden components evolve at different rates. The later total therefore adds a genuinely different equation, and subtraction recovers the second component before the first. In the control both components evolve identically. Any initial pair with the same sum produces the same observed pair. More repeated identical totals cannot recover the missing distinction under that model.

The Harvard notes define the observability matrix; our finite discrete equations show the calculation directly. The two hidden components are pedagogical state coordinates, not calibrated counts of real drafting and review queues. Actual queues have inputs, completions and noise. A future agentic application must identify those effects and test the assumed transition. An exact determinant in an invented case cannot establish live observability or justify acting on reconstructed values without receipts.

### Exercise 1

Form the stacked observation matrix.

<details><summary>Answer 1</summary>

The rows are `[1,1]` and `[1,2]`, giving determinant `1` and rank `2`. Two independent equations can identify both unknown coordinates under the stated assumptions.

</details>

### Exercise 2

Reconstruct the state from the outputs.

<details><summary>Answer 2</summary>

The observations are `[7,11]`. Subtracting gives the second component `4`; the first is `3`. This is exact reconstruction in the model, not a measured queue estimate.

</details>

### Exercise 3

Apply the equal-dynamics control.

<details><summary>Answer 3</summary>

Its determinant is `0`, rank is `1` and outputs are `[7,7]`. Reconstruction is `null` because a unique initial pair cannot be selected.

</details>

### Exercise 4

Would an unknown arrival preserve this calculation?

<details><summary>Answer 4</summary>

No. It adds an unmodeled contribution to the later output. Record that input or redesign the observation contract before interpreting the reconstructed components.

</details>

### Exercise 5

What additional evidence does a real dashboard need?

<details><summary>Answer 5</summary>

A justified transition, known inputs, observation times, source-bound receipts and noise limits. Observability of an authored matrix does not establish the identity or health of a live worker.

</details>
