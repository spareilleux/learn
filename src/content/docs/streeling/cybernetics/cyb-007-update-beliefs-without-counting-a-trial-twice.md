## The project question

Two agents summarize the same benchmark. Has a controller received two independent observations? The [GA agentic-engineering guide](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/76efbffd5af82b46c3146957a6bd4504008eeb89/docs/methodology/agentic-engineering.md) recommends scoped work and efficient review. That motivates preserving evidence through handoffs; its recommendations do not supply a probabilistic independence model.

Use [FUT-005](../../futurology/fut-005-value-a-renderer-pilot-before-running-it/)'s authored stable F/U condition, prior `1/2`, and positive likelihoods `4/5` in F and `1/5` in U. The action rule chooses S only above posterior `3/5`, otherwise K. We will distinguish a fresh trial from a new wrapper around an old trial.

## 1. Update with a likelihood ratio

Odds are `p/(1-p)`. The prior odds are 1. A positive outcome has likelihood ratio `(4/5)/(1/5)=4`; a negative outcome has ratio `1/4`. Multiplying prior odds by the appropriate ratio gives posterior odds. Converting back uses `p=odds/(1+odds)`, consistent with the [NIST Bayes formula](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/apr/section2/apr1a.htm).

After one positive result, odds are 4 and posterior is `4/5`. After a fresh negative result, odds return to 1 and posterior `1/2`. This multiplication assumes the fresh trial outcomes are independent conditional on the same latent state. Independence is a property of the stated observation model, not of the fact that two messages arrived.

### Practice exercise 1: trace fresh observations

Starting from prior `1/2`, calculate posterior odds and probabilities after a positive outcome, then an independent negative outcome. Which actions follow under the declared threshold?

> **Answer:** Odds become 4 and posterior `4/5`, selecting S. The independent negative multiplies odds by `1/4`, returning them to 1 and posterior `1/2`, selecting K. The results require the declared conditional-independence and stable-state assumptions.

## 2. Recognize a copy of one event

If the second message is a deterministic copy of the first trial's positive outcome, it is positive with probability 1 given that observed event under either latent state. Its additional conditional likelihood ratio is therefore 1. It does not multiply the odds by 4 again.

By contrast, two stipulated fresh independent positive trials give odds 16 and posterior `16/17`. The distinction concerns the underlying observation, not how confidently a summary is worded. A new wrapper ID, timestamp or different prose does not create a new experimental trial.

### Practice exercise 2: compare copy and repetition

After one positive trial, compare a copied positive report with a genuinely fresh independent positive trial. What posteriors result? Does assigning the copied report a new message ID settle the issue?

> **Answer:** The copy leaves posterior `4/5`; the fresh independent positive gives `16/17`. A new message ID identifies the wrapper, not a new conditionally independent observation. Preserve the underlying trial identity and the assumptions about how fresh trials were produced.

## 3. Watch the action change when evidence is duplicated

The [authored ledger](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/main/code/streeling/evidence/pilot-value-2026-10-22/belief-updates.json) contains three messages:

| Wrapper | Underlying trial | Outcome | Identity-preserving posterior | Naive message-counting posterior |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| wrap-A | trial-A | positive | `4/5` | `4/5` |
| wrap-A-again | trial-A | copied positive | `4/5` | `16/17` |
| wrap-B | trial-B | independent negative | `1/2` | `4/5` |

The correct trace after the new negative chooses K; the naive trace chooses S because it counted trial-A twice. This is a counterexample in an authored observer model, not a reproduced bug in a running project controller.

### Practice exercise 3: locate the spurious update

Which message should not add another factor of 4? After wrap-B, give both posteriors and actions. Why does message-counting produce a different decision?

> **Answer:** wrap-A-again repeats trial-A and adds no independent factor. After wrap-B, the identity-preserving posterior is `1/2`, choosing K, while the naive posterior is `4/5`, choosing S. Its extra positive likelihood factor came from a copy rather than a fresh trial.

## 4. Keep identity, independence and comparability distinct

Different genuine trials can produce identical summary bytes. Deduplicating only by a summary hash could wrongly erase fresh information. Conversely, two different summaries can describe the same raw trial. A useful handoff preserves trial provenance, collection time, environment, protocol and shared inputs, along with the artifact identity.

Even distinct trials need not be conditionally independent or comparable. They may share a disturbance, overlap in time, reuse one cached result, or observe a state that changed. The historical [BRUTAL M2 report](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/76efbffd5af82b46c3146957a6bd4504008eeb89/Experiments/Brutal/evidence/m2-20261019-235218.md) and [M3 follow-up](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/76efbffd5af82b46c3146957a6bd4504008eeb89/Experiments/Brutal/evidence/m3-20261020-000727.md) have distinct recorded runs and varying controls, with their own revision and dirty-state context. They were not generated by this binary likelihood model.

### Practice exercise 4: audit two identities

Two reports have different hashes but both cite the same trial. Two other reports have equal summary bytes but come from distinct fresh trials. Should hash equality alone determine belief updates? Does distinct trial identity prove conditional independence?

> **Answer:** No. Different hashes can wrap one event, while equal summaries can arise from distinct events. Preserve the underlying trial provenance and the dependence/comparability model. Distinct identity permits investigation of a fresh trial but does not itself prove conditional independence or a stable latent state.

## 5. Value another update before requesting it

After the first positive outcome, a second fresh trial has positive probability `17/25` and negative probability `8/25`. The second positive gives posterior `16/17` and optimal loss `10/17`; the negative returns posterior `1/2` and loss 4. Averaging gives expected loss `42/25=1.68`, compared with current loss 2. Its gross conditional value is `8/25=0.32` points.

After a first negative outcome, either second result still selects K, so the second trial's gross decision value is 0 there. Averaged before the first outcome, the second trial's marginal value is `4/25=0.16`. At cost 0.6, a second trial is not worthwhile in either branch of this authored model. This is a bounded stopping calculation; it does not install a production stopping rule or settle mandatory validation obligations.

### Practice exercise 5: decide whether to request another trial

After a first positive result, compare the second trial's gross value 0.32 with cost 0.6. What is its net value? What happens after a first negative result, and may this model cancel required technical validation?

> **Answer:** After a positive result, net value is `0.32-0.6=-0.28` points; after a negative it is `0-0.6=-0.6`. Do not request the optional second trial under these premises. Required validation is a separate admissibility obligation, and the likelihood, state persistence and cost model still require real justification before controlling a live process.
