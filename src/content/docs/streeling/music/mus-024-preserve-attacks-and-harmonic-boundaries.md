## Project question and deliverable

A chord planner receives a sequence of pitch collections. Which notes were newly attacked, which remained sounding, and where did the harmony change? Reconstruct an event ledger and show what a pitch-only handoff cannot recover. This applies MUS-004's duration vocabulary to a concrete GA representation boundary.

The [pinned GA tab solver](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/dc2e74cbd992a298f67efb928b5b3754615e1924/Common/GA.Business.ML/Tabs/AdvancedTabSolver.cs#L52) accepts a sequence of pitch collections. That method's arguments contain no onset, duration or tie field. Other GA models do carry such information: [ProgressionStep](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/dc2e74cbd992a298f67efb928b5b3754615e1924/Common/GA.Domain.Core/Theory/Harmony/Progressions/ProgressionStep.cs) has `DurationMs`, and its imported [MusicXML](https://www.w3.org/2021/06/musicxml40/musicxml-reference/elements/duration/) [note model](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/dc2e74cbd992a298f67efb928b5b3754615e1924/Common/GA.Business.DSL/Types/MusicXmlTypes.fs#L93) includes duration, voice and tie fields. This lesson inspects those types; it does not execute an integration or establish that all GA paths lose timing.

## 1. Keep note events distinct from chord labels

The [authored fixture](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/codex/streeling-craft-transfer-20261022/code/streeling/evidence/music-phrase-2026-10-22/timed-events.json) uses absolute quarter-note beats and C4 = 60. An event is active from its onset inclusive to its offset exclusive. Its identifier keeps one note's continuity across later snapshots.

| Event | Pitch | Onset | Offset |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| G bass | 43 | 0 | 2 |
| Shared B | 59 | 0 | 4 |
| D inner | 62 | 0 | 2 |
| F inner | 65 | 0 | 2 |
| E anticipation | 76 | 1.5 | 4 |
| C bass | 48 | 2 | 4 |
| E inner | 64 | 2 | 4 |
| G inner | 67 | 2 | 4 |

The harmonic annotations are separately authored: G7 from 0 to 2, Cmaj7 from 2 to 4. Calling E5 at 1.5 an anticipation of the tonic third is the author's interpretation. The same instantaneous notes could invite another harmonic reading; the table alone does not prove the label.

### Practice exercise 1

List the sounding pitches over the three intervals beginning at 0, 1.5 and 2. Does every new note onset require a new authored chord label?

> **Answer:** The collections are `[43,59,62,65]`, `[43,59,62,65,76]` and `[48,59,64,67,76]`, containing 4, 5 and 5 notes. No. The onset at 1.5 changes the sounding collection while the authored G7 interval continues to 2. Harmonic segmentation is an additional interpretation.

## 2. Inspect the change at the boundary

At beat 2, pitches 43, 62 and 65 stop. Pitches 48, 64 and 67 start. The B3 event 59 and E5 event 76 remain sounding; they are not new attacks at that boundary. Thus the attack-only collection contains a C-major triad while the complete sounding collection contains the B of Cmaj7 as well as a doubled E.

Half-open intervals make the boundary explicit: the old events ending at 2 are absent at 2, and the new events starting at 2 are present. All events are absent at 4. This is the fixture's mathematical convention, not a measurement of a renderer's release behavior.

### Practice exercise 2

At beat 2, write three separate lists: released pitches, new attacks and held pitches. Why would an attack-only chord analyzer miss a sounding chord tone?

> **Answer:** Released: 43, 62, 65. Attacks: 48, 64, 67. Held: 59, 76. B3 = 59 is the held major seventh of the authored Cmaj7. An attack-only collection omits it even though it remains active. Neither the omission nor a chord name inferred from it was observed in a live GA run.

## 3. Do not turn every snapshot into a new attack

The fixture contains 8 note events. A deliberately wrong conversion that reattacks every pitch in every sounding snapshot would create 4 + 5 + 5 = 14 attacks. The shared B would be attacked 3 times rather than its single authored attack. This is a calculated counterexample to that conversion rule, not captured output from GA or a playback engine.

A snapshot answers “what is sounding now?” An attack ledger answers “what began now?” A harmonic annotation answers “which chord does the author hear over this interval?” Keep these questions distinct even when their boundaries coincide in a simple score.

### Practice exercise 3

How many extra attacks does the hypothetical snapshot-to-attack conversion add? Can pitch membership alone identify the original sustained B event?

> **Answer:** It adds 14 − 8 = 6 attacks. No. Repeated pitch 59 in adjacent snapshots can represent one held event or separate reattacks. Preserve event identity and boundaries to distinguish them; the pitch collections alone are insufficient.

## 4. Declare the time unit before converting

At 120 quarter-notes per minute, one quarter-note beat lasts 500 milliseconds. The onset at 1.5 is at 750 milliseconds; the harmonic boundary at 2 is at 1000 milliseconds; the phrase ends at 2000 milliseconds. Each of the two authored harmonic steps lasts 1000 milliseconds at this tempo.

GA's `DurationMs` is explicitly a millisecond duration, with a default of 1000. That default does not determine a tempo. In [MusicXML](https://www.w3.org/2021/06/musicxml40/musicxml-reference/elements/duration/), duration values use division units. They are not milliseconds by default. Mapping quarter-note beats to division units needs the [divisions-per-quarter scale](https://www.w3.org/2021/06/musicxml40/musicxml-reference/elements/divisions/); converting durations to milliseconds additionally needs tempo. Equal numeric values can be correct when the declared scales agree. Check that agreement instead of assuming it.

### Practice exercise 4

Convert the onset 1.5 and the duration 2 to milliseconds at the declared tempo. Would copying the number 2 directly into `DurationMs` preserve the intended harmonic duration?

> **Answer:** They become 750 and 1000 milliseconds. No: a value of 2 in `DurationMs` means 2 milliseconds, not 2 quarter-note beats. The fixture declares its time unit and tempo; no importer or exporter was run.

## 5. Preserve continuity across representation changes

MusicXML's [tie element](https://www.w3.org/2021/06/musicxml40/musicxml-reference/elements/tie/) describes a sounding tie; its separate tied element describes notation. A split notated duration need not create a fresh attack. A model that retains only pitches cannot by itself preserve that distinction. Keep a correspondence to the original events when sending selected pitch snapshots to a planner.

Submit event identifiers, onset/offset units, held/attack lists and the separate harmonic annotation. The [prepared learner packet](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/codex/streeling-craft-transfer-20261022/code/streeling/evidence/music-phrase-2026-10-22/learner-task.md) checks the reasoning without pretending that listening or an import/export round trip has occurred.

### Practice exercise 5

An agent retains the three pitch snapshots and promises exact articulation on export. Which evidence and data are missing?

> **Answer:** The original event identities, attack/release boundaries, unit/tempo context and tie/articulation information are missing from that pitch-only handoff. Exact export would also require an actual tested conversion. The source types and this authored event ledger do not establish such an implementation.
