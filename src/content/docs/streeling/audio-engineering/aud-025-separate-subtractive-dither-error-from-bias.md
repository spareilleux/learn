A dither proposal can confuse added noise with the residual error after subtracting the same offset. Keep the encoder and decoder equations together. The fixed offsets are a deliberately finite teaching ensemble, not a random generator or a sample of independent uniform noise. A zero mean for this ensemble does not prove independence from every input. For a future [GA](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga) export, losing the offset correspondence would implement a different converter.

### Exercise 1

Compute the subtractive decoded values.

> **Answer:** They are `[0.375,0.125,-0.125,-0.375]`, although every encoded integer is 0.

### Exercise 2

What are the finite mean and mean-square error?

> **Answer:** The mean is 0 and the mean-square error is 0.078125 for this fixed ensemble.

### Exercise 3

Does this establish a stochastic independence theorem?

> **Answer:** No. A deterministic ensemble at one input cannot establish a distribution for every input.

### Exercise 4

Why is mean alone insufficient in the control?

> **Answer:** Its mean remains 0 while mean-square error becomes 0.1875 and individual errors change.

### Exercise 5

What falsifies the stipulated subtractive converter contract?

> **Answer:** A decoder offset not matching the encoder offset rejects the stipulated converter contract.
