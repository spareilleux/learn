## The project question

A practice agent asks for “1, 3, 5” or “do, re, mi.” Those labels do not identify absolute pitches until their conventions and musical reference are known. [WML-005](../../world-music-languages/wml-005-declare-note-name-conventions/) distinguishes pitch-name conventions; [MCL-005](../../musicology/mcl-005-preserve-written-and-sounding-pitch/) retains register and transposition. Here the missing reference is a declared tonic and scale form.

The author-published [Open Music Theory scale-degree text](https://openmusictheory.github.io/scales.html) defines degrees relative to a scale and describes movable-do usage. We read that legacy text, not the newer page that returned an access error. Our examples use a bounded major/natural-minor vocabulary in twelve-tone equal temperament; they do not classify all musical traditions or infer a convention from a player's nationality.

## 1. A degree is an index, not a semitone count

For this exercise, ascending degrees 1 through 7 use major offsets `[0,2,4,5,7,9,11]` or natural-minor offsets `[0,2,3,5,7,8,10]`. Add the selected offset to an explicit tonic note number. D4 is 62, so its major degrees 1, 3 and 5 are `[62,66,69]`: D4, F#4 and A4.

Using degree minus one as a semitone offset would instead turn degree 3 into `62+2=64`, E4. A numbered position in a declared scale is not a chromatic interval measured in semitones.

### Practice exercise 1: resolve the major triad

Calculate degrees 1, 3 and 5 above tonic D4 in the stipulated major form. Diagnose a resolver that produces 64 for degree 3.

> **Answer:** The offsets are 0, 4 and 7, giving `[62,66,69]`. Degree 3 should be F#4 at 66. Returning 64 treats the degree index minus one as a semitone offset and ignores the major-scale table.

## 2. Keep scale form and tonic register explicit

Under D natural minor, the same degrees become `[62,65,69]`, with F4 instead of F#4. Changing the scale form alters the third without changing the tonic. The generic word “minor” does not specify every possible raised or lowered degree; this table deliberately selects natural minor.

Tonic pitch class 2 also does not choose D4 rather than D3. A resolver that needs absolute notes must receive register or an explicit rule for choosing it. A pitch-class-only destination can retain that limitation instead of inventing an octave.

### Practice exercise 2: change one declared input

Compare the major and natural-minor triads with tonic D4. If only tonic class 2 is supplied, is either absolute triad uniquely determined?

> **Answer:** Major gives `[62,66,69]`; natural minor gives `[62,65,69]`. Their third differs by one semitone. A tonic class alone does not supply D4 or another register, and the scale form must also be declared. Neither absolute triad follows uniquely from class 2 alone.

## 3. A key-signature count does not provide every reference

[MusicXML fifths](https://www.w3.org/2021/06/musicxml40/musicxml-reference/elements/fifths/) counts sharps or flats in a traditional key signature. The separate [mode field](https://www.w3.org/2021/06/musicxml40/musicxml-reference/elements/mode/) distinguishes major, minor and other named modes.

With two sharps, D major and B minor already give different tonic classes, 2 and 11, within a restricted major/minor comparison. An absent mode cannot be silently replaced by major. Even a selected key and mode do not provide the tonic's register or prescribe every chromatic note in a melody.

### Practice exercise 3: preserve unresolved key context

A stored key has fifths 2 and no mode. An agent assumes D4 major and resolves every degree. Identify the guessed inputs and give a bounded alternative report.

> **Answer:** The agent guessed major rather than a different compatible context, and guessed register 4. Even within major/minor possibilities, B minor has tonic class 11 rather than 2. Retain the missing mode and register as unresolved; obtain the intended tonic/scale form and register policy before returning absolute notes. A signature count alone is insufficient.

## 4. Interpret a syllable under its declared vocabulary

Stipulate a relative-major syllable vocabulary where do, re and mi are degrees 1, 2 and 3 above tonic D4. It yields `[62,64,66]`. Separately stipulate a fixed natural pitch-name vocabulary at register 4 where those same syllables name C4, D4 and E4, yielding `[60,62,64]`.

These are explicitly declared controls, not a universal syllable dictionary or an assignment of systems to countries. An unqualified “do” supplies neither which vocabulary is intended nor an absolute register. This packet defines degrees only from 1 through 7; degree 8 is outside that chosen vocabulary unless an extension is specified.

### Practice exercise 4: distinguish the two requests

Resolve do, re and mi under both declared controls. What should happen to an unspecified do or degree 8? Does rejecting degree 8 here mean no musical system can use it?

> **Answer:** The relative-major control yields `[62,64,66]`; the fixed natural-name control yields `[60,62,64]`. Unspecified do needs its convention and register; degree 8 is outside this declared seven-degree interface. That boundary is a scoped design choice, not a universal prohibition on extended degree notation.

## 5. Read what GA's selected interfaces retain

The pinned [MusicXML key parser](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/76efbffd5af82b46c3146957a6bd4504008eeb89/Common/GA.Business.DSL/Parsers/MusicXmlParser.fs#L140-L153) stores fifths and an optional mode string. Its selected code does not resolve this degree vocabulary. The separate [pitch parser](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/76efbffd5af82b46c3146957a6bd4504008eeb89/Common/GA.Domain.Core/Primitives/Notes/PitchParser.cs#L5-L8) expects an absolute letter, optional single sharp/flat and octave. At this revision its patterns are anchored; that fact must not be projected onto a different revision used by an earlier lesson.

### Practice exercise 5: prepare a source-assisted label handoff

Read those interfaces and the [fixed degree record](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/main/code/streeling/evidence/capo-spelling-2026-10-22/degree-context.json). Specify what a proposed agent should retain before translating a scale degree or syllable into an absolute pitch.

> **Answer:** Retain the original token, declared vocabulary, tonic identity/register, exact scale form, direction/range policy, tuning convention, intended spelling and source revision. Preserve unresolved or unsupported inputs rather than assigning a plausible note. The selected absolute-pitch and key-storage interfaces are not evidence of an implemented syllable resolver.
