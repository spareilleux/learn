## The project question

A translated exercise preserves the number 6 but changes what the number counts. A matching numeral is therefore an inadequate localization check. We define an invented source request with an explicit unit and compare two candidate target instructions by duration and group starts. The task checks semantic units, without assigning vocabulary or rhythmic practice to a culture.

## 1. Exercise 1

The source requests six eighth-note units at quarter-note rate 120. Each counted unit is .5 quarters, so the total is 3 quarters and 1.5 seconds. Keep the count and its unit together. A translator may rephrase the instruction, but the operational quantity must remain recoverable.

Calculate the source duration in quarters and seconds.

> **Answer.** It is 3 quarters, equal to 1.5 seconds under the named tempo unit.

## 2. Exercise 2

Candidate A preserves six eighth-note units. Candidate B asks for six quarter-note units. B keeps the numeral but totals 6 quarters and 3 seconds. That negative control exposes a semantic unit substitution. Translation review should show this discrepancy directly rather than rely on a matching token count or familiar metrical word.

What duration does candidate B request?

> **Answer.** It requests 6 quarters, or 3 seconds, twice the original duration.

## 3. Exercise 3

Add source groups `[3,3]` in eighth units, with starts `[0,3]`. Preserving total duration does not authorize regrouping to `[2,2,2]`, whose starts are `[0,2,4]`. This extends the unit check with ordered boundaries. Neither set describes performed loudness; these are authored grouping markers only.

Give the two start lists and identify what equal totals fail to prove.

> **Answer.** They are `[0,3]` and `[0,2,4]`; equal totals do not prove identical grouping.

## 4. Exercise 4

A local term may be retained with a gloss instead of replaced by an assumed universal equivalent. The proposed receipt records original term, declared counted unit, group order, tempo unit and reviewer reasoning. Missing convention remains unresolved. Interface language or a person's location does not determine the intended musical counting system.

What should happen when the counted unit is unspecified?

> **Answer.** Preserve the original term and ask for its operational unit; do not select a plausible duration silently.

## 5. Exercise 5

Read the retained meter lesson's named-unit examples and the origin lesson's anchored starts before preparing the localized instruction. Our additional operation is a translation receipt that recomputes the counted quantity. It is not a dictionary or evidence that a fluent reviewer approved the wording. Keep actual reviewer decisions open.

What is the manual acceptance baseline?

> **Answer.** Match duration, counted units and ordered anchored groups; fluent-language and musician judgments remain separate pending observations.
