A GA phrase handoff preserves the two endpoints of a slide and assumes that the path between them is determined. It is not: the interpolated coordinate matters. We compare uniform change of speaking length with uniform change of logarithmic frequency. The endpoints and duration agree while the intermediate pitch differs. The operation extends the prerequisite’s articulation ledger into a continuous trajectory, without adding a production slide engine.

For a flexible ideal string with constant wave speed, frequency is inversely proportional to speaking length. A straight line in length therefore becomes a curved path in frequency. Uniform logarithmic pitch instead makes frequency a geometric interpolation. Neither route should be called the default behavior of every slide, and an ordinary fretted guitar can introduce contact transitions excluded by this continuous model.

The primary notation source identifies a slide as continuous and includes playback discretization attributes. That does not select our physical coordinate or certify that a parser retains it. The interior sample is the useful witness: identical endpoints cannot distinguish the policies. An agent that stores only the endpoints would need an additional agreed path convention to recover the intended result. Keep units, coordinate and contact assumption in a proposed record. A direct table already tests whether a later abstraction preserves the path. Actual finger movement, fret contact, pitch tracking and musician judgment require separate observations; the ideal calculation supplies none of them.

## Assumptions and evidence limits

Assume constant wave speed and a continuously variable ideal speaking length. Fret interruptions are absent. Both policies are authored trajectories; neither constitutes a normal fretted-slide prediction or a measured motion.

### Exercise 1

Derive the length at the interior time for linear length motion.

<details><summary>Answer 1</summary>

It is the arithmetic interpolation of the endpoint lengths, as listed in the fixed case.

</details>

### Exercise 2

Convert that length to frequency under constant wave speed.

<details><summary>Answer 2</summary>

Use the inverse-length relation; an arithmetic midpoint in length is not an arithmetic midpoint in frequency.

</details>

### Exercise 3

How does the control interpolate pitch?

<details><summary>Answer 3</summary>

It interpolates logarithmic frequency, giving a geometric frequency midpoint and a different speaking length.

</details>

### Exercise 4

Can shared endpoints establish shared slide behavior?

<details><summary>Answer 4</summary>

No. The interior values differ under the two declared policies despite identical endpoints and duration.

</details>

### Exercise 5

What falsifies an endpoint-only fidelity claim?

<details><summary>Answer 5</summary>

A receiver unable to distinguish these two interior trajectories cannot recover which authored slide path was requested.

</details>
