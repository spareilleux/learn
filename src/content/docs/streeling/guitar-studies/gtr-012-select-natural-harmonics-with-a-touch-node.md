A fretboard marker can tell a guitarist where to press, but a natural harmonic asks for another action: lightly touch a node while preserving the full vibrating length. Confusing these actions changes the predicted pitch and the teaching instruction. We use an ideal modal table to explain the difference. The lesson adds a contact constraint to the prerequisite's pluck-position model; the same coordinate does not mean the same physical operation.

## Read the retained evidence

The UNSW strings discussion identifies nodes and mode frequencies and explains natural harmonics through light touching. Read those passages alongside the retained Rust voice and excitation-related state. A stored frequency is not a touch-contact model, and an engine's output partials require actual measurement. Our table is an authored constraint calculation, with no guitar played and no harmonic rendered.

### Exercise 1

Choose fundamental `110 Hz` and candidate modes `[1,2,3,4,5,6]`. Ideal mode shape is `sin(n*pi*x/L)`. At touch fraction `1/3`, a mode can survive the zero-displacement constraint when its mode number is divisible by `3`. The contact does not shorten the string in this ideal rule; it suppresses incompatible motion.

Which candidate modes have a node at the touch?

<details><summary>Answer 1</summary>

Modes `[3,6]` survive the ideal node rule. Other listed modes have nonzero displacement at `1/3` and are suppressed under the stipulated constraint.

</details>

### Exercise 2

Ideal surviving mode frequencies are mode number multiplied by the original fundamental. Thus the listed surviving components are `[330,660] Hz`, with lowest surviving frequency `330 Hz`. A spectrum may contain several permitted components, and the rule alone gives none of their amplitudes. Do not turn the lowest permitted frequency into a measured salience or loudness claim.

What are the surviving frequencies and the lowest one?

<details><summary>Answer 2</summary>

They are `[330,660] Hz`, with lowest `330 Hz`. Their relative amplitudes and the listener's perceived pitch were not specified or observed.

</details>

### Exercise 3

A firm fret at the same coordinate would impose a new vibrating endpoint, and its pitch depends on which side remains between fret and bridge. Our coordinate is measured from one declared endpoint; it is not a numbered fret label. A learner must state origin and contact action before translating this geometry into a guitar diagram or an agent instruction.

Why cannot the touch fraction alone specify a fretted note?

<details><summary>Answer 3</summary>

Touch and fretting impose different constraints. A fretted-note prediction additionally needs the retained vibrating side and length, while the harmonic table retains the original `110 Hz` fundamental as its reference.

</details>

### Exercise 4

The negative control moves the touch to `1/2`. Surviving modes become `[2,4,6]`, with frequencies `[220,440,660] Hz`; the lowest is `220 Hz`. The sixth mode survives both positions, but the lowest component changes. Reporting only a shared high partial would miss a consequential change in the instruction.

What changes, and what remains shared?

<details><summary>Answer 4</summary>

The lowest changes from `330 Hz` to `220 Hz`. Mode `6`, at `660 Hz`, is shared. A common partial does not establish equivalent touch instructions or resulting spectra.

</details>

### Exercise 5

A real finger has finite width and pressure, and a real string has damping and stiffness. The ideal rule predicts permitted modes, not a guarantee that a player excites them cleanly. A future practice task should retain position, pressure description, pluck action and a raw recording, then check whether the predicted family appears without confusing another partial for the fundamental.

What would falsify applying this ideal rule to a particular take?

<details><summary>Answer 5</summary>

A controlled take with known touch position whose modal pattern conflicts with the node prediction would reject the simple account for that take. Missing contact or unclear geometry leaves the premise unverified instead.

</details>
