An agent may want to combine two selective capo descriptions without depending on message order. For each string, the highest active fret is the effective open boundary in the ideal model. Componentwise maximum supplies a commutative, associative and idempotent join. Those laws do not make removal invertible. Once the aggregate discards a lower stop, subtracting a higher stop cannot reconstruct what was underneath. Keep the active-device records when removal matters. The abstraction concerns ideal boundary maps and does not certify that two physical capo devices fit together.

## Specific assumptions and limits

Maps have equal length and nonnegative integer physical fret stops. Zero retains the nut boundary. The effective stop is the highest active stop on each string. Preserve individual devices and their multiplicity for later removal; no physical clearance is certified.

### Exercise 1

Calculate the main result and name its convention.

> **Answer:** The main join is `[2,3,4]`; adding c leaves `[2,3,4]`.

### Exercise 2

Which quantity must remain separate?

> **Answer:** Effective stop and active-device provenance differ; a maximum discards lower contributions.

### Exercise 3

What assumption prevents a stronger conclusion?

> **Answer:** Scalar maximum obeys all three laws, so each coordinate does too; componentwise equality proves the vector laws.

### Exercise 4

Explain the negative control.

> **Answer:** The control has the same aggregate for distinct a maps; removal of b cannot identify the hidden a from that aggregate.

### Exercise 5

What observation would reject the proposed claim?

> **Answer:** Two distinct underlying states with equal aggregate refute a unique inverse for removal.
