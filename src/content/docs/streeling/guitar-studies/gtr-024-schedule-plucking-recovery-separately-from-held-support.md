A long ringing note occupies an acoustic support interval but need not occupy the plucking finger throughout that interval. A scheduling proposal that uses note duration as finger busy time can reject an attainable pattern. Conversely, short notes do not guarantee enough recovery time for repeated attacks by the same finger. Keep a resource table of attack times and stipulated recovery durations, separate from held fretting support. This is a finite scheduling policy for [GA](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga) practice explanations, not a measured physiological limit or a recommended maximum playing speed.

## Specific assumptions and limits

Attack times are sorted in a common unit. Initially every finger is ready; an attack exactly at its deadline is allowed. Recovery is stipulated for this scheduling case and supplies no physiological or maximum-speed measurement.

### Exercise 1

Calculate the main result and name its convention.

> **Answer:** All attacks are ready in the main case; the final same-finger attack meets its deadline exactly.

### Exercise 2

Which quantity must remain separate?

> **Answer:** Held support `[0,8]` describes continued note support, not plucking-finger occupancy.

### Exercise 3

What assumption prevents a stronger conclusion?

> **Answer:** Recovery durations are stipulated scheduling inputs; they do not establish a player’s physical capability.

### Exercise 4

Explain the negative control.

> **Answer:** The final control attack is at 1.5 while the finger is ready at 2; index 2 violates readiness.

### Exercise 5

What observation would reject the proposed claim?

> **Answer:** A same-finger attack before its retained ready time rejects feasibility under the declared policy.
