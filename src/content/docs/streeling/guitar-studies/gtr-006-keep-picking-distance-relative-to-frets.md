## The project question

A guitarist keeps the picking hand the same distance from the bridge while moving up the neck. Has the pick remained at the same fraction of the sounding string? This matters when specifying a practice instruction or a future GA synthesis control. A millimetre distance, a fraction of vibrating length and a delay-buffer index carry different meanings.

We use an authored 650 mm open scale, ideal equal-tempered frets, unchanged tension and linear density, and no stiffness, compensation or action-induced stretching. These are model assumptions rather than measurements of a particular instrument. [GAS-006](../../guitar-acoustics/gas-006-relate-pluck-position-to-string-modes/) defines the ideal triangular pluck used for the modal predictions below.

## 1. Shorten the vibrating length

For fret k, the ideal vibrating length is `L_k=L_0*2^(-k/12)`. Frequency rises by the inverse length ratio under the declared fixed-wave-speed assumption. This follows the fixed-end string's length/frequency relation in the [Princeton wave-motion lecture](https://kirkmcd.princeton.edu/examples/Ph205/ph205l21.pdf#page=5), combined with the declared equal-tempered semitone ratio. It does not model a whole physical guitar.

| Fret | Vibrating length, mm | Frequency ratio to open |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 650 | 1 |
| 12 | 325 | 2 |
| 24 | 162.5 | 4 |

### Practice exercise 1: compute the sounding length

For the authored scale, calculate the vibrating length at fret 12 and its frequency ratio to the open string. Does the factor establish a measured tuning result on an instrument?

> **Answer:** The length is `650/2=325` mm and the frequency ratio is 2 under this model. No instrument was measured, so compensation, stretching and actual intonation remain outside the result.

## 2. Keep distance and fraction distinct

Declare bridge-relative pick distance d in millimetres and normalized position `p=d/L_k`, with `0<d<L_k`. With a fixed hand distance of 65 mm, p changes as the vibrating length changes:

| Fret | Fixed distance, mm | Fraction p | Ideal modal nulls |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 65 | `1/10` | multiples of 10 |
| 12 | 65 | `1/5` | multiples of 5 |
| 24 | 65 | `2/5` | multiples of 5 |

The null families come from the ideal triangular displacement and its `sin(n*pi*p)` factor. They are not measured spectral holes or instructions to touch a natural-harmonic node. A normal pluck/release and lightly touching a string during harmonic production are different initial/boundary operations.

### Practice exercise 2: follow a stationary hand

The hand stays 65 mm from the bridge while the left hand moves from fret 0 to fret 12. Calculate both fractions. Which ideal fifth mode changes from nonzero to zero?

> **Answer:** The fractions are `65/650=1/10` and `65/325=1/5`. Mode 5 is nonzero for the first ideal triangular pluck and zero for the second, because `5/10` is not an integer while `5/5` is. This is a model prediction, not an observed player spectrum.

## 3. Move the hand to preserve the fraction

To keep `p=1/10`, solve `d=p*L_k`. The required authored distances are 65 mm open, 32.5 mm at fret 12 and 16.25 mm at fret 24. The picking hand must approach the bridge as the sounding length shortens. A practice instruction can therefore specify either a fixed distance or a fixed fraction; it should say which is intended.

Keeping p constant preserves the normalized ideal coefficient pattern for fixed peak displacement. It does not guarantee equal radiated timbre, loudness, feel or pick force. Body resonances, string construction and the acoustic or pickup observation point remain relevant.

### Practice exercise 3: preserve a normalized location

Which bridge distances keep `p=1/10` at frets 12 and 24? Would leaving the distance at 65 mm satisfy that request?

> **Answer:** Use 32.5 mm and 16.25 mm respectively. Keeping 65 mm instead gives `1/5` and `2/5`, so it fails the fixed-fraction request under the declared geometry.

## 4. Do not infer a unique hand position from one null

At frets 12 and 24 with fixed 65 mm distance, both ideal cases suppress multiples of 5, yet their fractions are different. The modal amplitudes and signs need not agree. More generally, other causes can attenuate an observed partial, including the pickup or microphone response. A missing partial alone cannot identify the physical picking location.

The [geometry record](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/main/code/streeling/evidence/pluck-coordinates-2026-10-22/fret-geometry.json) also includes fret 7 as a non-octave transfer case. Its coordinates come from the same authored equation. No millimetre measurement or performance trace is attached to it.

### Practice exercise 4: challenge the inverse inference

An agent sees a suppressed fifth partial and reports a unique pick fraction `1/5`. Give the authored counterexample and explain why the observed recording would need more evidence.

> **Answer:** Fraction `2/5` also suppresses mode 5 in this ideal model. A recorded attenuation could additionally arise from other transfer paths, so a unique location cannot be inferred from that partial alone. More modal information, a stated observation model and independent position evidence are needed.

## 5. Translate the request into a future engine contract

The pinned [GA note entry point](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/76efbffd5af82b46c3146957a6bd4504008eeb89/Rust/guitar-web-wasm-demo/rust-engine/src/lib.rs#L530-L537) accepts frequency and velocity. Its [excitation heuristic](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/76efbffd5af82b46c3146957a6bd4504008eeb89/Rust/guitar-web-wasm-demo/rust-engine/src/lib.rs#L354-L379) derives a normalized delay-buffer offset from velocity. These inspected paths do not accept physical scale length, fret identity or bridge distance. A frequency can describe several string/fret realizations, so frequency alone does not supply those missing coordinates.

A proposed DSL instruction could carry a declared physical string realization and either a distance or a normalized location, plus its reference frame. This is a candidate design, not an implemented API or a requirement that every model use physical coordinates. The simpler alternative is to expose and document a purely synthetic buffer-shape control. The player's intended task decides whether the additional physical mapping is worth implementing and validating.

### Practice exercise 5: make the instruction executable in meaning

Rewrite “keep the pick at the same place” into two unambiguous requests across frets 0 and 12. Can the inspected frequency/velocity entry point directly represent both requests?

> **Answer:** One request keeps bridge distance at 65 mm, producing fractions `1/10` and `1/5`. Another keeps fraction `1/10`, requiring distances 65 mm and 32.5 mm. The inspected entry point has neither physical distance nor string/fret geometry fields, so it does not directly encode that distinction. Implementing and testing a mapping, or choosing a documented synthetic control, remains future work.
