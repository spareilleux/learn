A practice assistant raises the tempo after success and lowers it after failure, then reports a final tempo as a learner’s threshold. The update rule does not by itself justify that interpretation. We author a small staircase and audit exactly when its stopping condition is reached. The prerequisite supplies a pass definition; the new operation retains the sequential history needed to count direction reversals.

A reversal is a change of update direction, not simply a failed attempt. Consecutive failures continue the same downward direction. The first update cannot reverse a direction that does not yet exist. Record the tempo before each response as well as the next scheduled tempo, because a reversal summary can otherwise use the wrong side of the update. The fixed estimate deliberately averages the pre-update levels at reversals and is reported only once the stopping requirement is met.

The control contains the same number of completed attempts but never changes direction. Its final scheduled level is defined, while its stopping estimate remains unresolved. This separates a trial-count limit from a reversal rule. A production interface would also need bounds, missing-response behavior and a policy for interruption; none is silently supplied by these data. The actual practice packet makes target and cue policy explicit, so an implementation should retain those alongside the response history. No guitarist supplied this trace, and the arithmetic cannot establish a perceptual or motor threshold. A manual ledger is the baseline before recommending adaptive tutoring.

### Exercise 1

Trace the tempos before and after each response.

<details><summary>Answer 1</summary>

Apply the fixed step in the response’s direction, preserving the distinction between tested level and next scheduled level.

</details>

### Exercise 2

Which response directions are actual reversals?

<details><summary>Answer 2</summary>

Only changes from the preceding update direction count; repeated successes or repeated failures are not new reversals.

</details>

### Exercise 3

When does the main trace stop?

<details><summary>Answer 3</summary>

It stops at the displayed trial where the required reversal count is first reached; the prepared estimate uses the stated pre-update levels.

</details>

### Exercise 4

Why is the all-success control estimate unresolved?

<details><summary>Answer 4</summary>

It never meets the reversal stopping condition, even though every supplied trial is complete and a final scheduled level exists.

</details>
