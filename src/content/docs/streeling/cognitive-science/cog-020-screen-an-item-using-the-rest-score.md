A Learn quiz item is answered correctly by about half of an authored group. That does not show it distinguishes the intended skill. A mislabeled key or ambiguous prompt could reward the lower-scoring group more often. Before reusing the item in an agent-assisted explanation task, compare its outcome with performance on the other items and inspect the actual prompt.

Build the ranking from the rest score, excluding the target item. Including the item in its own ranking creates a part-whole association and can obscure the question being asked. Our deliberately small diagnostic splits the distinct rest scores into equal halves. It then subtracts the lower group’s success fraction from the upper group’s fraction. Overall ease is a separate summary of all responses.

The fixed case yields a negative contrast despite middling overall ease. The control reverses the item outcomes and keeps the overall proportion unchanged. Its contrast changes sign. The pair therefore shows why total correct count cannot replace the rest-score association. Preserve each row so a reviewer can inspect whether the scoring key, content alignment or wording could explain the pattern.

The primary teaching text discusses item-rest scoring, sample dependence and the dimensionality assumption behind traditional discrimination summaries. Our equal-half calculation is a declared finite diagnostic, not its full psychometric method. A low or negative result is a reason to investigate, not automatic permission to delete a conceptually necessary item. Real learners, appropriate consent, sufficiently representative responses and content review are still absent. A proposed adaptive DSL should preserve this uncertainty rather than turn the contrast into a universal item-quality label.

### Exercise 1

Compute overall ease.

<details><summary>Answer 1</summary>

There are `4` correct responses among `8`, so the fraction is `0.5`. This quantity ignores which rest-score group answered correctly.

</details>

### Exercise 2

Compute the signed contrast.

<details><summary>Answer 2</summary>

The lower group has `3` correct and the upper group `1`, each among `4`. The contrast is `-0.5`, a flag for investigation under this declared diagnostic.

</details>

### Exercise 3

Apply the response-reversal control.

<details><summary>Answer 3</summary>

Its overall fraction stays `0.5`, but the contrast becomes `0.5`. Equal totals therefore do not establish equal discrimination patterns.

</details>

### Exercise 4

Why exclude the target from ranking?

<details><summary>Answer 4</summary>

It prevents the target outcome from helping define its own comparison groups. It does not establish that the remaining score measures one valid skill or removes all sampling uncertainty.

</details>

### Exercise 5

Should an agent delete the item automatically?

<details><summary>Answer 5</summary>

No. Preserve the prompt, scoring key and row identities, then investigate content alignment, ambiguity and sample composition. A descriptive negative contrast does not itself identify the cause.

</details>
