Two reviewers give a GA practice explanation the same total score. Did they agree on the individual answers? A total can hide opposite disagreements. The probe-design prerequisite retains response patterns rather than only totals. Here the observations are fictional paired rubric ratings, and the operation is to preserve disagreement before an adjudicator chooses a final result.

The exact-source review lesson already separates the claims made by different receipt kinds. Use that boundary when reading this model: both raters must assess the same artifact and rubric revision. Different source bytes or different scoring rules produce a different comparison. Binary pass labels here are stipulated decisions for arithmetic, not authenticated human approvals. Their matching totals cannot establish independent review or correct assessment.

Build a paired table with outcomes for both pass, only the earlier-listed rater passes, only the other passes, and both fail. Every item belongs in exactly one cell. The diagonal gives agreement; the off-diagonal cells identify the cases that need discussion. Preserve item identity so an adjudicator can inspect the actual disputed explanations. A percentage without those identities is insufficient for targeted correction.

The two rating vectors have equal pass totals but disagree on specific items. A majority or consensus decision is a later operation and must not replace the original vectors. If an adjudicator decides those items after discussion, retain the initial ratings, stated reason and resulting label. Otherwise the record can misleadingly suggest that the initial independent ratings agreed. A consensus can also be wrong; this model provides no external correctness oracle.

The negative control copies one vector into the other. It yields perfect agreement arithmetically while destroying the independence needed for the intended review comparison. For the source-assisted task, choose an answer-free explanation task from the prerequisite and write a binary rubric with explicit failure examples. The direct baseline is a manually filled paired table. Ask an assistant to identify disagreements, then keep its assistance separate from any future human rating. Reject a report that turns matching totals into agreement or calls copied labels independent review.

## Retain a source-assisted learner attempt

Work from the pinned inputs listed above. Before asking for assistance, write your own prediction for the fixed case and its negative control, including the unit of every quantity and the exact comparison boundary. A short paper table is the direct manual baseline. Keep intermediate values, especially any result that contradicts your first prediction. This is an explanation task using existing sources and fixed data; it does not require installing a package, calling a paid service or introducing a new program.

Next use an assistant to locate the relevant source passage and critique your calculation. Retain the original prompt, the response, the source location it cited and the changes you accepted or rejected. A citation is useful only when its inspected content supports the specific claim. A declared stage, a data type and a prose comment supply different kinds of evidence from an executed outcome. Explain that boundary in the final answer rather than borrowing the authority of a successful-looking label.

Submit the manual baseline, corrected result, control outcome, falsifier and assistance history together. The prepared learner criteria ask whether the scope was identified, the calculation was correct, the counterexample was retained, the source supported the explanation and the conclusion respected its limits.
