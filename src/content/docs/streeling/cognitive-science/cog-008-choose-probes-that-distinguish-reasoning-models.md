## The project question

If a learner predicts one correct force, can we tell how they reasoned about the material frame? [PHY-012](../../physics/phy-012-rotate-directional-stiffness-with-the-material/) gives a tractable model for designing questions with distinguishable predictions. [COG-007](../../cognitive-science/cog-007-separate-unaided-recall-from-assisted-success/) separately retains assistance and attempt history. Here the task is to choose informative questions before assigning a total score or proposing adaptive tutoring.

The [Scott and Schumayer publisher abstract](https://journals.aps.org/prper/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.14.010106) discusses information in incorrect responses to a force-concept assessment. The [Persson author abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.06099v1) stresses checking assessment reliability and discrimination in a new context. Only these abstracts were read. They motivate caution and richer response records; they do not validate our new matrix task, establish its distractor frequencies or supply a learning effect.

## 1. Define a finite candidate catalogue

Use the authored local spring stiffness `diag(4,1)` N/mm. Consider four ideal prediction laws, not four measured learner populations:

| Law | Stated calculation |
| --- | --- |
| T | Rotate the full stiffness with the material. |
| F | Keep the original stiffness fixed in global axes. |
| D | Rotate stiffness, then remove its off-diagonal entries. |
| I | Use isotropic stiffness `2.5*I` at every angle. |

A probe specifies material angle and world displacement. Observe the predicted force vector in N and energy in N*mm. Probe P0 uses angle 0 degrees and displacement `[1,0]` mm. Laws T, F and D all predict `[4,0]` N; I predicts `[2.5,0]` N. One correct answer here cannot separate the first three laws.

### Practice exercise 1: locate an uninformative success

Derive the P0 predictions. A fictional response matches `[4,0]` N. Which catalogue entries remain compatible, and does that response prove correct understanding of rotation?

> **Answer:** At angle 0, the full, fixed and diagonal-only matrices coincide, so T, F and D predict `[4,0]` N; I predicts `[2.5,0]` N. The fictional response is compatible with T, F and D. It supplies no distinction among their rotation rules and is not an actual learner observation.

## 2. Combine complementary probes

Probe P1 uses a 90-degree material rotation and displacement `[1,0]` mm. Probe P2 uses a 45-degree rotation and the same world displacement. Their exact force predictions are:

| Law | P1 force, N | P2 force, N |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T | `[1,0]` | `[2.5,1.5]` |
| F | `[4,0]` | `[4,0]` |
| D | `[1,0]` | `[2.5,0]` |
| I | `[2.5,0]` | `[2.5,0]` |

P1 alone groups T with D; P2 alone groups D with I. Together their signatures are unique among these four stipulated laws. This is finite catalogue identifiability under exact predictions, not psychological identification of how a real person thinks.

### Practice exercise 2: interpret a fictional signature

One authored response pattern gives P1 force `[1,0]` and P2 force `[2.5,0]` N. Which catalogue law matches both? Why is using only P2 insufficient?

> **Answer:** Only D matches both within this catalogue. P2 alone is compatible with D and I. The two-vector signature separates the listed deterministic laws, but an arithmetic slip, guessing or a different unlisted rule could produce the same answers. No actual learner was diagnosed.

## 3. Preserve the observation that carries the distinction

At P2 the energies for T, D and I are all 1.25 N*mm; F gives 2 N*mm. Energy alone loses the transverse-force distinction between T and D for this particular displacement. Storing just “correct” or “wrong” can discard still more information about the calculation.

This does not mean scalar observations can never distinguish the catalogue. A different probe P3, at 45 degrees with displacement `[1,-1]` mm, gives energy 1 for T and 2.5 for F, D and I. Combined with P1's energies, P3 can distinguish all four catalogue entries. The usefulness of a projection depends on the chosen probes and question.

### Practice exercise 3: compare vector and energy evidence

Derive the P2 energy for T and D, including why the transverse force makes no contribution there. Then show how P1 and P3 energies distinguish T from D.

> **Answer:** For displacement `[1,0]`, energy is half its dot product with force, so both `[2.5,1.5]` and `[2.5,0]` give 1.25 N*mm. The zero second displacement removes the transverse contribution. P1 gives energy 0.5 for both T and D, but P3 gives 1 for T and 2.5 for D. The selected scalar pair separates them; P2 energy alone does not.

## 4. Challenge the catalogue itself

Define another authored law M: use T except at angle 60 degrees, where it uses isotropic stiffness instead. M agrees with T on P0, P1, P2 and P3. These finite probes therefore do not prove that a response rule equals T at every angle.

The [fixed signature record](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/main/code/streeling/evidence/constraint-feedback-2026-10-22/probe-signatures.json) retains the catalogue partitions and this held-out counterexample. It has no participant labels, prevalence, reliability coefficient or diagnostic accuracy estimate. The ideal laws also assume consistent exact arithmetic; actual reasoning can be context dependent or incomplete.

### Practice exercise 4: state the identification boundary

Does a T-like signature on all four probes rule out M? What would be required before calling the exercise a validated misconception detector?

> **Answer:** No. M was constructed to agree on those probes and differ at a held-out angle. The uniqueness result is conditional on the finite catalogue. Validation would require actual target-population responses, documented reasoning and assistance, suitable item review and evidence about reliability, discrimination and interpretation. A mathematical prediction table provides none of those empirical properties by itself.

## 5. Prepare an assessment before automating adaptation

### Practice exercise 5: write a source-assisted learner protocol

Read the two abstracts, PHY-012 and the fixed signature record. Propose a bounded real attempt that preserves useful errors while avoiding an unsupported mental-model label or learning-gain claim.

> **Answer:** Retain each prompt's angle, frame, displacement, units, predicted vector/energy, explanation, confidence and assistance before feedback. Check the answer key independently, vary held-out angles and cases, and ask why an answer was chosen instead of assigning a stable mental-model label from one match. Record missing or inconsistent responses. Review actual interpretation with learners and a subject expert before testing any adaptive feedback against a declared comparison and delayed endpoint. Compare simple written feedback before adding an agentic tutor. No participant validity, causal benefit or deployment was established here.
