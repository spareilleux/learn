## The project question

Two agents exchange only spectral magnitudes and conclude that their guitar attacks are identical. What information supports that conclusion? [INF-005](../../information-theory/inf-005-quantization-and-indistinguishable-renders/) examines quantization; here the discarded information is complex phase. [AUD-008](../../audio-engineering/aud-008-measure-a-filter-with-paired-input-and-output/) establishes paired transforms.

## 1. Construct an exact ambiguity

Take two authored two-tap impulse responses and pad each with zeros to length N = 8:

```text
A = [3/4, 1/4, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0]
B = [1/4, 3/4, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0]
H_A(omega) = 3/4 + (1/4)*exp(-i*omega)
H_B(omega) = 1/4 + (3/4)*exp(-i*omega)
|H_A(omega)|^2 = |H_B(omega)|^2 = 5/8 + (3/8)*cos(omega)
```

The magnitude equality holds for every real frequency, not just the eight DFT bins. The waveforms differ at both nonzero samples. This is more than a circular integer-shift ambiguity: the [fixture](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/main/code/streeling/evidence/filter-phase-2026-10-22/phase-pair.json) checks every shift from 0 through 7, and none maps A to B.

### Exercise 1 — Prove equality without reconstructing the waveform

Expand the squared magnitude of each two-tap response. Does equal magnitude prove equal samples, even after allowing an integer circular shift?

> **Answer.** Each squared magnitude is 9/16 + 1/16 + (6/16)cos(omega) = 5/8 + (3/8)cos(omega). The samples differ. Only the zero circular shift preserves the support at positions 0 and 1 for this length, and that shift does not exchange their unequal values. Thus none of the allowed integer shifts makes them identical.

## 2. Phase distinguishes what magnitude forgets

Use the [unscaled forward DFT](https://www.dsprelated.com/freebooks/mdft/DFT_Definition.html) with a negative exponential. At k = 2, omega = pi/2:

| Response | Complex coefficient | Magnitude, approximately | Phase, radians, approximately |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | 0.75 - 0.25i | 0.790569415 | -0.321750554 |
| B | 0.25 - 0.75i | 0.790569415 | -1.249045772 |

At DC both coefficients are +1. At Nyquist they are +0.5 and -0.5: the magnitudes still agree while the sign differs. Phase and sign are not recovered by converting magnitude to decibels.

### Exercise 2 — Identify a missing field

An agent sends the number 0.790569415 for k = 2. Which row does that identify? What additional value would distinguish these two candidates at that bin?

> **Answer.** It identifies neither uniquely: both have that magnitude. Their complex coefficient or phase would distinguish them there, with the stated transform convention and alignment. Phase at a zero-magnitude coefficient would need its own undefined-value policy; this particular pair has no zero on the unit circle.

## 3. Reconstruction needs the complex coefficients

The [inverse DFT](https://www.dsprelated.com/freebooks/mdft/Inverse_DFT.html) uses a positive exponential and a 1/N factor. Inverting each full complex spectrum reconstructs its own padded sequence within numerical tolerance. Inverting the magnitudes after assigning zero phase gives a third periodic sequence; it does not retrieve the original choice between A and B.

### Exercise 3 — Reject the invented phase

Why is replacing every coefficient by its magnitude before the inverse transform a new assumption? Can this counterexample establish that every overlapping magnitude spectrogram is impossible to invert under every constraint?

> **Answer.** It assigns phase zero instead of retaining the measured complex values; the resulting waveform follows that invented phase choice. No. This example concerns the full-transform magnitude map for the stated finite sequences. Overlapping frames and additional constraints change phase-retrieval questions; their uniqueness must be investigated under their actual assumptions.

## 4. Equal total energy can hide temporal redistribution

Both sequences have energy 5/8. Define the energy centroid in sample units as the sum of n times the squared sample, divided by total energy. A has centroid 0.1 sample; B has centroid 0.9 sample. These are numerical descriptors of the authored impulses, not measured attack quality, perceived timing or a group-delay curve.

### Exercise 4 — Compute a temporal distinction

Calculate both centroids and their difference. Does this show that a listener can distinguish the two filters or that either improves a guitar attack?

> **Answer.** A gives (1/16)/(10/16) = 0.1; B gives (9/16)/(10/16) = 0.9. The difference is 0.8 sample. It proves a temporal difference in this descriptor, not audibility, a pure delay, listener preference or a successful production improvement.

## 5. Specify both the transform and its input domain

At IX commit `bd1336b7c4045e427dcdbe80b102790e45da5ce1`, [stft and spectrogram](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ix/blob/bd1336b7c4045e427dcdbe80b102790e45da5ce1/crates/ix-signal/src/spectral.rs#L7-L54) expose different representations: complex coefficients versus scalar magnitudes or decibels. Preserve the former if a later operation requires their original phase.

GA also applies a Fourier transform to a different domain. At commit `76efbffd5af82b46c3146957a6bd4504008eeb89`, [SetClass](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/76efbffd5af82b46c3146957a6bd4504008eeb89/Common/GA.Domain.Core/Theory/Atonal/SetClass.cs#L150-L223) transforms the spectral prime form's pitch-class vector, then offers magnitude and phase views. Keeping that canonical vector's phase cannot recover an original transposition already removed by canonicalization.

The separate [SpectralPhaseAlignment](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/76efbffd5af82b46c3146957a6bd4504008eeb89/Common/GA.Domain.Core/Theory/Atonal/SpectralPhaseAlignment.cs#L75-L139) uses actual chroma and complex cross-products for trial rotations. Its [tests](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ga/blob/76efbffd5af82b46c3146957a6bd4504008eeb89/Tests/Common/GA.Business.Core.Tests/Atonal/SpectralPhaseAlignmentTests.cs) include major-triad transposition alignment. This is source/test reading, not a fresh C# run. Pitch-class rotation is not audio time delay; a Fourier index needs its declared input domain.

### Exercise 5 — Design a defensible agent handoff

Write the fields and claim limits for an agent comparing waveform identity and another comparing pitch-class alignment. Explain why “we kept phase” is insufficient on its own.

> **Answer.** Preserve the input domain and its normalization or canonicalization, source identity, transform sign and scaling, length, axes, sample rate for audio, time alignment, and complex coefficients or original data as needed. State whether the comparison concerns magnitude, exact samples, a specified time shift or pitch-class rotation. Phase of a canonical input cannot restore information erased before the transform. An adapter between these representations remains a proposed design, not an implemented contract.
