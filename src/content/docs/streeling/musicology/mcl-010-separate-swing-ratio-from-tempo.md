## The project question

A practice agent changes a swing ratio by slowing the entire phrase. The total beat duration then changes, while the requested operation was redistribution inside each beat. We use a bounded authored pair model to keep these controls separate. This numerical model neither defines a genre nor predicts the timing choices of an actual musician.

## 1. Exercise 1

Stipulate a quarter-note rate of 120 and divide each quarter into two nominal events. The beat duration is .5 seconds. A ratio 2:1 allocates two equal ratio parts to the first event and one to the second. The denominator of this allocation is the sum of ratio parts.

How long is the beat and how many ratio parts fill it?

> **Answer.** The beat lasts .5 seconds and contains 3 ratio parts.

## 2. Exercise 2

The two durations are `(.5)*2/3=1/3` and `(.5)*1/3=1/6` seconds. Their sum remains .5. This sum is the clock-preservation check. A display that rounds each event before accumulating many beats can introduce another error, so retain exact fractions when checking the authored example.

Calculate the event durations and their sum.

> **Answer.** They are `1/3` and `1/6` seconds, summing to `.5` seconds.

## 3. Exercise 3

Keep the tempo but change the ratio to 1:1. Both events last .25 seconds. The beat duration is unchanged, yet the internal boundary moves. This separates redistribution from slowing down. Conversely a global tempo change scales both durations and preserves their ratio under this constant-ratio model.

Which quantity changes between the two ratios at the same tempo?

> **Answer.** The internal division changes; total beat duration remains .5 seconds.

## 4. Exercise 4

As a negative control, slow straight timing to 60 quarters per minute. Its two events each last .5 seconds, totaling 1 second. It matches neither durations nor total of the original 2:1 example. Even if a phrase feels slower, that does not implement a ratio change with fixed tempo.

Does slower straight timing reproduce the original pair?

> **Answer.** No. `[.5,.5]` differs from `[1/3,1/6]` and changes the total from .5 to 1 second.

## 5. Exercise 5

The primary MusicXML swing reference defines nominal duration ratios and limits their scope. The retained meter lesson supplies the separate named tempo-unit requirement. A future GA proposal must identify which events are eligible and how its timing interacts with ties and tempo changes. A generic style word cannot supply these missing policies.

What must accompany a ratio in a handoff?

> **Answer.** Named beat unit, tempo, eligible event type, ratio and unsupported cases; no performed style or cultural identity follows.
