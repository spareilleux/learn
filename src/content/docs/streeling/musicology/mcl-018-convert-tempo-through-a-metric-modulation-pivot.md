A guitarist receives a practice chart with a metric relationship and a new tempo label. Copying the old beats-per-minute number can break the relationship even if the note symbols were preserved. We derive the destination quarter-note rate from an explicit equal-duration pivot. The new operation links two local tempo coordinates; it is different from merely naming the beat unit of a single metronome mark.

First convert the old pivot into seconds using the old quarter duration. Then divide that physical duration by the number of new quarters in the destination pivot. This order exposes the direction of the ratio. Multiplying by the inverse ratio would preserve neither pivot duration nor the intended transition. The fixed example equates an old dotted-quarter duration with a new quarter duration, so the new quarter must last longer.

The identity control uses equal old and new pivot lengths and preserves the original rate. It catches formulas that change tempo merely because a metric relationship exists. MusicXML’s metric-note element describes notation within such a mark, but reading it does not prove that GA imports or plays the relationship. An actual comparison would have to retain both note-unit descriptions, source tempo and event timing. No cultural accent, expressive timing or conductor intent is inferred from our equality. A manual duration chain is sufficient to check a proposed scheduling record before recommending a metric-modulation DSL.

## Assumptions and evidence limits

Assume an instantaneous exact equality between the old and new pivot durations. Each pivot length uses its own quarter-note coordinate. There is no tempo ramp, expressive timing or executed importer result.

### Exercise 1

Convert the old quarter and pivot into seconds.

<details><summary>Answer 1</summary>

Use the old quarter rate first, then multiply by the old pivot length in quarter units.

</details>

### Exercise 2

Derive the destination quarter duration.

<details><summary>Answer 2</summary>

Divide the shared pivot duration by the destination pivot length in new-quarter units.

</details>

### Exercise 3

Which direction does the quarter rate move in the main case?

<details><summary>Answer 3</summary>

It decreases because one new quarter occupies the longer old dotted-quarter duration.

</details>

### Exercise 4

What does the identity control establish?

<details><summary>Answer 4</summary>

Equal pivot lengths preserve the old quarter duration and rate under this equality rule.

</details>

### Exercise 5

What falsifies a reported modulation?

<details><summary>Answer 5</summary>

If the old and new pivot durations in seconds differ, the stated equality and derived tempo are inconsistent.

</details>
