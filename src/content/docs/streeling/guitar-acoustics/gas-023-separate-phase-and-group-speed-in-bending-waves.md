A body-wave animation may move crests and a packet envelope at the same speed by default. An ideal bending-wave dispersion law makes those speeds different. Differentiate angular frequency with respect to wave number instead of dividing it by wave number. The narrow-packet interpretation needs a local band and negligible distortion; it is not a claim that every visible pulse travels rigidly. This calculation supplies a candidate diagnostic for a [Godot](https://godotengine.org/) acoustic visualization, while actual meshes, materials and wave propagation remain untested.

## Specific assumptions and limits

Use positive wave number and coefficient with the displayed power law. Group speed is the local derivative for a narrow band. No material solver, calibrated body response, broadband pulse or scene simulation was executed.

### Exercise 1

Calculate the main result and name its convention.

> **Answer:** Angular frequency is 18, phase speed is 6 and group speed is 12 in consistent stipulated units.

### Exercise 2

Which quantity must remain separate?

> **Answer:** A crest follows phase; a narrow envelope follows the local derivative under the stated approximation.

### Exercise 3

What assumption prevents a stronger conclusion?

> **Answer:** A broad packet can spread, so assigning one unchanged envelope speed requires additional evidence.

### Exercise 4

Explain the negative control.

> **Answer:** The linear control returns equal speeds 2; it isolates the curvature of the dispersion relation.

### Exercise 5

What observation would reject the proposed claim?

> **Answer:** A claimed quadratic-wave animation with equal crest and local group speeds contradicts this model.
