A proposed pickup control moves a point observer but leaves its sensing width implicit. A real observation model can combine displacement across a region; then different parts of a mode may cancel even when the center is not a node. We study a uniform spatial average as an authored kernel. The prerequisite already separates excitation from point observation. The new operation integrates the observation rather than changing the pluck.

Integrate each sine mode across the centered aperture and divide by its width. The normalization ensures that a spatially constant field would keep its amplitude when the aperture grows. The result equals the center’s point value multiplied by a width-dependent sinc factor. Signed values matter: cancellation occurs before taking magnitude. Averaging magnitudes would remove the very cancellation being modeled.

In the fixed case the first and third modes both have nonzero center values but suffer different attenuation. The even modes already vanish at the center and remain absent under this symmetric kernel. That equality does not mean width never matters; it reflects this center, these modes and this symmetry. The point-limit control restores the center values without changing excitation. Keep the kernel and its coordinate normalization in a proposed GA parameter record. A practical pickup involves a different physical sensing process and frequency response. The direct integral identifies a useful question for future calibration while supplying no measured pickup response or engine implementation.

## Assumptions and evidence limits

Assume ideal sine displacement modes and a uniform normalized aperture entirely inside the string. Keep signed averages. Electromagnetic sensing, velocity response and an actual pickup calibration require a different model and observations.

### Exercise 1

Compute the signed point values at the declared center.

<details><summary>Answer 1</summary>

The listed sine values alternate through center antinodes and nodes; retain their signs rather than only magnitudes.

</details>

### Exercise 2

Why divide the aperture integral by its width?

<details><summary>Answer 2</summary>

The model is an average, not a total. Without division a change of width would also change the normalization.

</details>

### Exercise 3

Which nonzero mode is more attenuated in the fixed example?

<details><summary>Answer 3</summary>

The higher listed odd mode loses a larger fraction of its point amplitude through spatial cancellation.

</details>

### Exercise 4

What does the zero-width control recover?

<details><summary>Answer 4</summary>

The declared limit recovers every point value while retaining the same modal excitation and center.

</details>

### Exercise 5

What rejects a point-only claim about aperture fidelity?

<details><summary>Answer 5</summary>

Equal center locations with unequal averaged modal values demonstrate that center alone cannot specify this observation.

</details>
