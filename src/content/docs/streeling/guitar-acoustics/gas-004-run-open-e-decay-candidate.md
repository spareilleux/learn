## The engineering question

[GAS-003](../../guitar-acoustics/gas-003-decay-calibration-open-e/) proposed changing GA's base decay from **0.9978** to **0.9976** and requiring at least **1 dB** less absolute error against a recorded open-E decline. This follow-up reports the executed experiment. The setting improves the selected measure slightly and fails that pre-existing gate. Your deliverable is a defensible rejection record, with enough provenance for another engineer to reproduce the decision.

Read the [shared lab](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/main/code/streeling/guitar-runtime/README.md) and [evidence guide](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/main/code/streeling/evidence/guitar-runtime-2026-10-22/README.md) alongside this lesson. ## Fix the comparison before measuring

Each arm starts a fresh engine at **44100 Hz**, default guitar profile **0**, requested E2 frequency **82.4069 Hz**, velocity **0.1**, and duration **4 seconds**, producing **176400 samples**. Fresh initialization avoids carrying a previous note's delay, filter, or reverb state into the other arm. Brightness, attack decay, dispersion, and reverb settings stay fixed.

Initialization already selects profile **0**. Baseline keeps its base decay **0.9978**. Candidate initialization is followed by `engine_set_decay` with **0.9976**, then `engine_note_on`, then rendering. Selecting a profile after the setter would overwrite the requested decay. Read the actual [native adapter](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/main/code/streeling/guitar-runtime/native.rs) and [runner](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/main/code/streeling/guitar-runtime/run.mjs), rather than inferring call order from the candidate's name.

### Exercise 1 — Protect the intervention

Why must profile selection precede the decay setter, and why must each arm have a fresh engine?

> **Answer:** Profile selection writes the decay setting together with other controls, so a later selection could erase the intervention. A fresh engine gives both arms the same initial state; reusing one would confound the parameter change with accumulated voice, filter, and reverb state.

### Exercise 2 — Check reproducibility

Which evidence would establish that the new baseline recovered the historical experiment, beyond obtaining a similar rounded decline?

> **Answer:** The baseline WAV has the exact historical fixture hash, and replaying the unchanged primary measurement script produces a complete baseline JSON match with the archived measurements. Matching a rounded decline alone could conceal a different waveform or changed measurement method.

## Measure the same event

The method subtracts early-window RMS level from late-window RMS level, using **0.2–0.4 seconds** and **1.0–1.2 seconds** after onset. The recording's onset is approximately **1.1 seconds** into its file; its additional attack is near **2.75 seconds**. Its selected windows are therefore approximately **1.3–1.5 seconds** and **2.1–2.3 seconds** in file time. Both precede that additional attack. Render onset is at zero.

This is a local envelope measurement, not a whole-file decay constant. A different guitar, microphone, room, and pluck supplied the reference. Within-file level change removes a simple gain difference, but does not make the recording a universal physical target.

### Exercise 3 — Spot a contaminated window

Would a recording window beginning at **2.8 seconds** answer the same first-note decay question?

> **Answer:** No. It begins after the additional attack near **2.75 seconds**, introducing another excitation. Keep the onset-relative windows and document their approximate onset assumption; otherwise the experiment changes its question while keeping the same label.

## Read the full-precision receipts

The [primary replay](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/main/code/streeling/evidence/guitar-runtime-2026-10-22/primary-measurement-replay.json) records reference decline **−3.9221151304441193 dB**, use of the unchanged primary script, and the complete baseline JSON match. The [baseline summary](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/main/code/streeling/evidence/guitar-runtime-2026-10-22/baseline-primary-measurements.json) and [candidate summary](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/main/code/streeling/evidence/guitar-runtime-2026-10-22/candidate-primary-measurements.json) retain its broader measurements. The following full-precision values come from the [runtime results](https://github.com/spareilleux/learn/blob/main/code/streeling/evidence/guitar-runtime-2026-10-22/results.json); the replay documents negligible arithmetic-order differences between the implementations.

| Native PCM16 arm | Decline, dB | Absolute reference error, dB |
|---|---:|---:|
| Baseline | −1.0848117418372532 | 2.837303388606845 |
| Candidate | −1.198977891431639 | 2.723137239012459 |

The stored improvement is **0.11416614959438576 dB**. For readable arithmetic, **2.837303 − 2.723137 ≈ 0.114166 dB**. The pre-existing **1 dB** requirement would allow approximately **1.837303 dB** candidate error when applied to the unrounded baseline. Actual candidate error is approximately **2.723137 dB**, so it fails. Do not replace the threshold with a smaller one after seeing the output.

### Exercise 4 — Make the decision

Does a positive **0.114166 dB** improvement pass a requirement of at least **1 dB**? What should the experiment record say?

> **Answer:** It fails. Record that base decay **0.9976** slightly reduced this fixed E2 error but did not meet the pre-existing improvement criterion. Reject this setting under this gate. The result neither establishes that it sounds worse nor proves that the algorithm cannot improve.

## Preserve the remaining questions

Successful execution is distinct from candidate acceptance. The runner can finish successfully while `candidate_passes_decline_gate` is false. Sample-range, silence, and block-partition assertions cover specific runtime properties. They do not establish every GAS-003 guard. The primary summaries contain pitch and partial observations, but rounded summaries do not prove attack preservation or every spectral property.

### Exercise 5 — Write the next handoff

What must remain open before a product decision, even if a future decay candidate passes the numerical gate?

> **Answer:** Preserve separate work for onset sensitivity, attack preservation, pitch and partial-track guards, other notes and velocities, and level-matched blinded listening. Native rendering and offline WebAssembly execution do not certify browser AudioWorklet capture, scheduling, or long-running memory behavior. Human subject, fluent-language, and learner reviews and course HTML publication also remain pending.

Keep the failed candidate's artifacts. They replace an untested hypothesis with a bounded result and give the next experiment a reproducible baseline without turning a calibration measure into a listener verdict.
