Observatory Web export provenance

Built 2026-09-30 with the official non-.NET Godot 4.6.1-stable Web export
template. Godot renderer: gl_compatibility (WebGL 2). The Web export disables
SSR, SSIL and volumetric fog from the native Forward+ scene, and replaces its
4x MSAA with the anti-aliasing that the page picks (see the revision that
makes walk mode a body). It is separate from the higher-quality native build
and does not use hardware ray tracing.

This revision adds the detail pass: shaded book bindings (40,250 books), dressed
stone with a tooled relief, turned bronze balusters under a walnut handrail, a
brushed steel shader for the box and 24 stained-glass lancets. Its textures were
generated locally with ComfyUI and SDXL 1.0 base: book_grain, stone_relief,
steel_grime and the three glass_* panels. pier_ornament_normal is computed from
pier_ornament.

Under Compatibility the scripts change three things:
- the book shader skips its sRGB-to-linear conversion, because Compatibility
  shades in sRGB space and writes the result out unconverted;
- the stained glass throws no spot lights: there is no volumetric fog to show
  their shafts, and 24 more lights would crowd the per-object light limit;
- the books get a warm emissive fill, and the bronze and steel less metallic
  finishes, in place of the missing SDFGI, SSIL and SSR.
Forward+ is unaffected: its shelf close-ups are pixel-identical with and
without these changes.

A later revision, the same day, reworks the steel box, which still looked like
a cartoon. The seam bands, ribs, door frame, door panels and vents were
near-black and outlined every plate; they are now brushed steel in a darker
tone, with bevelled edges. The 426 chrome-ball rivets become 736 low domed
heads in even rows. The shader gives each plate its own tone and roughness, a
contact shadow beside the bands, grime running down from under them and dust
near the plinth; the brushing is finer and the sheet more satin, so
reflections no longer smear into vertical streaks. Under Compatibility the
steel also gets a warmer albedo, since that renderer lights it with the cool
keys and a blue ambient only.

The next revision shelves every course of the site: a face-out volume per
course on the ground floor, one bay per area with a plaque, a card for the
aimed volume, and a catalogue on L; a click or Enter opens the course in a new
tab. The course list is demos/observatory/courses.json, which the site
generates from its sidebar (src/pages/demos/observatory/courses.json.ts); the
scene fetches it at start-up and carries the copy listed below for when it
cannot.

The next revision puts the courses in the shelves themselves, in the row at eye
height: each area is a series in its own cloth between two brass bookends,
with its name on a card in a brass holder on the shelf edge, among ordinary
books. The course books share the other books' mesh, bindings shader and
MultiMeshes, and carry their real titles in gilt along the spine; an aimed book
slides out and shows its card. The shelf wood of each bay is merged into one
mesh: natively, under Compatibility at 1600x900, the overview drops from 733
to 268 draw calls. On the Web, MSAA gives way to FXAA: in Chrome on an RTX 5080,
full screen (3819x1959 canvas), a frame took 28 to 30 ms with 4x MSAA and
36 ms with 2x, against 2.2 to 3.0 ms without MSAA, and the scene now holds
60 fps where it held 25 to 34. A bare WebGL 2 page at the same size takes
0.3 ms, so the cost was the multisample resolve in the export, not the browser.

The next revision adds a marble statue of Hari Seldon in the hall, in front of
the Streeling University bay, on a dark pedestal with a gilt inscription whose
era follows the page's language; that bay's data-rain beam is left out. The
capitals, pier bases, carved panels, arches, entablature, drum and ceiling ribs
gain gilt mouldings, merged into four meshes, and the floor is paved in 18
rings of pale and dark marble around a compass rose (a shader, with darker
stand-in tints under Compatibility). The figure was generated locally:
Z-Image-Turbo (Apache-2.0) drew the concept, TRELLIS.2 4B int8 (MIT; its image
encoder is DINOv3, under the DINOv3 License) turned it into a 3.57 million face
mesh in 38 s on the RTX 5080, ComfyUI's RemeshMesh and DecimateMesh brought it
to 59,905 faces, and Blender normalised it (seldon_statue.glb, 1.08 MB). In
Chrome for Testing 153 on the same machine, full screen (3840x1872 canvas)
without vsync, a frame takes 1.9 to 2.2 ms in five views.

The next revision adds shadows and touch controls. A cool moonlight spot
inside the oculus drum, 24 degrees off the vertical at the box, lights the
floor, the steel box and the statue and casts their shadows, and the piers',
across the paving. Its shadow map is 32-bit, since a 16-bit one striped the
floor under Compatibility, and it stays cached while nothing in its range
moves. Contact shadows are painted into the paving at the foot of the box,
the piers, the pedestal and the shelves. On a touch screen, a stick walks, a
drag looks, and buttons fly, rise, descend, change view and open the course
catalogue; a book opens with two taps. In Chrome for Testing 153 on the RTX
5080, full screen (3819x1875 canvas) without vsync, a frame takes 1.9 to
3.0 ms in five views, against 1.9 to 2.2 ms before. In a phone emulation
(412x915 at 2.625x, an Android user agent, CDP touch events), the controls
walk, look, fly, change view and open and close the catalogue; a real phone
is still to verify.

The next revision, 2026-09-30, makes walk mode a body, turns the viewpoints
into short flights, marks the course bays, adds a language selector and a
corridor behind the box, widens the view on a phone held upright, and gives
the Web build the anti-aliasing it lacked. It ships only scripts, one shader
and the page's head: no new texture or model.
- Walking (scripts/walk_physics.gd): the box, the piers, the pedestal and the
  shelves stop the visitor; Space jumps 0.85 m, C crouches, C while sprinting
  slides. Keys 1 to 7 and the Views button fly to their viewpoint in 0.9 to
  1.6 s, rising up to 6 m and widening the field up to 9 degrees on the way,
  after GTA V's view switch (scripts/view_move.gd). With the browser's
  reduced-motion setting the views cut at once, and walking has no bob, dip,
  roll or change of field.
- Courses (scripts/course_library.gd): each bay has a banner in its area's
  colour under the gallery and, at eye level, a lacquered sign with the
  area's name and course count under a brass lamp. An EN / FR / ES selector,
  or T, switches titles, signs, cards, catalogue and course links; English
  is the default unless the visitor came from a French or Spanish page, and
  the address keeps the choice.
- Touch (scripts/touch_controls.gd): on an iPhone in Safari the stick walked
  but a drag did not turn the view (the author's report). A look drag used to
  send mouse events to the fly camera, which asks the browser for a pointer
  lock; it now turns the camera itself, per touch, and a drag whose index
  matches no press goes to the nearest touch. This replaces a hotfix of
  2026-09-29 that was never published. The stick sprints past its rim, and
  the arrow buttons jump and crouch, or rise and descend in flight. The help
  wraps clear of the Courses button and the language selector. A screen held
  upright gets a wider field (scripts/view_fit.gd): on an iPhone 50 degrees
  across instead of 32 (90 vertically), on an iPad 60 instead of 47.
- The multiverse corridor (scripts/multiverse.gd,
  assets/world_glimpse.gdshader): a double click on the box, or a double tap,
  fades into an endless row of numbered steel doors, each glowing with one
  of twelve procedural worlds, after Blake Crouch's novel Dark Matter; no
  frame, set or asset of any adaptation is used. The Leave button, Esc or
  the lit doorway behind the visitor lead back. The hall is hidden meanwhile.
- Anti-aliasing: under Compatibility, FXAA and SMAA change no pixel (60 to
  64 percent of them under Forward+), so the published build had none. Two
  scripts in the page's head now choose: 4x MSAA on touch screens, and on
  computers a canvas 1.5 times finer than the screen, which the browser
  scales down. Each steps down when the median frame exceeds 22 ms: MSAA
  from 4x to 2x to none, the supersampling from 1.5 to 1. ?msaa=0|2|4 and
  ?ss= fix them. Shimmer was measured in Chrome emulations with the camera
  moved a quarter of a pixel per frame along four paths, as the mean second
  temporal difference of luma after a blur of one CSS pixel. Against the
  published build it drops by 9 to 59 percent on an iPad and an iPhone, held
  either way, and by 13 to 42 percent on a computer at 1600x900. On a
  computer, thin lights far off, like the 4 cm rain beams, look fainter.
- The Prime Radiant and a metal gilt remain experiments: the export's
  exclude_filter leaves out their files, and the scene keeps the published
  centre beam and gilt.
- In the same emulations, with Chrome's CPU throttled six-fold, a phone's
  MSAA went from 4x to 2x after 3.8 s and to none after 8.8 s (a median frame
  of 33 ms); throttled thirty-fold, a computer's supersampling fell to 1
  after 6.3 s. At full speed both kept their level. With the browser's
  reduced-motion setting, key 3 cut to its view instead of a 1.34 s flight.
- scripts/camera_follow.gd ships too, asleep unless the address carries
  ?follow=1: it lets a test harness place the camera frame by frame.
