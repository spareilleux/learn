## Adoption contract to pre-register

1. **Question and baseline.** Choose one narrow task, such as GA intent routing or evidence support classification. Freeze the same inputs and independent labels for the local deterministic/learned baseline and Jev. Score in-scope accuracy, out-of-scope declines, macro-F1, unsupported/false-support errors, abstentions, latency percentiles, and total cost per successful downstream task. A passage-filter experiment should use one query–passage pair per judgment, as in TypeSafe's [RAG example](https://docs.typesafe.ai/cookbooks/classifying_rag_passages), while preserving a deterministic retrieval and action boundary.
2. **Quality gate.** Register acceptance and rejection bands before seeing the new labels or Jev responses, including an explicit outcome for transport failures, model drift, malformed responses, and timeouts. Count all failures in the denominator. The French Stage 2 result shows why probability validation must account for the model's reported two-decimal rounding under the chosen option count; set the rule before the next run and retain the old `KILL` verdict. Do not use confidence alone as a correctness or authority gate: TypeSafe says it is derived from the probability distribution, and the IX Stage 1 report found confidence 1.0 in 109/126 cases while still recording four routing misses, one at confidence 0.97. [TypeSafe confidence](https://docs.typesafe.ai/confidence), [IX results](https://github.com/GuitarAlchemist/ix/blob/755bc43eda1babb06fed609039de283e9b9a670c/state/router-spike/RESULTS.md).
3. **Cost gate.** Pin `jev-1.13.0` and recheck the current rate card immediately before any live run. The reviewed [model page](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models) lists USD 0.042 per million input tokens and free output tokens. Record each request digest, actual reported input/output usage, model ID, latency, failures, retries, and account-side billed amount separately. The course and IX total was approximately USD 0.021 **calculated**, not an invoice; one timed-out call has unknown usage. Measure any claimed savings after Jev, preprocessing, retries, fallback, and unchanged downstream model/cache costs. Stop if quality worsens or total dollars per accepted task do not improve; a token-only 50% target is insufficient. Local byte counts are preflight proxies, not provider-billed token caps. [Journal open checks](evidence/2026-09-25-jev-journal-snapshot.md#%C3%A0-v%C3%A9rifier), [TypeSafe models](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models).
4. **Privacy and authority gate.** Start with public or synthetic redacted fixtures. Before any real shadow-log sample leaves GA, define permitted fields, redaction, retention, consent/notice if applicable, and an independent review of a sample. TypeSafe says it does not train on customer requests, while its [legal page](https://docs.typesafe.ai/legal) describes enterprise zero-data-retention as a separate offering; neither statement by itself authorizes sending private code, personal messages, or agent secrets. A Jev answer may annotate or recommend; code and established human/repository gates retain merges, deployment, spending, and approval authority. TypeSafe itself documents weaknesses in counting, arithmetic, dates, large irrelevant state, adversarial content, and indirection. [Jev 1.13 jaggedness](https://docs.typesafe.ai/model-jaggedness/jev-1.13).

## Next smallest calibration

First, use the existing scorer **offline** to freeze a new, independently labeled set of sanitized examples that did not tune the Stage 1/2 prompts or thresholds. Include English/French/Spanish, ambiguous routing, missing evidence versus contradiction, and explicit `none`/abstain cases. Register the corpus digest, accepted probability-sum rule, timeout handling, paired baseline, cost/call ceiling, and quality bands. Have a second reader review the labels and rule before the live run. This local preparation makes no paid calls.

Then run a bounded pinned-model calibration on that frozen set, subject to the privacy gate. A first 100-case pilot can expose gross errors and produce Brier/reliability plots, but cannot certify a very low false-support rate: even zero false supports in 100 independent cases leaves an approximate 95% upper bound near 3%. A claim below 1% would need roughly 300 relevant zero-error cases (or another defensible statistical design), not merely the existing 12-case fixture. Re-check the account invoice and use an explicit operator budget; do not infer a hard charge ceiling from an after-response stop rule. Keep Jev in shadow until a separate real-traffic, consented or safely redacted evaluation beats the local baseline on decision quality and net task cost.

Potential follow-on arms, each with its own frozen question and gate: Jev labels GA shadow-log cases for human audit, escalates only low-margin local-router decisions, filters retrieved RAG passages, or annotates Gaia evidence receipts without changing transition authority. Compare each arm against a simpler local rule first.
